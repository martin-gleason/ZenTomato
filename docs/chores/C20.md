# C20 — the alarm that sounded with sound off

**Owner: both.** The agent narrows it; one short device test decides it.

**Severity: high.** `Sound on/off` is one of the seven settings `SPEC.md` names,
and it is the only one whose failure is loud.

## What was reported

Build `202608281026`, 2026-08-28. A sprint run with Do Not Disturb on and **the
app's own Sound setting off**. The alarm sounded.

## Why nobody caught this earlier

**Because it had never been looked at.** `O7` has been open since `F2` saying, in
one sentence, that this setting *"rests entirely on a bundled silent audio file"*
— and no entry anywhere records a phone being observed to stay quiet.

Worse, the assumption was propagated. `O6`'s note explained that an earlier DND
sprint could say nothing about silent mode *because* the sound setting was off,
"so nothing could have made a noise whatever the ringer was doing." That sentence
assumed exactly what `O7` says was never checked, and used it as evidence about
something else. Both entries are corrected.

## What is known to be right

- `AlarmKitScheduler` has **one** sound assignment, at line 128. There is no
  second path.
- With the setting off it resolves to `.named("Silence.caf")` —
  `AlarmSoundDecision.decide` returns `.silent`, and `.silent` maps to that file.
  `AlarmSoundDecisionTests` asserts the whole catalogue silences.
- `Silence.caf` is in the shipped bundle: half a second, mono, 8 kHz, checked in
  the built `.app` rather than in `project.yml`.

**So the setting reaches the framework correctly.** Whatever is wrong is on the
other side of that call, or is not about the file at all.

## Two mechanisms

**A — the snapshot, working as designed, seen from outside.** Settings freeze into
`TimerSettingsSnapshot` at block start. If Sound was switched off *during* the
sprint, the running block keeps the value it started with and alarms; the next
block would be silent. The owner **was** changing settings mid-sprint that
session — that is what `D27` came from — so this is entirely plausible and is not
a defect at all. It would mean one block sounded, not all of them.

**B — `Silence.caf` does not silence an AlarmKit alarm.** This is the one that
would hurt. An `AlarmKit` alarm is a *system alarm*, and this project spent `C14`
and a delta making it break through Focus and the ringer switch on purpose. A
sound designed to be unsilenceable by the phone may also be unsilenceable by the
app: iOS may ignore a named file it considers unusable, or may floor an alarm at
some audible default. If B is true, **`sound on/off` cannot be implemented this
way at all**, and that is a spec-level problem rather than a bug.

## The test that separates them, and it takes one minute

Set **focus length to 1 minute**, Sound **off**, and — this is the part that
matters — **change nothing after pressing Start**. Then start a focus block and
wait.

- **Silent** ⇒ mechanism A. Not a defect; `D27` removes the confusion by locking
  the screen during a block. `O7` closes for the first time on real evidence.
- **It sounds** ⇒ mechanism B. `O7` closes the other way, `D26` becomes urgent
  because there would be a noise the app genuinely cannot stop, and `sound
  on/off` needs a design that is not a silent file.

**One minute, one variable, nothing touched mid-block.** The reason to state that
so plainly is that the previous three attempts to learn something about this alarm
all had a second variable moving in them.

## Not proposed here

No fix. Under mechanism A there is nothing to fix; under B the fix is a design
question for the owner, not a patch. `C19`'s lesson applies unchanged: the `F2b`
arc ran to four successive fixes, each introduced by the one before it, every one
a reasonable guess made without a reproduction.
