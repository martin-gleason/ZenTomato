# C21 — A zenpom Focus, and the runbook for building one

**Owner: the agent** writes it; **the owner** runs it once, which is what verifies it.
**Status:** written, **unverified on a device.** Nothing below is proven until step 6 has been done.
**Milestone:** v1.5, item 1 of 12. Zero code, works on the already-shipped build.
**Source:** `docs/plans/parked.md`, "A ZenPom Focus, and a Shortcuts automation", decided 2026-08-26.
**Pairs with:** `F10`, the "start a sprint" App Intent, which automates what this does by hand.

## Why this is a runbook and not a feature

**zenpom cannot silence your other notifications, and no app can.** Verified in the SDK at the time
the decision was taken: nothing in `Intents`, `AppIntents` or `UserNotifications` sets a Focus or
suppresses another app's notifications. The one Focus API an app gets — `SetFocusFilterIntent` — runs
the *other* way, letting a Focus tell an app to change its own behaviour.

That is a feature of the platform's design rather than a gap in this one. An app that could silence
your other notifications is an app Apple would not ship.

So the capability is **assembled by the person**, not granted by the app, and the honest deliverable
is instructions. It costs nothing, it works on the build already on the phone, and its only price is
that you have to turn it on.

**This matters more here than in most apps.** The distraction log's whole purpose is measuring what
interrupts you. External distractions that a Focus would have prevented are noise in the instrument —
not because they didn't happen, but because they didn't have to.

## The runbook

**Every menu label below is written from the documented iOS Focus model and is NOT yet confirmed
against iOS 26 on the owner's phone.** Where a label differs, the correction goes in this file, and
that correction *is* step 6. Do not treat this section as verified until the record at the foot says
it was.

### 1 — Make the Focus

**Settings › Focus › + (top right) › Custom**

Name it **zenpom**. Pick a colour and the tomato icon if one is offered.

### 2 — Allow zenpom, and nothing you don't need

In **Apps**, set the list to **Allow notifications from** and add **zenpom** alone.

**Why an allow-list and not a block-list.** A block-list is a list of things you have already thought
of. Everything installed later is silently allowed, and the one that interrupts you next month is the
one you never listed.

**Do not add "Time Sensitive"** unless you find you need it. It is an escape hatch other apps can mark
their own notifications with, which is precisely the thing being closed.

### 3 — People

Set **People** to **Allow notifications from › None**, then add back only the people whose call
genuinely cannot wait — for most people that is nobody, or one person.

**Leave "Allow repeated calls" on.** It is the safety valve: someone who rings twice within three
minutes gets through. It means the allow-list can be strict without being reckless.

### 4 — Do not schedule it

**Skip "Set a Schedule" entirely.**

A scheduled Focus turns on whether or not you are working, and a Focus that is on while you are not
working is one you will start dismissing out of habit. Turn it on when a sprint starts — by hand for
now, or with `F10` once that lands.

### 5 — Turn it on and off from Control Centre

Swipe down from the top right → **Focus** → **zenpom**.

Set it to **Until I turn it off** rather than a duration. A one-hour Focus that expires mid-sprint
fails silently, and the failure looks like "the app didn't work."

### 6 — Verify it, which is what closes this chore

**Run one full sprint with the Focus on**, and answer four things:

1. Did a zenpom alarm sound at a block boundary? *(It must. `O6` already proved AlarmKit fires through
   an active Focus — this confirms the allow-list did not break that.)*
2. Did anything else get through that should not have?
3. Did anything **fail** to get through that you actually needed?
4. Did the block feel different?

Question 4 is the point of the exercise, and the export answers it more honestly than memory does:
**compare the external-distraction count for that sprint against a typical one.** That comparison is
the only real measure of whether this chore was worth writing, and it is available for free because
the log already counts I and E separately.

## Scope

**Instructions only. No code, no screen, no setting.** Automating the switch is `F10` and is a
separate gate.

**Not in this chore:** anything that reads or reacts to Focus state — `SetFocusFilterIntent` would let
zenpom change its *own* behaviour when a Focus turns on, which is a real feature and an entirely
different one.
