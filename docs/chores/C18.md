# C18 — GPL-3.0 for the repository, MIT for the app

**Owner: both.** The agent drafts the files; only the owner can license their own
work.

**PLAN ONLY. Nothing is implemented.** Written before the code, per the gate
reaffirmed in `conventions.md` on 2026-08-27.

---

## The correction that has to come first

**What you asked for is not "dual licensing" in the usual sense, and the
difference matters enormously.**

The common form is **disjunctive**: a project offers *"GPL-3.0 **or** MIT, take
your pick."* Anyone who wants the code simply takes MIT, ignores the GPL, and the
copyleft is worth nothing. Offering both to everyone for the same artifact is a
long way of saying MIT.

**What you actually want is licence per distribution channel:**

| What is distributed | Where | Licence |
|---|---|---|
| Source | GitHub | **GPL-3.0-or-later** |
| Compiled binary | App Store / TestFlight | **MIT** |

That is coherent and legitimate — you hold the copyright, so you may license
different distributions on different terms. But **it must never be written as
"dual licensed under GPL-3.0 or MIT"**, because that sentence grants everyone the
MIT option for the source and dissolves the copyleft you chose on purpose.

The wording is not decoration here. It is the whole mechanism.

---

## Does MIT actually solve the App Store problem?

**Yes, and for one precise reason.**

`C10`'s research established that the conflict is **GPLv3's prohibition on adding
restrictions** beyond the licence. Apple's terms add restrictions — device limits,
DRM — to everyone who downloads. VLC was pulled over exactly this, after a
copyright holder complained.

**MIT contains no such prohibition.** It permits use, copying, modification and
redistribution, and says nothing about whether a distributor may impose further
terms. Apple's rules sit on top of it without conflict.

So the fix is not a trick. It is that the incompatible clause is the one MIT does
not have.

---

## What MIT obliges you to do

One thing, and it is not optional:

> The above copyright notice and this permission notice shall be included in **all
> copies or substantial portions of the Software**.

For an app, that means **the licence text ships inside the binary**. Which is the
About screen already planned in `parked.md` — and this turns it from a nicety into
a **licence obligation**. If ZenPom ships under MIT with no licence text
reachable in the app, the app is out of compliance with its own licence.

**This is now the second obligation landing on that screen**, alongside `D24`'s
per-sound attribution. Worth building it once, properly.

## What MIT gives away that a proprietary EULA would not

Stated plainly so the choice is made with open eyes:

**Anyone who receives the binary may redistribute it.** MIT permits it. In
practice an `.ipa` is close to useless to a stranger — it needs signing to
install — so the cost is near zero. But it is a real grant, not a formality.

**MIT on the binary does *not* grant MIT rights to the source.** Someone holding
an MIT-licensed binary has MIT rights to *that binary*. The source on GitHub
remains GPL-3.0, and a fork of the source remains copyleft. That is the part that
makes the arrangement worth having.

**The alternative, for the record:** if the only goal were App Store compliance,
Apple's own standard EULA achieves it and grants nothing. MIT gives away more than
strictly required — and is the more honest fit for a project whose source is
public anyway. **The owner chose MIT; this records what it costs rather than
arguing with it.**

## The prerequisite that blocks everything

**`O22`. You cannot grant MIT on code you do not own.**

Today you own all of it — `CLAUDE.md` records the agent's work as made for you.
The moment one outside contribution is merged under GPL-3.0 with no broader grant,
that contributor's copyright is in the tree and **the MIT binary grant becomes
impossible without their permission**.

That is not theoretical: it is precisely how VLC's situation arose. A public repo
invites pull requests, and the first one that arrives without a CLA in place ends
this arrangement quietly.

**So `O22` is not a follow-up to this chore. It is a precondition.**

## The plan

### C18-T1 — State the copyright, before anything else

A `NOTICE` file, or a section in `README.md`, recording that copyright in the work
is held by Martin Gleason, and that contributions authored by an agent were made
for the owner. Currently that fact lives only in `CLAUDE.md`, which is a working
instruction rather than a statement of ownership.

### C18-T2 — `CONTRIBUTING.md` with the contributor terms

Decided by `O22`: a CLA or copyright assignment. **This lands before the repo is
advertised anywhere**, because declining a pull request for licensing reasons
after it arrives costs far more than saying the terms up front.

### C18-T3 — The licence files

- `LICENSE` — GPL-3.0-or-later, unchanged. This is what GitHub reads and shows.
- `LICENSE-APP.md` — the MIT grant covering compiled binaries, with wording that
  is explicitly **channel-scoped** rather than disjunctive.
- `README.md` — a short section saying which is which, in a form that cannot be
  misread as "pick one".

**The wording of the second file is the deliverable.** It has to say that the
grant covers binaries distributed by the copyright holder, and that the source
remains GPL-3.0.

### C18-T4 — The About screen carries the notices

Both obligations in one place: the MIT text for the app, the GPL statement and a
link to the source for the repository, and — once `D24` lands — the per-sound
attribution.

Waits on nothing further now: `C10` ruled dual licensing, and this chore settles
which licence the binary carries, which was the last thing blocking that screen.

### C18-T5 — A fence

`LicenseFenceTests`, in the shape the other fences take:

- both licence files exist and are non-empty
- `README.md` does **not** contain the phrase "dual licensed under" followed by
  both names in a disjunctive form — the one sentence that would void the copyleft
- the About screen's source names the MIT text and the source link
- the MIT notice actually reaches the built bundle, checked in the product rather
  than in `project.yml`

**The second of those is the one worth having.** Every other item here is a file
somebody would notice was missing. That one is a sentence somebody would add
believing it was a helpful summary.
