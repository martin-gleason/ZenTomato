## Paraphrasing it back

Right now, finished work leaves exactly one trace a person can look at: a number. The Pomodoros
sheet opens on today's count in large type, and `StatsScreen`'s own doc comment is a list of every
graphic it refuses — no chart, no ring, no heat grid, no streak, no badge, no best day. That refusal
was correct and this feature does not undo it.

What the owner has asked for is a second thing beside that number: **finished work showing up as
something that accumulates.** Poms grow a garden. Do forty poms this fortnight and the garden is
visibly larger than it was; do none next fortnight and it is exactly the same size as you left it.
It never shrinks, never resets, never breaks, and it never says anything about *when* the poms
happened.

**The whole feature is that second clause.** The growing part is a rendering problem and is the easy
half. The part that needs a gate, a delta and a fence is the promise that nothing is ever lost —
because everything about a growing picture pulls towards the thing the spec forbids. A garden that
wilts after a week off is a streak with a watering can. A grid of daily squares is a chain drawn as
squares. A "your best fortnight" caption is a record, and a record can be broken.

The ratified ruling states why in one line, and it is the sentence this plan is built to protect:

> Once a streak is on screen there is a reason not to log the distraction that breaks it, or not to
> open the app at all on a bad day — and the instrument starts measuring the wish to protect a
> number.

The standing rule is *if a scope decision threatens the log, the log wins.* A garden that can be
lost gives a person a reason to protect it. A garden that can only ever be added to gives them none.

## Proposed spec delta

### The garden delta — the garden accumulates, and nothing gamified may be lost

**Proposed 2026-09-09. Not ratified.** Required before `F16-T1` starts.

**The number was the garden delta in the drafted plan, and the garden delta is already taken.** `docs/plans/F12.md` line 304
proposes *"the garden delta — the app has a theme, chosen from a fixed audited set"*, and `docs/plans/F13.md`
line 162 conditionally claims the same number if the owner decides to number its §3.2 crossing. All
three were written against the same true fact — the register's highest allocated decision is `D31`,
ratified 2026-09-09 — and none of them could see the others. This plan therefore claims **no number**, and
records the reason rather than renumbering silently, because `docs/plans/00-register.md` already
carries a *"Defect — reused IDs"* section and a second one is not wanted. **Final allocation is the
owner's at ratification** — whichever of `F12`, `F13` and `F16` is ratified first may end up with
the garden delta; the requirement this plan asserts is only that no two ratified rows share an ID.

**Currently**, `SPEC.md` line 58 ends its out-of-scope list with:

> …themes · **streaks, badges, or any gamification layered on top of Todoist's own.**

**Proposed replacement:**

> …themes · streaks, badges, goals, records, targets, comparisons, or any gamification layered on
> top of Todoist's own — **with one exception, ratified as the garden delta: a display of finished work that
> only ever accumulates.** It may grow when a pom is finished. It may not shrink, decay, reset,
> break, or read differently because of *when* the poms happened, and it may not be shown per day.
> The exception is enforced by a test, not by this sentence.

**Two deltas want to edit the same line, and the owner should know before ratifying either.**
`SPEC.md` line 58 also names **themes**, which `F12` owes a delta against. This replacement keeps the
word *themes* on the list untouched, so the two are composable in either order — but if `F12`'s delta
ratifies first and rewrites line 58 itself, the quoted "currently" text above is stale and this
replacement is applied to `F12`'s wording rather than to the text shown here. Flagged rather than
resolved: the ordering is the owner's.

### The argument, which is about the mechanism and not about the word

**The clause was not written against pictures. It was written against a number a person can lose.**
Every device on that list — a streak, a badge you have not earned yet, a goal, a personal best —
shares one property: **its value depends on what you do next.** That is the entire source of its
motivating force, and it is also the entire source of the harm. A quantity that can fall is a
quantity worth protecting, and the two cheapest ways to protect it are both attacks on the log:
don't tap the distraction button, and don't open the app on a bad day.

**An accumulate-only garden has no such quantity.** There is nothing a bad day can take off it. Not
opening the app for three weeks leaves it precisely as it was; opening it after three weeks shows
the same garden, with no message about the gap, because the garden does not know there was one.
Tapping Internal or External eleven times in one pom changes it by nothing at all, because the
garden is a function of finished poms and of nothing else. **There is no behaviour a person could
adopt to protect it except doing more work**, which is the only incentive the app is entitled to
create.

**So the honest form of the clause is not "no gamification".** It is *no quantity that can be lost*.
That is a mechanical test, it can be written down, and — unlike the word *gamification*, which
somebody will argue about in six months — it can be enforced by a program.

### What the delta deliberately does not license

Recorded so that the exception is not read wider than it is written. Each of these is refused by
the garden delta itself, and the fence in `F16-T2` fails the build on it:

- **A per-day grid, calendar, or heat map.** This is the single likeliest reappearance and it is
  refused explicitly. A grid of days *is* an unbroken chain — the gaps are the point of it, and a
  reader counts the run without being told to. It fails the ruling in its plainest form.
- **Decay, wilting, or anything that reads as neglect.** A garden that dies when unwatered is a
  streak with the sign flipped: the fear of losing it is the mechanism.
- **Thresholds phrased as achievements.** See the open question `Q2` — a threshold that cannot be
  lost is arguably not a badge, and it is close enough to the line that the owner rules, not the
  agent.
- **Any comparison to a previous span**, best day, average, or target.
- **Growth from anything except a finished pom.** Notably not from a logged distraction: see `Q4`.

### The price of this delta, stated rather than buried

**An out-of-scope list with an exception in it is weaker than one without.** That is a real cost and
the honest reason to accept it is that the exception arrives with an executable fence attached — it
would be the first item on that list that is enforced by something other than the sentence. Prose
did not hold this line in F3 or F5, which `StatsFenceTests`' own header says out loud. the garden delta trades
one prose absolute for one prose exception *plus* a test that fails the build.

**The counter-argument, kept on the record:** a growing picture is still an extrinsic reward, and a
person could run short or padded poms to grow it faster. Three things blunt it, and none of them is
a claim that the risk is zero. The counting rule already refuses to count abandoned blocks —
`CountedBlock.init?` in `StatsQuery.swift` is where breaks and stops become nothing. Padding shows
up in the log itself as shorter focused seconds and more stops, on the same page. And the garden
renders a number the app already computes and already shows in large type at the top of the
Pomodoros sheet; it changes the rendering, not the record.

## The design ruling this plan asks for: derive, never store

**Recommended: derive the garden from the pomodoro records that already exist. Store nothing.** The
recommendation is not a preference; three separate things in this tree already decided it and one of
them would fail the build.

**`SessionPlan` made this argument in the neighbouring case**, and its doc comment is the precedent
the prompt points at:

> **There is one plan.** Making a new one replaces the old one. A plan is not history — what
> actually happened is already written down, block by block, on the finished-block rows, and a plan
> that outlived its session would be a second, competing account of the same day.

A stored garden is exactly that shape. It would be a row saying *the garden is this big*, sitting
beside the rows saying *these blocks were finished* — two accounts of the same fact, able to
disagree the first time a write is missed, a migration drops a column, or a restore runs. And when
they disagree there is no question about which one is right: **the log wins, always**, so the stored
copy was never the authority and only ever a thing that could be wrong.

**`StatsPeriod` says the same thing about totals in general**, and it is the file the count would
come from:

> Every total below is **computed by adding up the rows**, never stored alongside them. A stored
> total is a second answer, and a second answer can be stale.

**And `PolishFenceTests` would fail.** `noNewStoredShape` asserts `countAcrossApp("^@Model") == 12`.
A stored garden needs a thirteenth `@Model` type, so the fence built to catch exactly this — new
machinery arriving during a pass licensed to make things look better — stops it at the build. That
is not a coincidence; it is the fence working as designed.

**What deriving costs, measured rather than assumed.** This is the real objection and it deserves a
number rather than a shrug. `ExportCostTests` measured the counting path over 1,044 blocks — a year
of use — and recorded **57.5 ms for an all-time query**, roughly 0.22 ms per day of range, against
3.1 ms for the default fortnight. A lifetime garden needs the all-time answer, so at a year of
history it costs **about three and a half frames** on the screen's first draw — `ExportCostTests`'
own words are *"3.6× the frame budget"* — and roughly three times that at three years. *(Corrected at
review: the drafted sentence read "about a third of a second's worth of frames", which is an order of
magnitude above the measurement it cites and above this plan's own `Q5` figures of ~60 ms and
~180 ms.)*

Three things make that acceptable and one of them is a limit rather than an argument:

1. **It is off the timer's path entirely.** The garden is opened deliberately, like the export, and
   nothing about a running block waits on it. The cost lands where `ExportCostTests` already ruled a
   comparable cost acceptable, and for the same reason.
2. **It is asked once per open, from a `.task`, never from a `body`.** `StatsView` already
   establishes that pattern and says why: *"a query inside `body` runs on every redraw, and no
   amount of speed saves it."*
3. **`F16-T1` measures it and records the number before anything is drawn.** If the measured
   lifetime query on a realistic store is worse than the extrapolation, that is a finding at `T1`
   and it goes to the owner as `Q5`, not into a cache invented on the spot.

**The alternative I am rejecting, and its price.** A stored running total would make the garden open
instantly at any history size, and it is genuinely the faster design. It loses on three counts: it
is the second competing account `SessionPlan` refused, it needs the thirteenth `@Model` the polish
fence forbids, and it is the one design in which the garden can silently disagree with the log —
which is the failure mode the entire feature exists to avoid. **A garden that is wrong is worse than
a garden that takes 60 ms to appear**, and the trade is not close.

**One thing I could not verify, stated rather than asserted.** `StatsPeriod`'s doc comment records
that `StatsRange.everything` **has been removed** — *"it is used only by tests now that
`StatsRange.everything` has gone"* — and a grep for it in the app source returns nothing. So there
is at present **no all-time range in the shipping code**, and `F16-T1` re-introduces one. That is a
design decision at this gate rather than a detail: the lifetime span has to be built from the
earliest recorded block, which means one bounded read (`FetchDescriptor` sorted ascending with
`fetchLimit` 1) inside `StatsQuery` — the only file in the counting feature permitted to open the
database, per `StatsFenceTests.oneFileInTheFeatureOpensTheDatabase`. I have not measured that read
and will not claim it is free; `T1` measures it.

## The hook this feature owes

**Named in the v1.5 spec's hook intentions:** *a test that fails if any user-facing surface counts
consecutive days or renders an unbroken chain.* It is built in `F16-T2` as
`ZenTomatoTests/GardenFenceTests.swift`, and it has **three parts on purpose**, because any one of
them alone is defeated by a five-minute edit. *(The drafted plan said "two halves" and then wrote
three; the third is not an afterthought — it is the only part that tests the promise rather than the
spelling, so the count is corrected upward rather than the part dropped.)*

**Every test named below is named here so the mutation table can point at it.** A mutation whose
named test does not exist is not a mutation.

*A note on one word:* where this section says **shape** it means the ordinary English sense — the
form of the code, the form of the drawing. It is **not** `definitions.md`'s ratified *shape* (how a
stretch of time is cut into blocks; that is `F8`). F16 produces no shape in the ratified sense, and
nothing in the garden knows about time at all.

### Part one — the vocabulary, which catches the honest version

**`noSurfaceCountsConsecutiveDays`.** A whole-word, case-insensitive search over the garden's own files and over the files it touches, for
`streak`, `consecutive`, `inARow`, `chain`, `unbroken`, `sinceLast`, `currentRun`, `dailyGoal`,
`daysActive`, `perfectWeek`, and the existing forbidden list `StatsFenceTests` already carries.

**Comments are stripped before anything is searched**, reusing the mechanism `StatsFenceTests`
already ships and already passes in CI — its header explains why in a sentence worth repeating:
*"a fence that cannot tell a refusal from a violation is a fence somebody switches off within a
month."* This plan and the garden's own doc comments will state the ruling in the forbidden words,
and must not fail because of it.

**Part one is necessary and it is not sufficient.** A rename defeats it in one commit. Which is why:

### Part two — the shape, which catches the renamed version

**The garden is a function of one integer and nothing else.** Its growth function takes the count of
finished poms and returns what to draw. It is handed no `StatsDay`, no `StatsDayRow`, no array of
days, no `Date`, no `Calendar`, and no `StatsPeriod`. **A thing that cannot see days cannot count
consecutive ones**, whatever the variable is called — this is the same move `StatsPeriod` makes to
guarantee one counting rule, made structural rather than asked for in a comment.

Two named tests carry it:

- **`theGardenSeesNoDays`** — the garden directory contains no `Calendar`, no `Date(`, no
  `StatsDay`, no `StatsDayRow`, no `.days`, and no `import SwiftData`.
- **`theGardenReadsOnlyTheCountingPath`** — the garden's number comes from `StatsQuery`, and from
  nothing else. No file under `ZenTomato/Garden/` declares a `@Model`, reads `AppSettings`, or holds
  a stored total; the only count it is handed is the one `StatsPeriod.pomodoroCount` produced. This
  is the test `F16-M4` needs and the drafted plan named without defining.

### Part three — the behaviour, which is the actual promise

Two properties. **Both are driven through the real path — seed a store, ask `StatsQuery`, build the
garden from what comes back — and not by calling the growth function twice with the same literal.**
The drafted plan said these were tested *"against the growth function"*, and against a function of
`Int` alone, *"forty poms in two days"* and *"forty poms over eight months"* are both the expression
`f(40)`: identical by construction, distinguishing nothing, and green under every mutation in the
table. `docs/conventions.md` names that shape exactly — *"a fixture that satisfies both the right and
the wrong implementation distinguishes nothing."*

- **`theGardenIsAFunctionOfCountAlone`** — two seeded stores, forty poms crammed into two days and
  forty poms scattered over eight months with three-week gaps, each taken through the query and into
  the garden, produce **byte-identical** garden state. Any adjacency logic anywhere makes these two
  differ, whatever it is named.
- **`theGardenNeverShrinks`** — for every pair of counts *m ≤ n*, the garden at *n* is at least as
  large as the garden at *m*, on every measure the renderer exposes. Monotonic by assertion, not by
  intention. **This one is a property of the growth function and is stated as such** — it is the
  weakest of the three, because a shrink expressed anywhere *other* than in that function passes it:
  see `F16-M3`.

### The mutations

Named now, and **run against the shipped code before any of these tasks is called done**, with the
suite count recorded in the PR. Numbered per plan, following `F4c.md`'s precedent — there is no
`## Mutations` section in `docs/plans/00-register.md` today, and this plan does not invent one.

| Mutation — a specific edit to the shipped code | The named test that must fail |
|---|---|
| `F16-M1` · Add a `consecutiveDays` value derived from the day rows and let it scale the garden | `noSurfaceCountsConsecutiveDays` (part one) **and** `theGardenIsAFunctionOfCountAlone` (part three) |
| `F16-M2` · Change the growth function to take `[StatsDayRow]` instead of `Int`, keeping every name identical | `theGardenSeesNoDays` (part two) — part one passes, which is the point of having part two |
| `F16-M3` · Make the garden shrink by one plant when the most recent pom is more than seven days old | `theGardenSeesNoDays` (part two) **and** `F16-T5`'s assembled-screen assertion. **Not `theGardenNeverShrinks` alone** — see below |
| `F16-M4` · Feed the garden from a stored count written at each block boundary instead of from `StatsQuery` | `noNewStoredShape` in `PolishFenceTests` (the `@Model` count moves from 12 to 13) **and** `theGardenReadsOnlyTheCountingPath` |

`F16-M2` is the one that earns the design. It is the mutation a well-meaning person actually makes —
"the garden should know about days, it might be useful later" — and it passes part one untouched.

**`F16-M3` was mis-paired in the drafted plan, and the correction is the point of naming mutations at
all.** A shrink that depends on *how old the last pom is* cannot be written inside a function of
`Int` — so `theGardenNeverShrinks`, which varies only the count, would have stayed **green** while
the mutation shipped. The mutation has to be written where a date is reachable, which is the view or
the model feeding it, and that is caught by part two (a date in the garden directory) and by `T5`'s
assertion on the assembled screen. **If `F16-M3` is applied and `theGardenNeverShrinks` is the only
test that fails, the pairing in this table is wrong again and the row is rewritten, not the result.**

### Foundational — nothing user-visible

#### `F16-T1` — The lifetime count, measured · owner: **agent**

Re-introduce an all-time span and get one number out of it: **finished poms, ever.** No UI, no
drawing, nothing on screen.

The span is built from the earliest recorded block rather than from a hardcoded start date, because
a hardcoded date is a claim about somebody's history that stops being true when they restore a
backup. That is one bounded read inside `StatsQuery` — the only file in the counting feature allowed
to open the database — and the count itself is `StatsPeriod.pomodoroCount`, the same function today's
number and the Rhodia export already come from. **There is deliberately no second counter**, which is
the rule `StatsQuery`'s header exists to state and `StatsFenceTests` exists to enforce.

**This task measures before it decides.** `ExportCostTests` is the instrument and the precedent: it
measured the all-time path at 57.5 ms over a year of blocks and then explicitly declined to optimise
it, recording *"the number is recorded so the next person can decide with it rather than about it."*
This task adds the lifetime query to that measurement, over the same seeded store, and writes the
result into the PR. **If the number is bad, that is a finding and it goes to `Q5`.** No cache, no
actor, no stored total is introduced on the strength of a suspicion — that is the trade
`F6-contract.md` already ruled on.

*Checkpoint:* a test that seeds a year of blocks, asks for the lifetime count, and asserts the exact
number — with the measured cost of that query printed beside the existing fortnight and all-time
figures. Nothing on screen has changed.

#### `F16-T2` — The growth function and the fence · owner: **agent**

A pure function in a new `ZenTomato/Garden/` directory: `Int → garden state`. No SwiftData, no
calendar, no clock, no view. It says how many of what to draw, and nothing about when anything
happened.

`GardenFenceTests` is built in this task, not after it, and all four mutations above are run against
the shipped code with the suite count recorded. **A fence written after the thing it fences is a
fence that has only ever been seen to pass.**

*Checkpoint:* the growth function returns a stated shape for 0, 1, 40 and 1,044 poms; the fence is
green; and each of `F16-M1` through `F16-M4` has been applied, seen to fail its named test, and
reverted — with the counts in the PR.

### Vertical slices — each one a thing you can hold

#### `F16-T3` — The garden on screen · owner: **agent**

The garden gets a screen of its own, in `ZenTomato/Views/GardenView.swift`, reached by a door the
owner chooses at `Q1`.

**It is not a section on the Pomodoros sheet, and the reason is written in that sheet's own source.**
`StatsScreen`'s doc comment enumerates what is deliberately absent — *"no chart, no sparkline, no
trend line, no progress ring, no heat grid… If a number wants emphasis it gets typography, not a
graphic"* — and `StatsFenceTests` enforces that list across `ZenTomato/Stats`, `ZenTomato/Export`,
`ZenTomato/Sprint` and every `Views/Stats*.swift`. Putting a picture that grows with a value onto
that screen would contradict the sentence and fail the fence. The counting screen keeps its rule;
the garden lives outside it, under a different rule that the garden delta states and `GardenFenceTests`
enforces. **Two surfaces with two honest rules is better than one surface with an exception in it.**

The door itself is one plain labelled control. If it goes on the timer screen it must respect the
fences already covering that file: `TimerScreen.swift` is in `StatsFenceTests.featureFiles`, so no
`Gauge(`, no `ProgressView(`, no `Canvas(`, no `.trim(from:` and none of the forbidden vocabulary may
appear there.

**No capture surface is created.** There is no text field, no naming of plants, no arranging, no
search. The screen accepts one gesture — leaving it.

*Checkpoint:* on the simulator, open the garden on a seeded store and read back a picture whose size
matches the number on the Pomodoros sheet; open it on an empty store and get the honest empty state
rather than a zero dressed up as a beginning.

#### `F16-T4` — A finished pom makes the garden bigger · owner: **agent**

End to end, from the engine's write to the picture: run a block to its end, and the garden is one
pom larger than it was. **Stop a block early and the garden is unchanged**, because an abandoned
block is not a pom — that rule already lives in `CountedBlock.init?` and this feature must not grow a
second opinion about it.

This is the task where the garden and the log are checked to agree. The garden's number and
`StatsPeriod.pomodoroCount` are the same function called twice, and a test asserts they are equal on
the same store. If they can ever differ, the derivation ruling has been broken somewhere.

*Checkpoint:* a one-minute block on the simulator, run to completion; garden +1. A second block
stopped at thirty seconds with a reason; garden unchanged, and the stop still present in the log.

#### `F16-T5` — A bad day changes nothing · owner: **agent**

**This is the task that is the ruling.** A seeded store where forty poms are followed by a
twenty-three day gap, then one more pom. Assertions: the garden after the gap is exactly the garden
before it, plus one; no surface anywhere says how long the gap was, when the last pom was, or that
there was a gap; and the same forty poms rearranged into a different set of dates produce byte-
identical garden state.

It is a slice rather than part of `T2` because it is checked against the **assembled screen**, not
against the pure function. `docs/conventions.md` is explicit that a feature can judge its primary
output rigorously and emit a second artefact nothing checks — here the second artefact is the drawn
screen, and the assertion is made on what it renders rather than on the model that produced it.

*Checkpoint:* two runs of the app against two seeded stores holding the same poms on different dates,
and the two screens are the same.

#### `F16-T6` — The garden on real history · owner: **human**

`SPEC.md`'s standard: a *Done when* is closed on hardware, by the owner, with a build number and
their own words. Recorded as an `O<n>`.

Three questions, and the third is the only one that matters. Does the garden on your own history look
like the amount of work you have actually done? Is it worth the pause on open, given the number `T1`
measured? And — after the next bad fortnight — **did having it on screen make you less willing to tap
Internal or External, at any moment, for any reason?** A yes to the third is a defect in this feature
and the feature is removed, not tuned.

*Checkpoint:* closed by the owner with a build number, in their own words.

## Scope fence

Deliberately not in F16. Each needs its own gate, and most need a delta of their own on top of the garden delta.

- **A per-day grid, calendar view, or heat map.** *This is the one most likely to be asked for
  mid-build*, and it will not arrive by that name — it arrives as *"could each day be a little
  square?"*, which sounds like a rendering choice and is not one. **A grid of days is an unbroken
  chain.** The gaps are the most legible thing on it, nobody has to be told to count the run, and it
  reintroduces the exact incentive the ruling removed. It is refused by the garden delta in terms and by
  `GardenFenceTests` in code.
- **Notifications about the garden.** *"Your garden misses you"* is a streak with a watering can.
- **Growth from anything but a finished pom** — from distractions logged, tasks completed, minutes
  focused, or sprints finished. See `Q4`.
- **Sharing or exporting the garden.** The Rhodia export is prose and counts, and its format is
  pinned by committed golden files; changing it is a separate decision with its own evidence.
- **The garden on the watch or as a complication.** `SPEC.md` line 58 excludes watch-face widgets and
  `D30` is proposed and unratified.
- **The garden on the timer screen during a block.** A reward in the eyeline while you are meant to
  be working is a different feature with a different argument, and it is not this one.
- **Animation of growth over time, or a history scrubber.** Both need dates, and the fence forbids
  the garden seeing dates at all.
- **Naming, arranging, or choosing plants.** That is a capture surface. The app never accepts input
  from the user beyond what it already accepts.

- **No capture surface.** F16 adds no text field, no editor, no search field, and accepts no task,

- **Todoist writes are complete-task only.** F16 makes no Todoist call of any kind. It reads rows the

- **Todoist owns the hierarchy.** F16 defines no project, section, tag or task model, and reads no

## Open questions for the gate

Batched. Each changes what gets built, and none can be settled by investigation.

**`Q1` — where is the door?** The garden needs one way in. The two candidates are the corner of the
timer screen, beside the history control that `StatsFenceTests` already asserts is always present,
or a control inside the Pomodoros sheet. *I would pick the timer screen*, because the Pomodoros sheet
is the counting surface and putting the door there implies the garden is part of the count. But the
timer screen is the one place the app is deliberately austere during a block, and that is the owner's
call rather than mine.

**`Q2` — is a threshold a badge?** A garden of 1,044 poms cannot draw 1,044 tomatoes, so growth has
to be banded: a plant per N poms, or tiers that appear as the count passes them. **A tier you cross
and keep cannot be lost, so by the garden delta's own mechanism it is not a badge** — and it is close enough to
the line that I am not ruling on it. *This is the disagreement worth naming:* the rule says no
badges, the need says a picture must have some structure or it is a wall of identical shapes. I would
build bands with no names, no announcement when one is crossed, and no visible "next" threshold —
growth you notice rather than growth that tells you. Ratify or refuse that shape here, because it is
much cheaper to settle now than at the review.

**`Q3` — does the garden count poms recorded before F16 shipped?** *I would say yes*: they are real
finished work, they are in the log, and a garden that starts at zero on install day is a garden
telling somebody their previous months did not count. Confirming it because it means the feature
lands on a garden that is already large, which is a different first impression from an empty one.

**`Q4` — does an honestly logged distraction grow anything?** It is a tempting symmetry — the log is
the point of the app, so reward the logging. *I would refuse it*, and the reason is the same
mechanism the garden delta rests on: making a tap grow something makes tapping worth doing for its own sake, and
a tally that can be inflated is as broken as one that can be suppressed. The garden should stay a
picture of finished work. Ruling this here stops it arriving mid-build as an obvious improvement.

**`Q5` — what is an acceptable pause when the garden opens?** `T1` measures it; the extrapolation
from `ExportCostTests` is roughly 60 ms at one year of history and about 180 ms at three. If the
measured number exceeds what the owner will accept, the choice is between accepting the pause,
narrowing what the garden counts, or building machinery this project has twice declined to build
pre-emptively. **I am not choosing on the owner's behalf** and I will not introduce a cache because
the code reads slow.

**`Q6` — does `F15` own the garden's appearance?** The graphics pass is item 9 and this is item 10,
and `docs/ZenTomato redesign scope.zip` may already say what the app's illustrations look like. If the
garden's art comes from there, this feature draws to that specification; if not, it draws from the
existing design tokens and nothing else. **I have not opened that archive** and am not assuming
either answer.
