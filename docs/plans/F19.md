# F19 — Todoist, searched cleanly and undoable

**Status:** plan written, **awaiting the gate.** No code has been written.
**Spec:** `docs/specs/SPEC.md` (v0.1, binding) and `docs/specs/zenpom-v1.5.md` (v1.5, ratified
2026-09-09). Vocabulary from `docs/specs/definitions.md`.
**Source ruling:** the owner, 2026-09-09 — *"we need to test more todoist integration. I never want
zenpom to create a to do list. I do want to make sure that we can search through todoist cleanly and
mark items complete, or reengage the item if one was closed accidentally in zenpom."*
**Delta owed:** **yes, for part 3 only.** Referred to throughout as **the undo-a-completion delta**.
It has no number and must not be given one in this file: `DeltaIntegrityTests` walks `docs/` and fails
the build on a `D`-token with no heading in `00-deltas.md`, where `D31` is currently the highest.

---

## Paraphrasing it back

The owner said three things in one sentence, and they are not one feature. They are one *sentence*
about Todoist and three quite different pieces of work:

1. **Search.** Finding a task in Todoist through this app should be as quick as finding a project or
   a song already is. Small, and owes nothing.
2. **Complete.** The one write already exists and already ships. What does **not** exist is evidence
   that it behaves against a real account under the conditions that would embarrass it. That is a
   *verification* task, not a build task, and there is already an open owner item holding it.
3. **Undo a completion.** zenpom can currently do a thing to somebody's Todoist account that zenpom
   cannot take back. That is the hard one, it is the only one that touches the contract, and the
   whole middle of this plan is about it.

**And one thing the owner said that is not work at all** — *"I never want zenpom to create a to do
list"* — which is the standing rule, restated by the owner unprompted at the same moment they asked
for a second write. That coincidence is the reason §4 exists.

## 1. Search — the fact, checked rather than assumed

**Read before this plan was written, not taken from the prompt:**

- `ZenTomato/Views/ProjectPickerView.swift:57` — `.searchable(…)`, prompt from
  `PickerScreenModel.searchPrompt`, placement `.navigationBarDrawer(displayMode: .always)`.
- `ZenTomato/Views/MusicPickerView.swift:140` — `.searchable(…)`. Shipped by `F4e` under `D23`.
- `ZenTomato/Views/TaskPickerView.swift` — **143 lines, and no `.searchable` anywhere in them.** The
  file draws `picker.groups(inProject:)` and nothing else.

So the state is exactly as the owner describes it: **you can search your projects and you can search
your music, and you cannot search inside a project's tasks.**

**A second fact that changes the shape of the fix.** `ProjectPickerView.swift:116` calls
`picker.rows(matching:)` (`PickerScreenModel.swift:299`), and that function searches **every task in
the mirror plus every project**, not just the current project's. So a global task search already
exists and already ships — it is reachable only from the project list, one screen above the place you
are standing when you want it. What `TaskPickerView` is missing is not the *matching rule*; it is the
field, scoped to the project you have already opened.

**This is why no delta is owed, and the argument is `D23`'s, reused deliberately.** `D23` ratified a
music search field on the reading that `SPEC.md` *"specifies that you pick and from what… it says
nothing about how the picker is navigated."* `SPEC.md`'s locked decision here is stronger still:
*"**All** projects, sections, and tasks are visible in the picker."* A project with two hundred tasks
in it satisfies that clause on paper and defeats it in the hand. A search field is the picker meeting
a requirement already written down.

**The matching rule is not rewritten.** `ZenTomato/Views/SearchMatching.swift` (37 lines) holds
`contains(_:caseAndAccentInsensitively:)` and exists precisely so that a second picker does not
restate it — its own header says two copies *"pass every test on the day they are written and drift
on the third edit."* A third caller uses it; it does not copy it.

## 2. Complete — nothing to build, and this plan does not pretend otherwise

`POST /tasks/{id}/close` is `TodoistAPI.closeTask(id:)`, the single write in
`scripts/todoist-allowed-endpoints.txt`, driven by `ZenTomato/Todoist/TaskCompletion.swift` and
surfaced by `ZenTomato/Views/TaskCompletionSection.swift`. `TodoistEndpointTests.swift:35`
(`theOnlyPostEndpointIsClose`) binds the method to the address. There is no code here to write.

**The owner asked for it to be tested, and `O12` is already that item.** `docs/plans/00-register.md`
carries `O12` — *"Three Todoist API facts, against a live token"*, P1, open, `@verify` — pointing at
`scripts/check-todoist-facts.sh`, which is written, read-only by construction, and has never been
run against the owner's account.

**How this plan relates to `O12`: it extends it in place. It does not open a second item.** A
duplicate would be worse than nothing — two rows for one afternoon at a live token, and neither able
to say whether the other was the one that got run.

Concretely, `F19-T3` below is *"run `check-todoist-facts.sh` **and** add a fourth question to it"*,
and its result is recorded **on `O12`**, not on a new `O`. The fourth question is the one this plan
adds and `O12` does not currently ask:

> **Claim 4 — what does Todoist say when you close a task that is already closed?**

It matters here and nowhere else: it is the difference between an undo control that can tell *"this
already came back"* from *"this failed"*, and one that guesses. `TaskCompletion.Outcome.alreadyGone`
already exists for the 404 case on close; the reopen path needs to know whether the same shape holds
in the other direction. **I have not established this by reading, and I am not going to assert it
here.** It is a claim about somebody else's system, and `conventions.md` is explicit that such a
claim is not ratifiable until a command has been run and its output recorded.

**One consequence for the gate:** the undo-a-completion delta cannot be *ratified* until `F19-T3` has
run, because the delta asserts that Todoist will reopen a task this app closed. Everything else in
this plan can proceed without it.

## 3. Undo a completion — the delta, argued

### 3.1 The argument for, which is the strongest one this project has heard

**zenpom can currently do harm it cannot undo, and the harm is zenpom's fault rather than the
user's.** The complete button sits on the end-of-block sheet, which is the one surface that appears
while somebody is mid-sprint, possibly with an alarm still in their ears, deciding three things at
once. A mis-tap there closes a real task in a real account. The app's own shipped copy admits it —
`ZenTomato/Views/TaskCompletionSection.swift:237`:

> `private static let undoNote = "This can only be undone in Todoist."`

That sentence is honest and it is also the whole complaint. The app made the mistake and hands the
repair to another app.

**It is a stronger case than any other Todoist request this project has refused**, and the reason is
worth stating because it is what stops it being a precedent for everything: every refused request so
far has asked zenpom to do something *new* to somebody's account. This one asks zenpom to undo
something **zenpom itself did**. Those are different rules, and only one of them widens what the app
can reach.

### 3.2 The argument against, which is the file's own words

`scripts/todoist-allowed-endpoints.txt` holds four lines and says, above the fourth:

> `# --- The one and only write. ---`
> `# Completing a task. Nothing else on this side of the file, ever, in v0.1.`

and, above all of them:

> `# Adding a line to this file is therefore a deliberate, visible change… That visibility IS the
> control. Do not add a line to make a build pass.`

`SPEC.md`'s locked decisions: *"Todoist writes — **Complete a task** only. No create, no edit, no
comment. Enforced by hook, not by prose."* `zenpom-v1.5.md`'s standing rules repeat it.

So the honest statement of what is being proposed: **a fifth endpoint and a second write, against a
file that says in its own header that there will never be one.** That is not a technicality to be
argued past. It is the control working exactly as designed — the change is visible, and it is being
made in front of the owner instead of behind them.

**The "ever, in v0.1" clause is not a get-out.** This is v1.5, and reading the clause as expired
would be the cheapest possible way to lose the rule. The delta must win on its merits.

### 3.3 The recommended narrow form — evaluated, not adopted

The prompt proposes: reopen **only** a task zenpom itself completed, **only** within a bounded
window. Evaluating it rather than accepting it:

**It is the right shape, and the reason is not moderation for its own sake.** A general reopen turns
the app into a thing that can change the state of any task in the account. Once that exists, "let me
see my completed tasks so I can pick one to reopen" is the obvious next request, and that screen is a
task browser with a write on it — the capture surface arriving through the back door, exactly as the
prompt says. The narrow form is not a smaller version of a general reopen; it is a **different
feature**, whose subject is *this app's own last action* rather than *your Todoist*.

**Where the narrow form is weaker than it looks, said plainly:**

- **A bounded window makes a real case unreachable.** Somebody who mis-taps at the end of a block and
  notices at the end of the sprint is inside any sane window. Somebody who notices the next morning is
  not, and for them the app is exactly as it is today. The window buys containment and pays in the
  tail of the very problem it is fixing. That is a trade the owner should make knowingly — it is the
  first open question in §7.
- **Provenance and the window are two separate guarantees**, and only the first is a fence. "zenpom
  closed this" is a fact the app holds. "within twenty minutes" is a policy the app chooses. Blurring
  them would produce a fence nobody can test, so `F19-T1` builds them as two predicates and tests
  them separately.

**Adopted, subject to the owner's ruling on the window's length and on §7's questions.**

### 3.4 How the code proves "zenpom completed this one" — by construction, not by placement

**The UI must not be the guarantee.** A control that only appears in the right place is a control
that appears in the wrong place the moment somebody reuses the view — and this project has already
been bitten by a fence that lived in a screen rather than in the type it was fencing.

`ZenTomato/Models/CompletedTaskRecord.swift` is the record that makes provenance provable, and it was
built for a neighbouring purpose that happens to be exactly right here. Its own header:

> *"**Written only after Todoist confirms.** The row is inserted after the close command has
> succeeded, never before and never optimistically."*

So a `CompletedTaskRecord` **is** the assertion "zenpom closed this task, at this time, and Todoist
said so." Nothing else in the tree can make that claim, and no other code path writes one — the
insert happens in exactly one place, `TaskCompletion.recordLocally(taskID:titleSnapshot:at:)`.

**The construction, stated so it can be reviewed:**

- The reopen function's parameter is **a `CompletedTaskRecord` fetched from the store, never a task
  id `String`.** A caller cannot name an arbitrary task, because there is no parameter to name it in.
  This is the same trick `TodoistAPI.Endpoint`'s `fileprivate init` already uses to make a fifth
  address unconstructable — the guarantee is *"there is no way to express the bad call"*, not
  *"nobody makes it"*.
- The task id sent to Todoist is read **off that record**, inside the function, and never passed in.
- The window is evaluated **inside** the same function against `record.completedAt`, so a caller
  cannot supply its own clock or its own deadline.
- `TodoistAPI.isOpaqueIdentifier(_:)` already exists and already guards the one substituted address;
  the reopen address is substituted the same way and goes through the same check.

**The fence test, `reopenCannotBeCalledForATaskZenpomDidNotClose`,** asserts the shape rather than
the behaviour: the reopen entry point takes a persisted record and there is no overload, no
convenience initialiser and no id-taking path anywhere in the type. Written as a compile-shaped
assertion where possible, in the manner of `NoCaptureSurfaceTests.thePickerCanOnlyDrawProjectsSectionsAndTasks`,
whose switch has no `default` so that a fourth row kind stops the bundle compiling.

### 3.5 The exact allowlist line, and what fails if it is wrong

The delta adds **one line**, in a new section of `scripts/todoist-allowed-endpoints.txt`:

```
POST /tasks/{id}/reopen
```

**This is Todoist API v1's documented reopen address as I understand it, and I have not run it.**
That sentence is deliberate: `conventions.md` requires that a decision asserting what another system
can do carries the command and its output, and I have neither. `F19-T3` is where it is established,
and the delta is not ratifiable before then. If the live check returns something other than a
reopen at that path, **the delta changes and this plan is wrong**, rather than the line being adjusted
quietly to match.

**What enforces the pair.** `ZenTomatoTests/TodoistEndpointTests.swift:66`,
`endpointTableMirrorsTheAllowlist`, reads the committed file off disk with `#filePath` and compares it
to `TodoistAPI.allEndpoints` **as a set, in both directions**:

```swift
#expect(allowlisted.count == 4)
#expect(table == allowlisted)
```

So, precisely:

- **Code without the file** — a fifth `Endpoint` constant added and the allowlist untouched: the set
  comparison fails.
- **File without the code** — the line added and no constant: the set comparison fails, *and* the
  hard-coded `count == 4` fails first.
- **Both, changed together** — the `count == 4` still fails until it is edited to `5` in the same
  diff, which is a third deliberate edit in the same pull request.

That third edit is a feature, not friction. It means the number of addresses this app can reach
cannot change without somebody typing the new number.

`theOnlyPostEndpointIsClose` (`TodoistEndpointTests.swift:35`) also fails, because it asserts
`writes.count == 1`. It is **renamed and rewritten**, not deleted: `theOnlyTwoWritesAreCloseAndReopen`,
asserting two writes, naming both, and asserting that neither is `POST /tasks` — the create address,
which is the one this whole apparatus exists to forbid and which must stay unnameable.

`scripts/check-todoist-writes.sh` needs no change: it compares paths against the file it already
reads, and a new listed path is a listed path.

### 3.6 The record problem, which is the part most likely to be got wrong

`CompletedTaskRecord`'s second stated rule is **append-only**: *"A row is written and never changed
and never deleted."* And `ZenTomato/Stats/StatsQuery.swift` reads those rows, so they feed the counts
and the Markdown export — the artefact the whole project exists to produce.

**An undo therefore cannot just work.** Three shapes, all with costs, none of them mine to choose:

| Shape | What it costs |
|---|---|
| **Delete the row** | Contradicts the append-only rule in the file's own header. Also silently rewrites history: a fortnight's export changes meaning after the fact. |
| **Add a `reopenedAt` field to the row** | Mutates a row the header says is never changed. Cheapest for the stats query; weakest for the rule. |
| **Write a second row — a reopen record** | Keeps append-only intact and keeps the history honest: *closed at 14:02, reopened at 14:05* is what actually happened. Costs a second model and a join in `StatsQuery`. |

I would take the third and I am not going to act on that preference — it changes what the export
means, and the export is the one thing `zenpom-v1.5.md`'s hard stop is measured in. It is question 2
in §7.

**What is not in question:** a completion that was undone must not still be counted as a completion
in the export. Whichever shape is ruled, `F19-T5` is not done until
`StatsMarkdownGoldenTests` shows an undone completion absent from the counts, and a mutation proves
that assertion can fail.

## 4. The rule that does not move, and the fence on it

> **zenpom never creates a task and never creates a list. Not in v0.1, not in v1.5, not as a
> convenience, not as an empty state, and not as a side effect of anything in this plan.**

`SPEC.md` out-of-scope: *"creating, editing, or commenting on Todoist tasks · any capture surface of
any kind."* `zenpom-v1.5.md` files it under **Never — not deferred**: *"It is not a feature request;
it is a rule change."* `CLAUDE.md` calls it a standing rule from the owner's productivity system.

**This plan is the single most dangerous place in the project for that rule**, and the reason is
structural rather than rhetorical: it adds a search field to a screen that lists tasks, and it adds
a *write* to the Todoist surface, in the same feature. Every other app on the phone answers an empty
search result with an offer to create the thing you typed, and `PickerScreenModel`'s own comment on
`noMatchHeading` says so: *"This is the single most likely place in the app for the no-capture rule
to be broken."*

**The fence, and it is three existing tests doing more work rather than one new one:**

- `NoCaptureSurfaceTests.thePickerCanOnlyDrawProjectsSectionsAndTasks` already switches over
  `PickerScreenModel.Row` **with no `default`**. `F19-T2` adds no fourth row kind, so a future one
  stops the bundle compiling. Unchanged, and relied upon.
- `NoCaptureSurfaceTests`' by-words half checks a collected list of sentences for the vocabulary of
  creation — and there are **two** such lists, `everySentenceThePickerCanSay` and
  `everySentenceTheHistoryScreenCanSay`. **Neither covers `TaskCompletionSection`**: the
  end-of-block sheet's copy is not read by that fence today. So `F19-T2`'s strings go into the
  picker's list, which already covers that screen — and `F19-T5`'s have nowhere to go, which means
  `F19-T5` adds a third list over the sheet's own sentences. Without it the fence has a hole exactly
  the size of the new feature, and the copy rule below has nothing enforcing it.
- `theOnlyTwoWritesAreCloseAndReopen` asserts, as a named expectation rather than a side effect, that
  **`POST /tasks` is not among the app's addresses**. Creating a task and listing tasks are the same
  path and differ only by method, which `check-todoist-writes.sh`'s own header admits it cannot see.
  This is the test that can.

**And a copy rule for the undo control:** it may say *undo*, *undone*, *put back*, *reopen in
Todoist*. It may never say *add*, *new*, *create*, or *make*. A reopen button labelled with a
creation verb is the rule broken by wording alone, and wording is what the by-words fence reads.

### Foundational — no user-visible change

#### `F19-T1` — The undo rule, as arithmetic · owner: **agent**

Two pure predicates over a `CompletedTaskRecord` and a clock. No SwiftData write, no network, no UI,
no endpoint. **Nothing on any screen changes when this lands**, which is what makes it safe to build
before the delta is ratified: it decides *whether* an undo would be offered, and there is nothing yet
to offer it with.

- `isUndoable(record:now:)` — provenance **and** window, and it is composed of the two below rather
  than written as one condition, so a bug in either is visible.
- The provenance half is *"a `CompletedTaskRecord` exists"*, which is true by having one in hand.
- The window half is arithmetic on `record.completedAt`, against an injected clock, never `Date()`
  read inside.

**Cases that are the tests:** inside the window · one second outside it · exactly on the boundary
(and the boundary is inclusive or exclusive by decision, not by whichever way `<` fell) · a record
whose `completedAt` is in the future, which a clock change can produce · a record for a task already
absent from the mirror, which must not affect eligibility since the mirror drops the row on
completion by design.

**`F19-M1`, run before this task is called done:** widen the window predicate to always return
`true`. `undoIsRefusedOutsideTheWindow` must fail, and the failure count is recorded in the PR.

*Checkpoint:* a record completed 25 minutes ago is refused and one from 30 seconds ago is allowed,
with the clock supplied by the test and no screen anywhere aware that either function exists.

### Vertical slices

#### `F19-T2` — Search inside a project · owner: **agent**

**A real slice, and it is filed as one.** It puts a field on a screen, which is a user-visible
change, so it does not sit in the foundational group however small it is. It is listed first among
the slices because it is the one part of this plan the owner can have without ratifying anything.

`.searchable` on `TaskPickerView`, matching `ProjectPickerView.swift:57`'s spelling exactly, including
the three modifiers that screen carries and the comments explaining why each is there:
`.textInputAutocapitalization(.never)`, `.autocorrectionDisabled()`, `.submitLabel(.search)` — *"a
return key that says 'Go' on an empty result is half of a create button"* — and, like it, never
auto-focused, which is a property of the field rather than a fourth modifier.

**The scope of the search is the decision, not the mechanics.** This field searches **the project you
are standing in**, because that is what a person in a project means by searching. `rows(matching:)`
searches everything and is reached from the screen above; a second global search one level down would
give two fields with different corpora and the same prompt. A new model function
`tasks(inProject:matching:)` sits beside `groups(inProject:)` (`PickerScreenModel.swift:255`) and
reuses `contains(_:caseAndAccentInsensitively:)` from `SearchMatching.swift` — a third caller, not a
third copy.

**Sections while searching.** `groups(inProject:)` draws section headings, and `TaskPickerView`'s own
header explains that an empty section is still drawn because `SPEC.md` locks that all sections are
visible. A search that hides a section whose tasks all matched away is *filtering results*, not
hiding a section — that distinction wants stating in the code, and the empty-result copy stays
`PickerScreenModel.noMatchHeading` with its third line intact.

**No new `Row` kind. No new control. No footer.** The empty result is the existing sentence.

**`F19-M2`:** make the project filter return every task in the mirror rather than the project's.
`searchIsScopedToTheOpenProject` must fail — and the fixture must contain a task in *another* project
whose title matches, or the assertion distinguishes nothing. That is the tautology trap
`conventions.md` names, and this test is written to avoid it by construction.

*Checkpoint:* on the device, open a project with more than forty tasks, type three letters from the
middle of one title, reach it in one gesture — and confirm that a matching task in a different
project does **not** appear.

#### `F19-T3` — The live-token facts, and the fourth question · owner: **human**

Runs `scripts/check-todoist-facts.sh` against the owner's real account, both phases, and records the
output. **This closes `O12`** — the row is updated in place with the verdicts, the date and the build,
and no new owner item is opened.

The agent's half, done first: add **Claim 4** to the script — *close a task, then close it again, and
record the status and the first line of the body*. The script stays read-only in spirit but no longer
in fact, since claim 4 completes a task; the header's promise that *"nothing was created, modified or
deleted"* is now false and must be corrected rather than left standing. **A throwaway task, made by
hand in Todoist by the owner, is the fixture** — the script does not create one, because a diagnostic
script that creates a Todoist task is precisely the sentence this project must never be able to write.

Then, and only then, the reopen half: reopen it, and record what comes back. That is the command the
undo-a-completion delta needs before it can be ratified.

*Checkpoint:* four verdicts pasted into `O12` with the date, and the reopen address confirmed or
refuted **by output, not by documentation**.

#### `F19-T4` — The fifth address · owner: **agent** · **needs the ratified delta**

One `Endpoint` constant, one allowlist line, and the two endpoint tests widened as §3.5 sets out —
`count == 4` becomes `5`, `theOnlyPostEndpointIsClose` becomes `theOnlyTwoWritesAreCloseAndReopen`,
and the new test names `POST /tasks` as forbidden rather than merely absent.

**Nothing calls the new address in this task.** The address exists; no screen can reach it. That is
deliberate — it makes the diff that widens the fence readable on its own, rather than buried in the
diff that uses it.

**`F19-M3`:** delete the allowlist line and leave the constant. `endpointTableMirrorsTheAllowlist`
must fail. Then the reverse: leave the line and delete the constant. It must fail again. **Both
directions are run**, because that test's whole claim is that it works in both, and a claim tested one
way is half a claim.

*Checkpoint:* `make ci` green with five addresses, and the two mutations above seen red with their
counts recorded.

#### `F19-T5` — The undo, end to end · owner: **agent** · **needs the ratified delta**

`TaskReopen`, built as `TaskCompletion`'s mirror image and deliberately not merged into it: a plain
type, one method, no protocol — `TaskCompletion.swift`'s own header says a protocol there *"would be a
shape for writes to Todoist to grow into"*, and that is more true with two writes than with one.

Its method takes a persisted `CompletedTaskRecord` (§3.4) and returns an `Outcome`-shaped answer of
its own. **It never queues and never retries**, for the reason already written into `TaskCompletion`:
*"a write performed later, unwatched, is a write nobody can see."*

The control appears on the same sheet, in the state where `TaskCompletionSection` currently shows
`Completed in Todoist · 14:02`, and only while `isUndoable` is true. `undoNote` at
`TaskCompletionSection.swift:237` — *"This can only be undone in Todoist."* — **stops being true and
is rewritten**, and the new sentence must say what the window is, because a control that vanishes with
no explanation reads as a bug.

Then the record, per whichever shape §7 question 2 is ruled, and `StatsQuery` following it.

**`F19-M4`:** make the reopen path write its record but leave the completion counted. The golden
export assertion must fail. If it passes, the assertion is reading the model rather than the artefact
— the exact failure `conventions.md`'s *validate every artefact* rule was ratified over, cited by
name rather than by its upstream number because this project's register stops at `D31` and a higher
`D`-token in this file fails `everyCitedDeltaIsDefined` — and it is rewritten before this task is
called done.

*Checkpoint:* complete a task, undo it, and read three things: the task open again in Todoist's own
app · the export showing it neither completed nor silently missing · and the undo control gone once
the window has passed.

#### `F19-T6` — A real mis-tap · owner: **human**

`SPEC.md`'s standard: a *Done when* closes on hardware, by the owner, with a build number and their
own words.

Complete the wrong task during a real sprint — the actual failure, not a simulation of it — and undo
it. Answer three things: was the undo reachable when it was wanted · did Todoist agree · and did the
fortnight's export still read correctly afterwards.

*Checkpoint:* recorded as an `O<n>` and closed by the owner with the build number.

## 7. Open questions for the owner — the gate

These are not answered here, and `F19-T4` onward do not start until they are.

1. **How long is the window, and what is it anchored to?** Twenty minutes, the end of the current
   sprint, the end of the day, or *"until the app is next launched"* — each is defensible and they
   fail differently. §3.3 records the cost of every bound: the case the window excludes is the
   next-morning realisation, which is a real case.
2. **Which record shape?** Delete the row, add a field, or write a second row (§3.6). It changes what
   the export means, and the export is what v1.5's hard stop is measured in.
3. **Does an undo restore the task to the plan?** `SessionPlanStore` and `SprintCompletions` both read
   completion state. A reopened task reappearing in a plan mid-sprint may be the correct behaviour or
   an unwelcome surprise; I have not formed a view worth acting on.
4. **What happens to a *recurring* task?** `D21` records that closing a recurring task advances it to
   the next occurrence rather than finishing it, and `CompletedTaskRecord.wasRecurring` holds that
   answer. What "undo" means for an advanced recurrence is a question about Todoist's semantics that
   `F19-T3` can partly answer and the owner must rule on.
5. **Does the delta name a milestone, or is the second write permanent?** A ratified delta carrying a
   future milestone is how this project parks things. Whether the reopen address is v1.5-and-onward or
   is reviewed again at v2.0 is the owner's, and it belongs in the delta text rather than in a plan.

## 8. Scope fence

Not in `F19`, each needing its own gate: editing a task · a due date, a label, a priority written back
· a comment · **anything that creates** · a completed-tasks history screen · undoing anything zenpom
did not do · a general reopen · queueing or retrying either write · bulk complete or bulk undo ·
searching Todoist over the network rather than the mirror.

**"Let me see my completed tasks and pick one to reopen" is the one most likely to be asked for
mid-build**, and it will be asked for the moment the undo control exists and somebody misses the
window by four minutes. It sounds like a list. It is a task browser with a write on it, and the write
is no longer scoped to *this app's last action* — which is the entire argument the delta is being
ratified on. Granting it mid-build would retroactively widen a delta the owner ratified for something
narrower.

**And the search half has its own:** the empty result offers nothing. Not a button, not a disabled
button, not a footer, not a `ContentUnavailableView` with its action slot filled. That slot is the
back door, and `PickerScreenModel` already says so in its own comments.
