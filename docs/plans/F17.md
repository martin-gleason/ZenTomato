# F17 — A watch-face complication

**Status:** plan written, awaiting the gate. No code has been written.
**Spec:** `docs/specs/zenpom-v1.5.md`, ratified 2026-09-09, item 11 of the order.
**Baseline still binding:** `docs/specs/SPEC.md` (v0.1) and `docs/specs/definitions.md`.
**Delta owed:** yes. `D30` is proposed and unratified. **This plan asks for `D30` to be superseded
rather than ratified as written** — the argument is the first section below.
**Source request:** the owner, 2026-08-28 — *"also, make sure zenpom can be installed as a
complication on a ultra watch face"* — recorded as `O32`, P1, open.
**Pairs with:** `F14`, *the watch can launch a pom*, item 6 of the same order.

---

## Paraphrasing it back

Today the wrist shows a block only while the watch app is open, and the watch app is opened by
finding it in the app list. What the owner asked for is the block on the **watch face itself**: a
glance at the wrist, no app, and — on the reading the v1.5 spec took — a way to begin a pom from
there too.

Concretely: a small self-updating countdown in a face slot, labelled with the kind of block and,
where the slot has room, what it is attached to. When nothing is running it shows something honest
rather than a stale number. Tapping it either opens the app or starts a pom, and that is the first
open question.

**What it is not.** It is not a second timer. `D2` is untouched and this plan does not reopen it:
the phone still runs the only engine, the face still draws arithmetic from an absolute end instant
the phone sent, and nothing on the face decides anything about a block. It is a third window onto
the same state — after the phone screen and the watch app — and, if the owner rules that way, a
third door onto the same *one* start command.

**And it is the largest of the eleven small items, for one structural reason.** It needs a build
target that does not exist. Every other v1.5 item edits an app that already builds.

---

## `D30` does not cover this, and should be superseded rather than ratified

`D30` was written on 2026-08-28 as an honest refusal. Its analysis of the cost is still correct and
this plan leans on it. Two of its parts have since gone out of date, and one of them would ship the
wrong feature if the owner ratified the text as it stands.

**First, and this is the substantive one: `D30`'s proposed spec text forbids the half of the feature
the ratified v1.5 spec bought.** `D30` proposes adding to `F7`:

> A complication on the watch face shows the current block and its remaining time, and opens the app
> when tapped. **It starts nothing and captures nothing.**

`docs/specs/zenpom-v1.5.md`, ratified 2026-09-09, states `F17`'s earned entry as:

> Lets a block be **seen and started** from the watch face.

Those two sentences cannot both be the contract. Ratifying `D30` unchanged would ratify a
complication that may not start a block, in the same milestone whose spec says starting a block is
what it is for — and the contradiction would be discovered at the review, which is exactly what the
v1.5 spec's *Deltas this milestone owes* table exists to prevent.

**Second, `D30`'s recommendation section is anchored to a stop condition that no longer exists.** It
argues, at length and correctly for the day it was written, *"the hard stop is September 13, 2026 —
sixteen days"*, and that `D29` was worth more. v1.5 replaced that date with a condition: *v1.5 ends
when zenpom has driven four consecutive fortnightly Rhodia reviews from its own export.* A delta
whose ratification argument turns on a date that has been superseded is a delta that would be read,
years later, as saying something it does not mean. `conventions.md` is explicit that a document
written when an item was opened says what was true then, in the present tense, forever.

**So: a new `D<n>`, number allocated by the owner at ratification, in build order — three plans in this batch each reasoned independently to the same next-free number and none could see the others (`D31` is the highest today, in
`docs/plans/00-deltas.md` — proposed at this gate, superseding `D30`.** `D30` stays in the register,
marked superseded with a link forward, per the ADR lifecycle in `conventions.md`. It is not edited
and not deleted: its cost analysis is the best short account of this feature's risk in the
repository, and it should stay legible.

**The alternative was to amend `D30` in place**, which is legal — it is proposed, not ratified, and
the immutability rule bites at ratification. It loses anyway. The amendment would have to delete the
recommendation-not-to-build section, and that section is the record of a judgement the owner
overturned by ratifying v1.5. Deleting it hides a decision that was actually made. Superseding keeps
both on the record with their dates attached, which is the whole point of the register.

**What the complication delta should say**, drafted here for the owner to rule on rather than assumed. **The quoting
is not cosmetic and the reason is two paragraphs below** — the `**Currently:**` marker is literal and
the spec fragment is inside backticks, because that is what the detector reads:

> **Currently:** `SPEC.md` line 58 reads, in part,
> `widgets beyond the Lock Screen Live Activity`.
>
> **Proposed:** replace that fragment with
> `widgets beyond the Lock Screen Live Activity and a watch-face complication showing the running block`.
>
> And, as `F17` in the v1.5 spec's own list rather than as an edit to `F7`: a complication on the
> watch face shows the current block, its kind and its remaining time; it starts a pom through the
> same single engine entry point `F14` and `F10` use, and through no other; it captures no
> distraction, displays no tally, and creates no task.

Note what that last clause does. It keeps `D30`'s *"captures nothing"* — which was right and which
this plan defends at length in the scope fence — and drops only *"starts nothing"*, which the
ratified v1.5 spec had already overtaken. **The agent never edits `SPEC.md`.** The text above is a
proposal; the owner ratifies and the owner applies it.

**And ratifying it without applying it turns the suite red on the next run.** This is checked, and it
was checked before this paragraph was written. `docs/specs/AMENDMENT-BASELINE.txt` holds `0`, and
`DeltaIntegrityTests.everyRatifiedSpecAmendmentIsApplied` asserts
`outstanding.count <= baseline` — so **the first ratified delta whose replacement text has not
reached `SPEC.md` fails immediately**, by design. So the complication delta's ratification is one change, not two: the
delta is marked ratified, the replacement fragment goes into `SPEC.md` line 58, and the complication delta is added to
the `## Amendments applied` list at `SPEC.md` line 78. All three, or CI is red and the branch cannot
merge under branch protection.

**The worse failure is the silent one, and `D30` is already sitting in it.** The detector finds the
spec fragment through `DeltaIntegrityTests.fragments(of:)`, which reads **backticked spans and
straight-quoted spans of twelve characters or more, and nothing else.** `D30`'s own block is
`**Currently:** *…widgets beyond the Lock Screen Live Activity…*` — italics, no backticks, no straight
quotes — so it yields **no fragment at all** and would not be counted even after ratification. `D31`
misses for a second reason: it writes `**Currently**,` without the colon inside the bold, and the
marker is matched literally. Neither is a live failure today, because `D30` is unratified and `D31`
quotes the v1.5 spec rather than `SPEC.md`. **Both are the shape the complication delta would have inherited had it
been drafted in the house style**, and `AMENDMENT-BASELINE.txt` warns about exactly this: *"A future
delta that paraphrases the spec rather than quoting it will be invisible the same way. Quote
exactly."* Hence the backticks above.

`docs/specs/AMENDMENTS-TO-APPLY.md` is **not** the mechanism, and this plan said it was in an earlier
draft. That file states plainly that *"Nothing here is outstanding… the file is kept as the record of
what each amendment changed and why, not as a to-do list."* What it is worth is its **format** — each
block gives the exact current line and the exact replacement, so applying one is a copy and a paste
rather than a judgement. the complication delta should be written to that format for that reason, in `00-deltas.md`,
where the detector actually reads.

**If the owner declines the complication delta, delete this file.** Nothing else in the project depends on it, by
design — the same clause `F7.md` carries.

## What this actually costs, stated before the tasks

`D30` named four costs. All four are still true; two of them are worse than it said, and this section
says so rather than letting the tasks imply the feature is four screens of drawing.

### A new target, and the failure mode this project has already shipped three times

`project.yml` is the whole of how this app is built, and it carries three separate comments about
silent install failures on the watch: a missing `dependencies:` embed, missing provisioning, and an
asset catalogue the target could not see. Each of the three **built cleanly** and shipped something
that looked installed and was wrong. The comment on the iOS Live Activity says it exactly:

> WITHOUT THIS LINE THE EXTENSION COMPILES AND IS NEVER INSTALLED, which presents as "the Live
> Activity simply does not appear" with no error anywhere — no crash, no warning, nothing in the log.

A watchOS widget extension is a fourth chance at the same failure, and the symptom is
indistinguishable from the feature not being finished: *the complication is not in the gallery.* That
is why the first vertical slice below ships a deliberately ugly placeholder before any design work
happens, and why `F17-T2` carries a fence test rather than a promise.

The new target is `ZenTomatoComplication`: `type: app-extension`, `platform: watchOS`, its own
generated `Support/ZenTomatoComplication-Info.plist` with
`NSExtensionPointIdentifier: com.apple.widgetkit-extension`, bundle id
`com.martingleason.ZenTomato.watchkitapp.Complication` — a child of the watch app's id, as
`ZenTomatoActivity`'s `com.martingleason.ZenTomato.Activity` is a child of the phone app's —
`TARGETED_DEVICE_FAMILY: "4"`, `SKIP_INSTALL: "YES"`, and a `dependencies:` entry with `embed: true`
**on `ZenTomatoWatch`, not on `ZenTomato`.** A watch widget extension is embedded by the watch app,
which is itself embedded by the phone app. Two levels of embedding, and the outer one is already the
one this project got wrong before.

**Its sources are three paths, not one**, and the third is the one that is easy to leave out:
`ZenTomatoComplication` (its own views), `ZenTomato/WatchLink/Payload` (the wire types), **and
`ZenTomato/Timer/BlockKind.swift`** — because `WatchBlockState.Block.kind` is a `BlockKind` and that
type lives in `ZenTomato/Timer/`, outside the payload directory. Both other secondary targets already
list that one file for the same reason and say so in `project.yml`: *"if the watch spelled them itself
the two could disagree, and a disagreement on a wrist is the hardest possible place to notice one."*
**One file, never the whole `Timer` directory** — `F17-M3` below is the fence on that. If the
complication draws the app glyph it also needs `ZenTomato/Resources/Assets.xcassets`, which is
`project.yml`'s recorded **third** silent failure: *"WITHOUT THIS THE WATCH APP INSTALLS WITH A BLANK
ICON and nothing says so."*

It also needs adding to the `ZenTomato` scheme's build list. `project.yml`'s existing comment says
why in its own words: a watchOS target *"is a different platform, and an iOS-destination build will
not reach for one on its own — which presents as a phone app that installs with no watch app inside
it, silently."* **This is a second, separate way to ship nothing**, independent of the embed, so it
gets its own mutation (`F17-M9`) rather than riding on `F17-M2`.

**Provisioning is asserted nowhere in this plan, and that is deliberate.** An earlier draft said the
bundle id being a child of the watch app's meant *"the existing wildcard profile covers it."* **I have
not verified that and it should not be believed.** `project.yml` sets `CODE_SIGN_STYLE: Automatic`
with `DEVELOPMENT_TEAM: $(DEVELOPMENT_TEAM)`; there is no profile in the tree to read, so the claim was
about the Apple developer account, which is outside this repository — and `conventions.md` is explicit
that such a claim is not ratifiable until something has run. **Provisioning is also precisely where
this project already shipped a silent failure:** `scripts/check-watch-provisioning.sh` exists because
on 2026-08-24 the watch app *"was built, embedded and signed correctly, installed with the phone app,
and never appeared on the wrist,"* and *"four separate checks of the bundle all looked healthy, because
the bundle WAS healthy."* So provisioning for the fourth bundle is an **unknown** until it has been
observed on hardware, and open question 5 is where the owner rules on it.

**And the existing script does not cover it as written.** `check-watch-provisioning.sh` inspects
`DerivedData/Build/Products/Debug-iphoneos/ZenTomato.app/Watch/ZenTomatoWatch.app` — the watch app's
own embedded profile, its platform list, and the paired watch's UDID. A profile nested one level
further in, inside the complication's bundle, is not a path it looks at. Extending it to walk the
extension is part of `F17-T2` and it is named here so it is a task rather than a discovery.

### A data path that does not exist, and it is worse than `D30` said

`D30` says a complication *"needs the block's kind and end time on the watch, in a place a widget
extension can read — an App Group, which nothing in this project uses."* That is right, and I
confirmed it: a search of the tree for an application-groups entitlement or a `group.` identifier
returns nothing outside prose. There is no shared container anywhere in this project today.

The part `D30` does not say is **how the state gets into that container in the first place.** The
existing path is `PhoneWatchLink.send(_:)` → `updateApplicationContext` →
`WatchLink.session(_:didReceiveApplicationContext:)`, and that last method is code **in the watch
app**, not in a widget extension. So the chain has to be: phone sends context → watch app receives it
→ watch app writes `WatchBlockState` into the App Group container → watch app calls
`WidgetCenter.shared.reloadAllTimelines()` → the extension reads the container and rebuilds its
timeline.

**Which puts the whole feature's freshness on a link in the chain nobody has measured: does the
system deliver an application context to a watch app that is not running, promptly enough for a face
to be right?** I could not verify this from the tree and did not run anything — this plan was written
without building. `conventions.md` is unambiguous about what that means: *a decision about what
another system can do is not ratifiable until something has run.* So `F17-T1` is a spike whose whole
job is to answer that on hardware, and the complication delta should not be ratified before it has.

**Two fallbacks exist if the answer is no**, named now so the spike has somewhere to land rather than
becoming a re-plan. One: the phone additionally uses `transferCurrentComplicationUserInfo`, whose
stated purpose is exactly this and which carries a daily budget — I have not verified its behaviour
or its budget on watchOS 26 and will not assert either. Two: the face falls back to what a
complication is normally allowed to be, which is *slightly stale and honest about it* — an entry that
counts down from the last instant it was told about, and says it has lost touch rather than inventing
a state. That second fallback is the same discipline `WatchTimerScreen` already applies: *"when it
reaches zero the watch says it has lost touch rather than deciding the block finished. Only the phone
ends a block."*

### The countdown is a timeline, and the right answer costs one entry

A complication is refreshed on WidgetKit's budget, not driven by the app. Drawing a countdown by
supplying a `TimelineEntry` per minute would burn the budget in an hour and be wrong the moment the
block changed.

The right answer is the one the watch app already uses: `Text(_:style: .timer)` against the absolute
`endsAt`. watchOS updates that label itself without redrawing the view — `WatchTimerScreen`'s comment
calls it *"the difference between a glanceable number and a battery complaint."* So the timeline is
**one entry per block**, valid until `endsAt`, plus one entry at `endsAt` for the ended state. Two
entries, not twenty-five.

This is also why `WatchBlockState.endsAt` being an absolute instant rather than a remaining duration —
already the load-bearing decision in that type, already tested by
`WatchLinkTests.theStateCarriesAnInstantNotADuration` — is what makes this feature affordable at all.
A duration would need a refresh a second and would be unbuildable on a wrist.

### Battery, answered as honestly as I can without hardware

**What this feature adds to the phone: nothing.** `PhoneWatchLink.send(_:)` is already called at every
block boundary and already guards on `isPaired` and `isWatchAppInstalled`. No new message, no new
cadence.

**What it adds to the watch:** one container write and one `reloadAllTimelines()` per block boundary —
a handful per sprint, matching what `WatchStatePublisher` already sends — plus whatever the system
spends drawing a `.timer` label it is already optimised to draw. The self-updating label is the
mechanism watchOS provides precisely so that a ticking complication is not a wakeup per second.

**What I will not claim:** a number. I have not measured it, this project's standard is that
assertions are not evidence, and *"negligible"* is an assertion. `F17-T6` is a real overnight
observation on the owner's own watch, and until it has run the honest answer is *expected to be
small, unmeasured.*

### Four families, drawn four ways

`D30` is right that *"works on an Ultra face"* is a per-family design question. Which families, and
why, is below.

---

## Which complication families, and what each one shows

**The four accessory families are `.accessoryCircular`, `.accessoryCorner`, `.accessoryRectangular`
and `.accessoryInline`**, as `D30` states. I am taking that from `D30` and from the shape of
`WidgetFamily`; **I have not compiled anything against the watchOS 26 SDK in writing this plan**, and
the exact family set and the exact slot mapping of the Ultra's own faces are the first thing
`F17-T1`'s spike confirms.

**The recommendation is to support all four, but to design two of them and let two be cheap.**

| Family | What it shows | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **`.accessoryCorner`** | A ring of remaining time, with the block kind as a short curved label | **The Ultra answer.** The faces the Ultra is bought for — Modular Ultra and Wayfinder — are built around corner slots, and a complication that skips this family is the one the owner asked for and did not get. Designed properly. |
| **`.accessoryCircular`** | A progress ring with the kind's glyph in the middle, or the countdown if it fits | The workhorse. It appears on nearly every face on every watch, so it is what makes this work on something other than an Ultra. Designed properly. |
| **`.accessoryRectangular`** | Kind, countdown, and **the attachment title** | The only slot with room for *what the block is for*, which is the thing this app is actually about. `WatchBlockState.Block.attachmentTitle` already resolves task-then-project on the phone, so the string exists and is already correct — the bug where a project-attached block reached the wrist blank was found and fixed, and `WatchLinkTests.aProjectAttachedBlockStillSaysWhatItIsFor` guards it. |
| **`.accessoryInline`** | One line: kind and remaining, e.g. *Focus · 18m* | Nearly free once the other three exist — it is a string, and the string is already computed. Refusing it would cost more argument than building it. |

**Rejected: shipping circular alone and calling it done.** It is the cheapest thing that technically
satisfies *"can be installed as a complication"*, and it fails the request. The owner named an Ultra,
and the Ultra's distinguishing faces are corner-shaped. A feature that is technically present in the
gallery and useless on the face the person actually wears has not been delivered.

**Rejected: designing all four with equal care.** The inline and rectangular slots have one
reasonable layout each. Time spent on them is time not spent on the corner ring, which is the one
with a real design question in it.

## When nothing is running, the face says nothing is running

**The rule: the idle entry shows the app's glyph and a dash, with no number and no count.**

Three things it deliberately does not do, each for a reason already ratified somewhere else:

**It does not show a stale countdown.** A complication that keeps displaying the last block's
remaining time is the single most damaging thing this feature could ship, because it is wrong in the
direction that looks right. `WatchTimerScreen` already refuses the equivalent: only the phone ends a
block, and the watch says it has lost touch rather than inventing an outcome.

**It does not show a tally.** No poms today, no streak, no chain. `WatchBlockState`'s own comment
gives the reason — *"a count on the wrist is also one step from a score, and `SPEC.md` excludes
gamification"* — and the v1.5 spec's `F16` ruling extends it: the incentive problem is the streak,
and a number on a watch face is the most glanceable possible place to put one. v1.5's second new hook
intention is *the garden cannot grow a streak*; a face that counts consecutive days would be the
first thing to violate it. `F17-T3` carries the fence test.

**It does not say "Start" unless the owner rules that it can start.** That is open question 3.

**One more state, and it is the one that will actually be seen most often at the wrong moment: the
block has ended and the alarm is sounding.** The entry at `endsAt` must render *ended*, not zero
counting upward, and — this is the trap — **it must not render *completed***. The reasoning is
already written down in `TimerEngineHolder` and in `DismissBlockIntent`: reconciliation from an end
time alone reads an abandoned block as finished, and *"a block the person deliberately abandoned four
minutes in is counted as a finished pomodoro."* A face is even further from the truth than
reconciliation is — it holds a payload the phone sent before the block ended and knows nothing about
what happened at the end of it. So it says the block's time is up and nothing more. `F17-M5` below is
the mutation for this.

### And what the face cannot do about the alarm, per `O31`

`O31` records, parked, that **watchOS draws its own alert UI and this app cannot change that label**:
the phone sets its button to *Done*, the watch app's own source has no Stop button at all, and the
word on the wrist is watchOS's. That is context this plan must not walk into.

**So: nothing in `F17` promises any control over what the watch shows when an alarm fires.** The
complication does not silence an alarm, does not carry a Dismiss button, and does not attempt to
match the system alert's wording. The face's only claim is about the block, and it makes that claim
in the app's own words in the app's own slot. Any surface that appears to offer alarm control from
the face would be promising something the platform does not give, and the owner would reasonably read
its absence as a defect.

## Starting a block from the face is `F14`'s door, and it must not grow a second one

**If the owner rules that the complication can start a pom, then it starts one by invoking exactly
the App Intent `F14` defines, and it defines no intent of its own.**

The argument, which is the same one `DismissBlockIntent` already makes and won: *"The rule lives in
one tested place, and a button that assumed the answer would be a second copy of it that could
drift."*

There are now three doors onto the same operation — `F14` from the watch app, `F10` from a Shortcut,
and `F17` from the face. **Three doors onto one room is fine. Three rooms is a defect**, and it is
the specific kind of defect this project keeps catching: two copies of a rule that agree on the desk
and disagree in the field. The room is `TimerEngine.start()`, reached the way `F14` reaches it.

**And the watch cannot reach it directly, which makes this stricter rather than looser.** The watch
has no engine — `WatchLinkTests.theWatchOwnsNoTimer` reads the watch target's sources and fails if
one appears, and `project.yml` states the rule as *"a watch that can see the store is a watch that
will one day write to it."* A widget extension on the watch is a third process, further out still.
So there is no local start to implement even if somebody wanted one: the tap must travel to the
phone, and building that transport is `F14`'s entire content. `F17` contributes zero start mechanism.
It contributes a button that calls one.

**Therefore `F17-T5` is conditional on `F14` having merged.** The v1.5 order already puts `F14` at
item 6 and `F17` at item 11, so this asks for nothing that was not already planned — it just says out
loud what happens if `F14` slips: `F17` ships `T1`–`T4` and `T6`, the face shows a block and opens
the app, and the start button arrives as a retrofit `F17b` when `F14` lands. That is a real,
shippable half, which is the point of decomposing this way at all.

### Foundational — no user-visible change

#### `F17-T1` — The data path spike · owner: **agent**

**Owner is `agent`, singly.** `conventions.md` gives a task one owner, `human` or `agent`, and an
earlier draft of this line named both — which reads as a shared owner and is exactly the ambiguity the
field exists to remove. The agent holds the pen for all of it. The hardware reading in the checkpoint
below is a **step the owner runs**, not a second owner: the same relationship every other agent-owned
task in this project has to its device check.

Prove, before any design work, that the chain in *A data path that does not exist* actually carries.
An App Group entitlement shared by `ZenTomatoWatch` and `ZenTomatoComplication`; the watch app writing
the decoded `WatchBlockState` into the container in `session(_:didReceiveApplicationContext:)`; the
extension reading it back.

It is first because **everything else in this feature is drawing, and drawing something that is
usually stale is worse than not shipping it.** If this link does not carry, the feature the owner gets
is materially different from the one described above, and that is a fact worth having before the
target exists rather than after four families are laid out.

**The question the spike must answer in the owner's terms, not in mine:** with the phone in another
room and the watch app not open, how out of date is the face? Seconds, a minute, or *only when you
open the app* — the last of which is a different feature and would need the owner to hear about it.

**No new wire type.** `WatchBlockState` already carries exactly the four facts a face needs, is
already `Codable`, and is already compiled into the watch target. Inventing a second payload for the
complication would be a second description of the same data — the failure `WatchLinkKeys`'s comment
describes, where *"a string typed twice is a string that eventually differs"* and the mismatch is
silent. The extension compiles `ZenTomato/WatchLink/Payload` for the same reason
`ZenTomatoActivity` compiles `ZenTomato/Shared`.

**Mutation `F17-M1`:** change the App Group identifier in one target's entitlement and not the other's,
which is the most likely real-world version of this breaking. `ComplicationBridgeTests
.theTwoTargetsNameTheSameContainer` must fail. This is a `project.yml`- and entitlement-reading test,
in the manner of `AlarmMetadataTests.sharedSourcesAreCompiledIntoTheWidget`, which already narrows to
one target's section of the file precisely so another target's identical line cannot satisfy it.

*Checkpoint:* on the owner's watch, with the app closed, the phone starts a block and a diagnostic
readout inside the watch app shows the container holding that block's `endsAt`, with the lag written
down. **This is the step only the owner can run**, and its result decides whether `T3` draws a live
countdown or an honestly-stale one.

#### `F17-T2` — The target, embedded, installed · owner: **agent**

Add `ZenTomatoComplication` to `project.yml` as described above, embed it in `ZenTomatoWatch`, add it
to the scheme, and ship **one deliberately plain placeholder complication** — the app glyph and the
word ZenPom, no countdown, no design.

**The placeholder is the point.** This project has three recorded instances of a target that compiled
and was never installed, each presenting as *"it simply does not appear."* An empty-looking
complication in the gallery is proof that the packaging is right, separated from every question about
whether the drawing is any good. Bundling the two means a blank corner slot has two possible causes
and no way to tell them apart.

**Mutation `F17-M2`, and it is the one this project has actually been bitten by:** delete the
`dependencies:` embed entry that puts this extension inside the watch app. The build stays green —
that is the whole problem — so a build cannot be the test. `ComplicationTargetTests
.theComplicationIsEmbeddedInTheWatchApp` reads `project.yml`, narrows to the `ZenTomatoWatch:`
section as `AlarmMetadataTests` narrows to `ZenTomatoActivity:`, and fails when the embed is absent.

**Mutation `F17-M3`:** add `ZenTomato/Timer/TimerEngine.swift` to the complication target's sources.
`ComplicationTargetTests.theFaceOwnsNoTimerAndNoStore` must fail, in the manner of
`WatchLinkTests.theWatchOwnsNoTimer` — the same rule, one process further out.

**Mutation `F17-M9`, and it is the second silent-install path, not a variant of `F17-M2`:** remove

## Standing rules — explicitly confirmed

Confirmed against this plan, item by item, because the confirmation is cheap and the omission is not:

- **No capture surface.** This feature accepts no task from the user, and the scope fence below
  refuses the one surface that would come closest.
- **Todoist writes: complete only.** `F17` makes no Todoist call whatsoever. The complication has no
  network access, no token, and no reason for either. `scripts/check-todoist-writes.sh` runs in `make
  ci` as usual and has nothing new to allow.
- **Todoist owns the hierarchy.** No new model. The face draws one already-resolved `String`,
  computed on the phone by `WatchStatePublisher.attachmentTitle()`, and holds no identifier.
- **Local only, no analytics, no network beyond Todoist and MusicKit.** A widget extension that
  reads a local container and draws a label. It adds no network path at all.
- **Secrets never enter the tree.** The new target uses `Config/App.xcconfig` like every other, and
  the App Group identifier is not a secret — it is a public bundle-scoped name, and it belongs in
  `project.yml` where it can be reviewed.
- **The agent never edits `SPEC.md`.** the complication delta is proposed above for the owner to ratify. No amendment
  is applied by this feature.
- **`D2` is untouched.** The phone runs the only engine. Nothing in `F17` puts a timer, a store or a
  decision on the wrist.

## Scope fence

Not in `F17`. Each needs its own gate.

- **Distraction capture on the face.** This is the one most likely to be asked for mid-build, and it
  is the one to refuse hardest — see below.
- **A tally, a streak, a chain, or the `F16` garden on the face.** Ruled out above and fenced by `F17-M6`.
- **Sprint progress dots.** `SprintDots` exists for the Live Activity. On a corner slot it is a score
  in a smaller font.
- **Any alarm control from the face**, including silence, dismiss, or matching the system alert's
  wording. `O31` says the platform does not give it. Promising it would be promising control this app
  does not have.
- **A Smart Stack or Lock Screen widget on the iPhone.** the complication delta as drafted admits a *watch-face
  complication*, singular. An iPhone widget is a different surface, a different target and a different
  argument.
- **Any change to the iOS Live Activity or to `ZenTomatoActivity`.** Different platform, different
  bundle, and it currently works.
- **A standalone or more independent watch app.** v2.0, and it contradicts `D2`.
- **Music controls anywhere on the face.** `SPEC.md`: the watch *"never controls music."*

### The one that will be asked for, and why it loses

**Putting Internal and External on the watch face.** It will come up, it will sound small — the
buttons exist on the wrist already, the payload already says whether a block accepts taps, and a
corner slot is *right there.* Four reasons it is a different feature:

**A face tap has no receipt.** `WatchLink.record(_:)` counts a tap *before* it sends it, and the
comment says exactly why: the send was measured at over two seconds on a real wrist, the button read
as dead, and the owner — reasonably — pressed it again, several times. Each press became a real row.
The fix was to make the count appear instantly, on screen, beside the button. **A complication cannot
show that receipt.** It is redrawn on a budget, not on a gesture, so the fix that made wrist capture
trustworthy is unavailable on the surface being proposed.

**A face tap has no confirmation and no undo.** The watch face is a surface you brush against.

**It would inflate the one number this app exists to produce**, in the direction that flatters, which
is the specific failure `WatchTap`'s identifier and `WatchTapInbox`'s duplicate check were both built
to prevent.

**And `D30` already ruled it**, in the half of its text this plan is keeping: *it starts nothing and
captures nothing.* the complication delta drops *starts nothing* because v1.5 overtook it. **It keeps *captures
nothing*, deliberately**, and that clause is what this fence rests on.

## Open questions for the gate

Batched. Each changes what gets built and none can be settled by investigation.

**1 — Ratify the complication delta superseding `D30`, or ratify `D30` as written?** They are different features. The
first section is the argument; the recommendation is the complication delta. **If neither is ratified, delete this
file.**

**2 — Does the complication start a pom, or only open the app?** This is where a rule and the evident
need disagree, and `conventions.md` says name the disagreement rather than resolve it silently. The
rule — `D30`'s proposed text — says *starts nothing*. The need — the ratified v1.5 earned-entry
sentence — says *seen and started*. **I would pick starting**, because the v1.5 spec is the later and
ratified document, because `F14` is building the transport regardless, and because a complication that
only opens an app is a shortcut with extra steps. But it is the owner's call and it is the reason this
feature is `M/L` rather than `M`.

**3 — If it does not start a pom, what does the idle face show?** A dash and the glyph, or the word
*Start* as a prompt to open the app? *Start* on a thing that does not start is the kind of small lie
that erodes trust in a surface.

**4 — Which faces, exactly?** The plan names **Modular Ultra** and **Wayfinder** as the Ultra's
distinguishing faces and designs `.accessoryCorner` first on that basis. If the owner actually wears
something else, the family priority changes and it is cheaper to know now than at `T3`.

**5 — Is a new App Group entitlement acceptable?** It touches the Apple developer account and the
provisioning profile — `C2`'s territory, and the owner's. There is no way to build this feature
without a shared container, so a *no* here is a *no* to the feature.

**6 — Does `F17` wait for `F14`?** Recommended yes, per the order already ratified. Confirming it at
the gate means `T5` is a planned dependency rather than a mid-build surprise.

**7 — If `F17-T1`'s spike finds the face can only be fresh while the watch app has recently run, is
the stale-but-honest version still worth building?** I think yes — a face that is right when you have
been using the watch, and honest when it has not, is still better than opening an app. But that is a
materially smaller feature than the one the owner asked for, and hearing it described that way before
four families are drawn is worth a round trip.
