### The theme tables — the handoff's values, measured

Measured with the WCAG formula the suite uses (`ContrastRatio`), over every audited pairing in both
appearances. **Ten of twelve theme × appearance cells pass as handed off. Two miss by a hair:**

| Theme | Cell | Pairing | Handoff | Adjusted to | Now |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ripen | dark | `action` on `surfaceRaised` | `#E06A50` → **4.49:1** | `#E67D65` (existing `red400`) | 5.29:1 |
| Matcha | light | `textSubtle` on `surfacePrimary` | `#6B7261` → **4.46:1** | `#676D5C` | 4.78:1 |

**The accent themes' other steps are derived, and the rule is checked against Sage.** The handoff
states only `action` for Ripen, Teal, Plum and Ink. `actionHover`, `actionActive` and `actionSubtle`
are derived by one rule — hover and active mix the accent 17% and 31% toward black in light (12% and
24% toward white in dark); subtle mixes it 86% toward white in light (80% toward the dark page in
dark). **Applied to Sage, that rule reproduces the shipped steps within one unit per channel**
(`#4C5D35` vs `sage700 #4C5C36`, `#3F4D2C` vs `sage800 #3F4C2D`), so it is the existing scale's own
spacing, not a taste call. Lowest derived pairing across all four: **6.63:1**. `focus` follows `action`
in every theme, as it does today.

**Where the values live.** `Palette` is a closed transcription of the Civic Data ramps (*"Adding a step
is a design-system change, not a patch"*), and these values are not from that source. They go in a new
primitives file beside it, **`ThemePalette.swift`**, on the precedent of `TodoistPalette.swift`. The
`palette_outside_token_layer` lint rule is extended to the new name so no screen can reach it.

### The tomato, per theme

The fruit is the theme's accent **as drawn on dark** — the Island is always dark-resolved — so each
theme has a real tomato: red (Ripen), green (Sage, Matcha — a green-when-ripe variety), purple (Plum),
near-black graphite (Ink), and teal (Teal, the one with no real-world match, stated rather than
dressed up). **One collision was measured and resolved:** under Sage and Matcha the flesh *is*
`sage400`, the same as the leaf — **1.00:1, the crown vanishes.** Green tomatoes take the handoff's own
garden crown, `sage700 #4C5C36` (2.55:1 against the flesh). Every other theme keeps the `sage400` crown
it has today. The skin stays `stone500`. **Taste is the owner's, at `T6`.**

### Auto

The default, top of the list. **Hour < 6 or ≥ 21 → Ink; August or September → Ripen; otherwise Sage**
— the handoff's rule verbatim. The old body fenced out *"automatic theming — by time of day"*; the
handoff, under `D57`, specifies it, and the owner's instruction to build the picker is read as taking
the handoff's picker, Auto included. **Flagged, not buried:** it is a behaviour, not a palette, and it
is the one part of this feature the old plan argued against. It is a pure function of the date, tested
at its boundaries, and recomputed on each hour by a `TimelineView` at the root — so foregrounding the
app after 9pm repaints it without any timer of the app's own.

**What `nil` means for an existing install: Auto.** The `D24` precedent says the only migration worth
trusting changes no behaviour, and this one does — an install that today draws Sage will draw Ink after
9pm. It is chosen anyway because Auto is the handoff's default and the only install is the owner's; the
alternative (`nil` → Sage) is one line if the owner prefers it.

### The mechanism changes: a bridged trait, not a torn-down tree

The body's `F12-T4` chose *"key the cache by theme and `.id(theme)` the root"*, and called it an
unverified claim. **A better mechanism was found and run first.** iOS 17 lets a SwiftUI environment
value bridge to a custom UIKit trait (`UITraitBridgedEnvironmentKey`), and the `UIColor` rule that
`Color(building:)` already hands iOS receives that trait when it resolves. So the theme joins the
*rule* the cache already stores — beside light/dark — and every one of **234 `Color(.role)` call sites
in 29 files** follows the theme with **no call site touched and no tree torn down.**

Spike, run 2026-09-30 before anything was built on it (a throwaway test, deleted):

```
SPIKE before=#0000FFFF after=#FF0000FF
✔ Test bridgedTraitReachesColorResolution() passed after 0.002 seconds.
** TEST SUCCEEDED **
```

A colour whose rule read `traits[SpikeThemeTrait] == 1 ? red : blue`, resolved through
`Color.resolve(in:)` with the environment value unset and then set. **What that does not prove:** that
a widget extension's renderer carries the trait the same way. It is the same SwiftUI resolution path,
and `T5`'s device check is where it is seen.

## Paraphrasing it back

Today the app has exactly one look: warm stone grounds, slate ink, sage as the one chromatic thing on
the screen, with a light and a dark version of each. Every screen asks for that look by **role** —
"the page", "the ink on an action button" — and no screen anywhere names a colour. F12 lets the owner
choose a *different set of colours behind those same roles*, and the screens do not change at all.

**The mechanism is nearly free, and this plan is not about the mechanism.** `ColorRole` resolves
every role through a single `private var pair` — one `switch` over all twenty cases returning a
`(light:, dark:)` tuple of `Palette` steps. A theme is that switch, written a second time with
different steps on the right-hand side. No view changes. No new role is added. The
`palette_outside_token_layer` rule in `.swiftlint.yml` already fails the build if any screen names a
`Palette.` step directly, and that rule is what makes the swap safe rather than hopeful: there is
nowhere else a colour could be hiding.

**The work is proving each theme is still accessible.** Every colour in the current system carries a
measured contrast figure in its own doc comment, and those figures are the reason each shade is the
exact shade it is — `ColorRole.action` sits one step darker than the brand sage purely because the
brand hex measures 4.33:1 against the page and text needs 4.5:1. A second theme is a second complete
set of those claims. If the audit does not run over *every theme × every audited pairing × both
appearances*, then a theme ships unmeasured while the suite reports green, and the app has quietly
acquired an unreadable look that only the owner's eyes will catch.

**One honest correction to the spec's own words.** `zenpom-v1.5.md` says the design system has "42
semantic colour roles". It has **twenty roles**, each defined in two appearances — forty values.
`PolishFenceTests.theTokenLayerDoesNotGrow` pins the count at 20 and I read it there. The figure does
not change the argument (the seam is real and the swap is cheap), but a plan that repeated a number it
had not checked would be exactly the failure this project keeps writing down.

## What this feature is built on, verified rather than assumed

Four facts were read out of the tree, and each one changes the shape of the plan.

**1. The token layer is genuinely two layers, and the fence has teeth.** `Palette` is `internal`
because Swift's `private` does not cross files, and the rule is carried by
`custom_rules.palette_outside_token_layer` in `.swiftlint.yml`, which greps `\bPalette\.` across
`App|Views|Models|Timer|Alarm|Shared|Stats|Export|Sprint|Todoist|Plan|Music`, `ZenTomatoActivity` and
`ZenTomatoWatch`. So the claim "views never name a colour" is enforced on every commit, not merely
believed. **F12 must not widen that rule**, and nothing in this plan needs it widened.

**2. The colours are cached once per process.** `ColorRoleTable.colors` in
`Color+ColorRole.swift` is a `static let` dictionary built on first use, holding one prepared `Color`
per role. What it caches is the *rule* ("dark or light, pick accordingly"), which is why light and
dark still follow the phone live. **A theme is not part of that rule today**, so a naive swap would
repaint nothing until the app was killed and relaunched — the cache would keep handing back the old
theme's colours forever. This is the single most likely way F12 ships looking correct in tests and
broken on the phone, and `F12-T4` exists for it.

**3. The launch screen cannot be themed, and a test already says so.** The blank frame iOS draws
before the app's first frame takes its colour from `LaunchBackground.colorset` in the asset catalog —
data, not code. `LaunchBackgroundTests` decodes that JSON and asserts it equals
`ColorRole.surfacePrimary` in both appearances. An asset catalog cannot read a SwiftData row, so under
a non-default theme **the launch flash will be the default theme's page colour**. That is a real
visible cost and it belongs in the plan rather than in a review.

**4. The Live Activity is a different process and cannot see the database.** `project.yml` compiles
`ZenTomato/DesignSystem` into the `ZenTomatoActivity` target as well as the app, and the comment
beside it states the rule plainly: nothing that touches the database may go in those shared
directories. Everything the Lock Screen shows travels inside the alarm's own payload
(`ZenTomato/Shared/FocusAlarmMetadata.swift`, **three** fields — `kind`, `completedInSprint`,
`pomodorosPerSprint`; the draft said five and was corrected at review by counting them). So the Lock Screen **cannot** learn the
chosen theme unless that payload gains a field — a change to a wire type shared by two processes,
which `project.yml` also documents as one of the silent ways this feature can look broken. This plan
fences it out and asks the owner (Question 3).

**The watch is unaffected**, verified: `ZenTomatoWatch` does not compile `DesignSystem` at all and its
screens use the system's own `.secondary` styling. A theme changes nothing on the wrist.

## The design, and the alternative that lost

### A theme is a second primitive table behind the same roles

`ColorRole.pair` becomes a lookup **on a theme** rather than a property of the role:
`Theme.default.pair(for: .action)`, with `role.light` / `role.dark` keeping their current spelling and
resolving through whichever theme is current. Every theme is required to answer for all twenty roles
in both appearances, and the same `switch`-over-every-case trick keeps that compiler-enforced: a theme
that forgets a role does not compile, exactly as a role that forgets dark mode does not compile today.

**No new `ColorRole` case.** `PolishFenceTests.theTokenLayerDoesNotGrow` fixes the count at 20 and F12
keeps it there. That fence was written to catch a theme system arriving "one reasonable-looking role
at a time", and it is an asset here rather than an obstacle: if a candidate theme needs a role that
does not exist, it has found a gap in the design system and that is a conversation, not a patch.

**A theme may add primitive steps but may not move an existing one.** Each theme brings its own ramp
values; `Palette`'s existing steps are the default theme's, and the default theme must resolve
**byte-for-byte** to what ships today. That is the acceptance condition on `F12-T1` and the reason the
whole of `DesignTokenTests` can stay green through the foundational work.

### The alternative that lost: a tint colour

The cheap version of this feature is one accent colour the owner picks, applied to `action`,
`actionHover`, `actionActive` and `focus`. It is a tenth of the work and it is refused, at a price
worth stating: **an arbitrary accent cannot be audited.** The action colour is one step darker than
the brand sage *because* the brand hex measures 4.33:1 against the page. A colour wheel hands the app
a value nobody has measured, against three grounds, in two appearances, for `onAction` as well as for
`action` — and the app would then be shipping a look it cannot make an accessibility claim about. A
fixed set of themes, each measured by the same helper that measures today's, keeps every claim in the
tree true. This is also the thing most likely to be asked for mid-build, so it is in the scope fence
below as well.

### The second alternative that lost: dark mode as a theme in the list

Presenting "Light / Dark / Sepia / …" in one picker collapses two axes that are currently orthogonal,
and it would break the mechanism that makes dark mode reliable: `Color(building:)` hands iOS a
`UIColor` rule that resolves at draw time, so the app follows the phone live and has **no appearance
branch anywhere outside that one initialiser**. A "Dark" entry in a theme list would need the app to
override the trait, and the compiler guarantee that every role is defined in both appearances would
stop meaning anything. **Every theme defines both appearances; the phone still decides which one is
drawn.** The owner may disagree — it is Question 2.

### Foundational — no user-visible change

#### `F12-T1` — The theme seam · owner: **agent**

Introduce a `Theme` (a `String`-backed, `CaseIterable`, `Sendable` enum, mirroring how `AlertSound`
is shaped) and move `ColorRole`'s twenty-case `switch` behind it. The default case returns exactly
what the switch returns today. Second and third themes bring their own primitive steps, added to
`Palette` as new named families — never by editing an existing step, because an edited step changes
the default theme and `DesignTokenTests.everyRoleMatchesTheDesignSystem` compares against an
independently transcribed hex table for a reason.

It is first because it is the whole of the mechanism, and because it can be landed with **zero**
observable change: the app still draws the default theme, no setting exists yet, and the existing
suite is the acceptance test.

**Naming, and a fence that has to be retargeted rather than deleted.**
`PolishFenceTests.nothingFromTheParkedList` forbids the literal `\bThemeProvider` across all shipped
Swift, as one of ten terms standing for work parked for later. Themes are no longer parked, so that
term is now guarding a decision the owner has reversed — but *modular* themes (a theme loaded from a
file, a URL, or anything the user authors) remain out of scope for ever as far as this feature is
concerned. So the term is **replaced, not removed**: the fence becomes "no theme is constructed from
data" — no theme decoded from JSON, read from a file, or built from a `UIColor`/hex supplied at
runtime. A fence that is deleted because it fired is not a fence.

**Mutation, run before this task is called done:** change one primitive step behind the **default**
theme — the page, `stone50` — and confirm `everyRoleMatchesTheDesignSystem` fails naming
`surfacePrimary`, and `LaunchBackgroundTests` fails as well. That is the proof the default theme is
still pinned to the published table and did not become "whatever the new code returns".

*Checkpoint:* `make ci` green with the theme type present, the default theme selected everywhere, and
`git diff` showing not one line changed under `ZenTomato/Views`, `ZenTomatoActivity` or
`ZenTomatoWatch`.

#### `F12-T2` — The audit, over every theme · owner: **agent**

**This is the task the feature actually is.** `DesignTokenTests` today measures **seventeen** text
pairings and three border pairings, each in both appearances, via `expectContrast(_:)` looping over
`Appearance.allCases`. (The draft said eighteen. `textPairings` has seventeen entries, counted at
review — the same class of unchecked number this plan corrects the spec for two paragraphs above.)
Those loops are correct for one theme and silently wrong for several: a pairing measured once,
against one theme, is not measured.

**And the helper is where the tautology would hide.** `Appearance.color(of:)` reads `role.light` /
`role.dark` directly, so if roles resolve through a *current* theme the cross-product would loop
over theme names while measuring the same colours every time — green, and worth nothing. So
`Appearance.color(of:)` and `expectContrast(_:)` must take the theme **as a parameter**, not read an
ambient one. That signature change reaches outside this file: `SprintProgressTests` calls
`DesignTokenTests.expectContrast` at two call sites (lines 39 and 42) and moves with it.

The change is that a pairing is measured over the **cross-product** — `Theme.allCases ×
(textPairings + borderPairings) × Appearance.allCases` — with the failure message naming the theme,
the pairing, the appearance, the measurement and the threshold. `ContrastRatio` needs no change at all;
it already works on plain `RGBColor` with no reference to iOS, which is exactly what makes this
testable without a screen.

**The exemptions are per theme, and this is the subtle half.** `ColorRole`'s documentation records two
deliberate exemptions: anything on `surfaceInset` in light (a disabled control, exempt under WCAG
1.4.3 — `textSubtle` measures 4.13:1 there and the coloured inks 4.28–4.47:1), and `border`, which is
decorative at 1.09–1.66:1. Those are **measured facts about the current palette**, not permanent
properties of the design. Carried across as a blanket exemption they would hide a genuine failure in a
second theme. So each theme declares its own exemption list, each entry carries the measured figure
and the written reason, and a theme that needs a *new* exemption is a theme the owner has to look at.

`everyForegroundRoleIsAudited` gains the same treatment: it must hold for every theme, so a theme
cannot introduce an ink that nothing measures.

**Mutation — the one this whole plan is designed around, and it must be run on a NON-default theme.**
Set the second theme's `action` (light) to the brand sage `0x667A49`, which measures 4.33:1 against
`stone50` and is documented in `Palette.sage500` as failing for exactly this reason. The named test
that must fail is **`textOnSurfaceMeetsAA`**, and its failure message must name the second theme by
name. If the suite stays green, the assertion is looping over one theme and the audit is a tautology
with a green tick — which is the scar this project already carries in writing.

**A second mutation, because one is not enough here:** delete one theme from the cross-product's
argument list (leaving the assertion looking identical) and confirm the count of executed test cases
drops. A suite that shrinks silently is blind, not clean, and the harness contract in
`docs/conventions.md` requires the count to be reported.

*Checkpoint:* the test count rises by a factor equal to the number of themes, the figure is in the PR,
and the brand-sage mutation is recorded as an `M<n>` with the run in which it was seen to fail.

### Vertical slices — each one a thing you can hold

#### `F12-T3` — The stored choice, and its migration · owner: **agent**

`AppSettings` gains an eighth stored property, `themeRawValue: String?`, following the `D24`
precedent in the same file line for line: **optional**, because every row already on a phone has no
value for it and SwiftData must fill it with `nil` rather than refuse to open the store; and stored as
a **`String` rather than the enum**, so a value written by a later version — a theme this build has
never heard of — reads back as the default instead of failing to decode. `AlertSound.stored(_:)` is
the existing softening function and `Theme.stored(_:)` mirrors it.

**Two fences move, and both moves are the fences working.**
`PolishFenceTests.noNewStoredShape` asserts `Schema([AppSettings.self]).entities.first.properties.count
== 7`. It becomes 8, in this PR, as a visible line in a diff the owner reads — which is precisely what
that comment says the bound is for ("this is the exact change it was watching for, arriving as a
ratified amendment rather than as a quiet extra field"). The `@Model` count stays at **12**: a theme is
a column, not a new stored type.

**The file's own header has to move with it, and this was missed in the draft.**
`AppSettings.swift` opens with `EXACTLY SIX PROPERTIES, AND WHY THAT IS A RULE RATHER THAN A
COINCIDENCE` and, a few lines down, *"There is no `theme` or `appearance`. Light and dark follow the
system setting, with no control anywhere in the app. Themes are explicitly out of scope for v0.1."*
Adding the column and leaving that paragraph is how a file ends up arguing against its own contents.
It is rewritten in this task the way the seventh field's arrival rewrote it — naming the ratified
delta, keeping the *"Nothing else"* discipline, and keeping the two remaining absences (`musicEnabled`
and any per-appearance override) stated as the deliberate refusals they still are.

**The migration is verified, not assumed.** The register records `O2` closed positively — a build
installed over a running app with history intact — so the mechanism is known to work; but "known to
work once" is not evidence for this change. It is checked two ways: a test that opens a store
containing an `AppSettings` row written **without** the new column and asserts it reads back with the
default theme and every other field untouched; and the device half in `F12-T5`, which installs over
the owner's real install and confirms the distraction log is still there. The order matters — the log
is the reason the app exists, and a migration is the one change in this feature that can lose it.

**Mutation:** make the reader ignore the stored value and always return the default. The named test
that must fail is **`settingsRoundTrip`**, extended to cover the theme column. A second mutation:
make the property non-optional with a default value, and confirm the legacy-store test fails to open
the store rather than passing quietly.

*Checkpoint:* a store written by the shipped build opens under the new build, reports the default
theme, and every other setting and every distraction row is unchanged.

#### `F12-T4` — Choose a theme, and the app repaints · owner: **agent**

A picker in `SettingsView`, in its own section, listing the themes by name. Choosing one writes the
column and **the whole app changes colour without a relaunch.**

**That last clause is the work.** `ColorRoleTable.colors` is a `static let` built once per process, so
without deliberate handling the app would keep drawing the previous theme until it was killed. Two
options were weighed:

- **Make `ColorRole` a `ShapeStyle` that resolves from the SwiftUI environment.** The correct-by-the-
  book answer, and refused on churn: every call site in the app spells `Color(.action)`, and
  converting them all touches dozens of view files while other agents are working in this same
  checkout. A large mechanical diff across every screen is also the worst possible thing to hand a
  reviewer who does not read Swift.
- **Key the cache by theme, and rebuild the tree when the theme changes** — the dictionary becomes
  `[Theme: [ColorRole: Color]]`, still built lazily and still caching the *rule* rather than the
  answer, and the composition root carries `.id(theme)` so SwiftUI discards and rebuilds the view tree
  on a change. Blunt, one place, no call site touched. **That last step is an unverified claim about
  SwiftUI until `F12-T4` runs it on hardware** — nothing in this repository exercises identity-driven
  teardown today, and if the tree does not repaint, the `ShapeStyle` option above is the fallback and
  it is a larger change than this task is scoped for, which is a thing to find out on day one of the
  task rather than at its checkpoint. **Chosen**, with its cost named: a theme change
  rebuilds every screen, which is acceptable because it happens when somebody taps a row in Settings
  and never while a block is running.

**A fence sits on this screen, and the draft did not name it.** `SettingsLockTests` reads
`SettingsView.swift` as text: `theCustomizationRowsLockTogether` slices **from the first
`Group {`** to `.disabled(isBlockRunning)`, requires `blockLengths`, `sprint` and `whenABlockEnds`
inside that slice, and asserts `.disabled(isBlockRunning)` occurs **exactly once**;
`musicAndTodoistAreNotLocked` asserts the slice contains neither `music` nor `todoist`. Two
consequences for `F12-T4`, both mechanical:

- The theme section must not introduce a `Group {` **before** the customization group, or the slice
  starts in the wrong place and the fence fails on code that is correct.
- The section must sit outside that group, and the word `theme` must not appear inside the slice.

*Shown to fail, not assumed:* wrap the theme section in its own `Group { … }
.disabled(isBlockRunning)` and confirm `theCustomizationRowsLockTogether` fails on the
occurrence count — the evidence that the fence still measures this screen after a section is added
to it.

**The theme picker stays live while a block is running, and that is a deliberate reading of `D27`.**
`D27` makes settings read-only mid-block, and `docs/plans/00-deltas.md` records its scope explicitly:
*"`D27` covers the customization row only."* The reason behind it is that a running block must not
have its arithmetic changed underneath it. A colour cannot change how long a block is. If the owner
wants the picker greyed out mid-block for consistency, **it is not the one-line change the draft
claimed**: `theCustomizationRowsLockTogether` allows exactly one `.disabled(isBlockRunning)` in the
file, so locking the theme row means either moving it inside the customization group — which makes
that group's name a lie, since a colour is not a snapshotted timer setting — or amending the fence,
which is a decision about what `D27` covers and therefore the owner's. Better asked for now than
discovered at review.

*Checkpoint:* on the device, with a block **not** running, switch theme in Settings and watch the
timer screen, the stats screen and the picker all repaint immediately; background the app, return, and
confirm the choice held; kill and relaunch, and confirm it held again.

#### `F12-T5` — Each theme on a real screen · owner: **human**

`SPEC.md`'s standard is that a *Done when* is closed on hardware, by the owner, with a build number
and their own words. Four things only the owner can answer:

1. **Does each theme look like something you chose**, in light and in dark, on a real screen in real
   light? The audit proves legibility. It cannot prove taste, and taste is the entire earned-entry
   argument for this feature.
2. **The upgrade.** Install this build over the running one and confirm the distraction log, the
   stats and the settings all survived.
3. **The launch flash.** Under a non-default theme, does the default-coloured launch frame read as a
   bug? If it does, that is a `D<n>` about shipping a per-theme launch asset, not a fix inside F12.
4. **The Lock Screen.** The Live Activity keeps the default look (see the fence). Does the mismatch
   between a themed app and an unthemed Lock Screen matter enough to spend the wire-format change?

*Checkpoint:* recorded as an `O<n>`, closed by the owner with the build number and their own sentence.

## Proposed spec delta

**The theme delta — the app has a theme, chosen from a fixed audited set.** Proposed, not ratified.
The agent never edits `docs/specs/SPEC.md`; this is the text for the owner to ratify and apply.

**The number is deliberately not written here, and that is a correction made at review.** The draft
of this plan called it *the next free integer after `D31`*. Two things are wrong with that, both
checked in the tree:

- **`DeltaIntegrityTests.everyCitedDeltaIsDefined` walks every `.md` under `docs/` except
  `00-deltas.md` and the vendored `conventions.md`, and fails on any `\bD[0-9]{1,3}[a-z]?\b` token
  with no heading in `00-deltas.md`.** `D31` is the highest heading there. So committing a plan that
  cites an unallocated number turns `make ci` red before the owner has read a word of it.
- **The number is contested.** `docs/plans/F16.md` and `docs/plans/F17.md`, drafted in the same batch
  and also untracked, each propose *their own* delta under that same next-free integer, and
  `docs/plans/F13.md` names it as the number it would take if the owner says to number it. Whichever
  gate is crossed first takes it; the rest move. An ID is allocated when the decision is written into
  `00-deltas.md`, not when a plan guesses at the next free integer.

So this plan refers to it as **the theme delta** throughout and the owner allocates the number at
ratification. When it is written into `docs/plans/00-deltas.md` it goes **into that file's index
table** as well —
`DeltaIntegrityTests` asserts every delta appears there — and the amendment reaches `SPEC.md` in the
same change, because `docs/specs/AMENDMENT-BASELINE.txt` is **0** and the test fails the moment the
real count exceeds it. Quote the current text exactly: that file records that `D18` was never counted
because it paraphrased a full stop as a semicolon.

**What the spec currently says — line 58, the out-of-scope list:**

> standalone watchOS · macOS · CloudKit sync · creating a default "focus playlist" · creating,
> editing, or commenting on Todoist tasks · any capture surface of any kind · widgets beyond the Lock
> Screen Live Activity · themes · streaks, badges, or any gamification layered on top of Todoist's
> own.

**Proposed replacement for line 58:**

> standalone watchOS · macOS · CloudKit sync · creating a default "focus playlist" · creating,
> editing, or commenting on Todoist tasks · any capture surface of any kind · widgets beyond the Lock
> Screen Live Activity · user-authored or downloadable themes, and any colour the user picks by hand ·
> streaks, badges, or any gamification layered on top of Todoist's own.

**And a new row in the *Locked decisions* table, after `Timer customization` (currently line 30):**

> | Theme | A fixed set of built-in themes, chosen in Settings, stored as the eighth `AppSettings`
> field. Each theme defines every semantic colour role in **both** light and dark, and light/dark
> continues to follow the system setting — a theme is not an appearance. No theme is constructed from
> data at runtime, and no colour is chosen by hand. Every theme is held to the same contrast floors as
> the default: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for a control's boundary, with each exemption declared per theme and
> measured. Nothing else. |

**Why the out-of-scope entry narrows rather than disappears.** "Themes" was refused in v0.1 as
open-ended surface, and the open-ended part is still refused: a colour picker, a theme file, anything
the user authors. What is admitted is a closed, audited list. That distinction is the whole of the
delta, and it is what keeps `nothingFromTheParkedList`'s guard meaningful after `\bThemeProvider` is
retargeted in `F12-T1`.

**Why the locked-decisions row is separate from `Timer customization` rather than appended to it.**
`D24` moved that row by exactly one to admit the alert sound, keeping "Nothing else." on the end,
because an alert sound genuinely is a property of the timer. A theme is not: it changes nothing the
timer does. Folding it into that row would make the sentence untrue in a way that costs nothing today
and misleads later.

**What ratifying the other way would cost, recorded so the option shows its price.** Refusing the
delta refuses `F12` outright; the seam stays unused and the feature leaves the v1.5 list. That is a
legitimate outcome — the feature was admitted "partly on cost", and the cost estimate in
`zenpom-v1.5.md` cited a role count that turned out to be wrong. The mechanism is still cheap; `F12-T2`
is not, and the owner is entitled to spend the review slot on `F13` instead.

**A vocabulary addition rides with it.** `definitions.md` has no entry for *theme*, and this plan uses
the word in a specific sense. Proposed row: **Theme — a complete set of colours behind the roles the
app already names. Every theme defines both appearances; light and dark remain the system's choice,
not the theme's.** A change to `definitions.md` is itself a `D<n>` by that file's own rule, so it is
part of the theme delta rather than a tidy-up.

**A second baseline correction rides with it, and it was not declared in the draft.**
`docs/specs/zenpom-v1.5.md` is **ratified as a baseline** in its own header — *"From here it is not
edited to match reality"* — and line 112 says the design system has "42 semantic colour roles". It
has twenty (`theTokenLayerDoesNotGrow` pins the figure). Correcting a baseline is a `D<n>` like any
other, so the correction is **owed** and is not a typo fix the agent may make. Whether it rides
inside the theme delta or takes its own number is the owner's call at the gate; what is not optional
is that the plan declares it, because the v1.5 spec's cost estimate for `F12` rests on that figure.

## Standing rules — explicitly confirmed

Each is stated because the plan is held to it, not as a formality.

- **No capture surface.** F12 adds one picker over a fixed list of built-in themes. There is no text
  field, no name to type, no colour to enter. Nothing in this feature accepts input that becomes
  content. `StatsFenceTests.noCaptureSurfaceInThisFeature` and `NoCaptureSurfaceTests` are the
  standing guards and neither needs relaxing.
- **Todoist writes: complete-task only.** F12 makes no network call of any kind and touches no
  Todoist code path. `scripts/check-todoist-writes.sh` runs unchanged in `make ci`.
- **Todoist owns the hierarchy.** No model of any kind is added. `F12-T3` adds one column to an
  existing `@Model`; the `@Model` count stays at 12.
- **Local only, no analytics, no network beyond Todoist and MusicKit.** A theme is a table of hex
  values compiled into the binary. Nothing is fetched, and `F12-T1`'s retargeted fence forbids a theme
  built from data at runtime, which is the only door through which a downloaded theme could arrive.
- **Secrets never enter the tree.** F12 adds no configuration and reads no build setting.
- **The agent never edits `docs/specs/SPEC.md`.** The delta above is proposed text. This plan writes
  one file, `docs/plans/F12.md`.

## Scope fence

Not in F12. Each needs its own gate.

- **A colour the owner picks by hand**, an accent picker, or a hex field. **This is the one most
  likely to be asked for mid-build**, because once the seam exists it looks like a five-minute change.
  It is out because it destroys the only thing this feature is actually spending its effort on: an
  arbitrary colour has no measured contrast against three grounds in two appearances, so the app could
  no longer make the accessibility claim that every comment in `ColorRole.swift` currently makes. The
  delta above refuses it in the spec text, so admitting it later is a `D<n>` that has to answer this
  paragraph.
- **Theming the Live Activity.** It is a separate process that cannot read the database; the theme
  would have to travel in `FocusAlarmMetadata`, a wire type shared by two targets, with in-flight
  alarms scheduled under the old shape. `project.yml` documents that mismatch as one of the three
  silent ways the Lock Screen breaks. Question 3 puts it to the owner as its own decision.
- **A per-theme launch screen.** The asset catalog is data and cannot read a setting; a per-theme
  launch colour needs a different mechanism entirely.
- **Theming the watch.** `ZenTomatoWatch` does not compile the design system and uses system styling.
  Bringing it in is a change to what that target may see, and `WatchLinkTests` guards that boundary.
- **New `ColorRole` cases, or theming typography, spacing and radius.** `theTokenLayerDoesNotGrow`
  pins all four counts. A theme that needs a new role has found a gap in the design system.
- **A theme that changes anything other than colour** — a different type scale, rounder corners.
  `radiusScaleStaysSharp` exists because a well-meaning rounding-up is the exact thing that erases a
  deliberate choice.
- **Automatic theming** — by time of day, by block kind, by Focus mode. Each is a behaviour, not a
  palette.
- **`F15`, the graphics pass.** F12 changes colour values behind existing roles and nothing else. If
  a theme makes a layout look wrong, that is F15's problem and F15's gate.

## Open questions for the gate · owner: **human**

Batched. Nothing below `F12-T1` starts until they are answered, and each is recorded as a `D<n>` or
inside the theme delta.

1. **How many themes, and which?** The plan's arithmetic works for any number, but the number decides
   the size of `F12-T2` and of the owner's own review. My proposal: **three** — today's Stone/Sage as
   the default, plus two — because two is enough to prove the mechanism and to prove the audit loop is
   really looping, while five would multiply the review without multiplying the learning. What the two
   others should *be* is a taste question I should not answer for you.

2. **Is dark mode one of the themes?** I have argued above that it should not be — every theme defines
   both appearances and the phone still chooses which is drawn, which preserves the compiler guarantee
   that no role can lose dark mode and keeps the app free of appearance branches. But "Light / Dark /
   Sepia" in one list is what most apps show, and if that is the shape you want, say so now: it
   changes `F12-T1` fundamentally rather than at the edges, and it is much more expensive discovered
   at the review.

3. **Does the theme reach the Lock Screen?** Cost: a new field in `FocusAlarmMetadata`, a wire type
   compiled into two processes, with alarms already scheduled under the old shape when the app
   updates. Benefit: the Lock Screen matches the app. My recommendation is **not in F12** — ship the
   themes, look at the mismatch on a real Lock Screen in `F12-T5`, and decide with the evidence in
   hand.

4. **If a candidate theme cannot clear the floors, is it adjusted or refused?** My proposal:
   **adjusted once, then refused.** A theme whose colours have to be nudged repeatedly until they pass
   is a theme being fitted to the test rather than to the eye, and shipping one under a fresh exemption
   would turn the exemption list from a record of two documented decisions into a place where failures
   go to be forgiven.

5. **Does the theme picker live in Settings, or wait for `F15`?** Settings is where it belongs
   mechanically and it is where this plan puts it. If the graphics pass is going to restructure that
   screen, putting the row in now costs a small rework — worth knowing before, not after.

6. **A rule and the need disagree, and I am telling you rather than resolving it.** The learning dial
   is at the floor and the agent authors everything, so nothing here needs your hands on the code. But
   this is the one feature in v1.5 whose success criterion is **taste**, and taste cannot be delegated
   to the author or to a contrast ratio. If you would rather choose the actual palettes yourself — hex
   values, or even "make it look like this app" — say so at this gate and `F12-T1` takes them as input.
   Following the dial silently would mean I choose how your app looks and you find out at the review,
   which is the expensive place to disagree.
