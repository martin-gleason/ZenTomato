# F8 — Fit a sprint to the time you actually have

**Status:** plan written, **awaiting the gate.** No code has been written.
**Spec:** `docs/specs/zenpom-v1.5.md` (DRAFT).
**Source decision:** `docs/plans/parked.md`, "Divide a stretch of time into a plan", raised 2026-08-27.
**Unblocked by:** `F9-T1`'s vocabulary ruling, made 2026-09-09. That was the only part of `F9` this
feature waited on.

---

## Paraphrasing it back

Today you state the **parts** — block length, break lengths, pomodoros per sprint — and the total
falls out. F8 inverts it: you state the **total** and the app works out the parts.

The owner's words: *"say I have 2 hours to work on something. I want to break it up into 3 sprints
over 120 minutes, with a good focus block and a minimum 5 minute break and a 10 minute long break."*

It fits what the app is actually for. You rarely have "four pomodoros". You have the gap before the
next thing.

**This is a change to the timer, not a screen on top of one.** `TimerSettingsSnapshot` is built from
`AppSettings` at every block boundary, at three sites. A sprint whose blocks differ from settings
means diverting that, and that is engine work with a fence around it.

---

## The rulings this plan needs · owner: **human**

These are the gate. Nothing below `T1` starts until they are made, and each is recorded as a `D<n>`.

### Ruling A — what is this thing called?

**`SessionPlan` already exists and already means "the ordered list of tasks you mean to work
through".** `parked.md` calls this feature "divide a stretch of time into a plan", which would put a
second, unrelated "plan" in the same app — the same vocabulary failure `F9-T1` just fixed in the
copy, arriving in the engine instead.

The two are genuinely different and will coexist: a session plan says **what** you will work on, this
says **how the time is cut up**. They compose — a two-hour shape working through a session plan is
the ordinary case.

**RULED 2026-09-09: a *shape*.** Settled by `docs/specs/definitions.md`, ratified the same day:
*shape* is how a stretch of time is cut into blocks, *plan* is what you work on. Both words are in the
definitions table and the fence in `F9-T2` enforces them.

**One divergence from that ratified file, recorded rather than smoothed over.** `definitions.md` names
**cycle** — one pom plus the break that follows it — and says of this feature: *"`F8` computes in
**cycles**, because that is what time is measured in."* This plan uses the word **zero times** and
computes in individual blocks with an absorption order over them. Nothing here misuses a word, so the
`F9-T2` fence is not at risk; but a ratified baseline states how this feature does its arithmetic and
this feature does it another way. That is either a restatement `T1` owes, or a `D<n>` against
`definitions.md`. Not settled here.

### Ruling B — does it write your settings, or run once?

**The larger fork by far**, and `parked.md` names it first.

Writing the six settings means a two-hour gap on Tuesday silently redefines your defaults forever.
Running once means the engine must follow a shape that differs from settings — the real work in `T2`.

**RULED 2026-09-09: both, and the shape is editable.** A computed shape can be adjusted before it
runs. It then **runs once by default**, and writes nothing — unless the person explicitly presses
**Save to settings**, which is the only path that may touch `AppSettings`.

This keeps the protection that mattered: a two-hour gap on a Tuesday never silently redefines your
defaults. It only ever does so as a deliberate act, which is a different thing from a side effect.

**The fence in `T2` changes shape but does not go away.** It is no longer "this path never writes
`AppSettings`" — it is **"this path writes `AppSettings` only through the explicit save, and never as
a consequence of running a shape."** Still one assertion, still mutation-testable.

### Ruling C — the arithmetic does not come out even. Which promise does zenpom keep?

**RULED 2026-09-09: both promises are kept, by absorbing the remainder rather than leaving it.** The
shape fills the budget exactly. It does not overrun and it does not hand time back.

**The owner's absorption order:** *long break, then short break, then focus.*

**Caps, so one bucket cannot swallow the whole remainder** — taken from the technique's own published
ranges rather than invented: a **long break absorbs up to 30 minutes** (the technique says 15–30), a
**short break up to 10** (it says 5–10), and **focus absorbs whatever is left**.

**The trailing long break: RULED — included only if the sprint actually completes.** This matches the
doctrine already in the source, that a long break is something a pomodoro *earns* and a skipped block
earns nothing. In shape terms: long breaks are **interior**, falling between poms after every
completed sprint. The break after the final pom is not scheduled, because a break you spend walking
out of the door is time the app gave back for nothing.

**BLOCKING — the paragraph above contradicts its own ruling and everything downstream of it.**
*"Long breaks are interior"* and *"the break after the final pom is not scheduled"* cannot both stand
with *a shape is one sprint*: a one-sprint shape's last pom **is** the final pom, so an interior-only
long break means **no shape ever gets one** — while every row of the worked table below ends with a
long break, the costing paragraph prices the trailing break at fourteen minutes of focus at two hours,
and Ruling D ships an **end with a long break** toggle that is **on by default**. A reader cannot tell
which half the owner ruled, and `T1` cannot be written until they can. Settle it before `T1` starts.

#### The selection rule, and the two traps under it

A shape must choose **how many poms** and **how long each is**. That objective function turned out to
be the whole design, and two natural-looking answers both degenerate.

**"Maximise focus minutes" fails.** Against the real defaults it returns **one 120-minute pom** for a
two-hour budget — every break deleted, the technique abandoned, and genuinely the most focus minutes
of any candidate. The correct answer to the question, and the wrong question.

**"Maximise the number of poms" fails the other way.** With the floors below it returns **eight
10-minute poms** for the same budget: maximum fragmentation, and just as far from the technique.

**The rule that works: a shape is one sprint.** It targets `pomodorosPerSprint` poms — four, from
settings — and flexes the **pom length** between the floor and the setting to fit the budget. Only
when four poms will not fit even at the floors does it reduce the count, and reduce
`pomodorosPerSprint` with it, so the final pom still completes its sprint and still earns its long
break.

**RULED 2026-09-09, two supporting decisions:**

- **Floors: a pom is never shorter than 10 minutes, a break never shorter than 5.** Shortening below
  the person's settings is allowed rather than refused — a 20-minute budget produces a real shape.
- **Below the minimum, warn.** The **minimum shape is 60 minutes and four poms**, and a shorter
  budget still produces a shape but says plainly that it is under the suggested minimum.

**Sixty minutes is not an arbitrary threshold.** `4 × 10 + 3 × 5 + 5 = 60` exactly: it is the smallest
budget that holds a full four-pom sprint at the floors. The owner's two stated numbers are the same
fact seen twice.

Worked against today's settings (25 / 5 / 15, four per sprint). **Each row is a test.**

| Budget | Shape | Focus | Unused |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 1 pom of 10m, long break 10m | 10m | 0 — **under minimum, warn** |
| 30 | 2 poms of 10m, 1 short of 5m, long break 5m | 20m | 0 — **warn** |
| 45 | 3 poms of 10m, 2 short of 5m, long break 5m | 30m | 0 — **warn** |
| **60** | **4 poms of 10m, 3 short of 5m, long break 5m** | **40m** | **0 — the minimum** |
| 90 | 4 poms of 15m, 3 short of 5m, long break 15m | 60m | 0 |
| **120** | **4 poms of 22m, 3 short of 5m, long break 17m** | **88m** | **0** |
| 180 | 4 poms of 30m, 3 short of 10m, long break 30m | 120m | 0 |

**Odd minutes go to the earliest poms**, one each, so a test can assert an exact shape rather than a
range.

**BLOCKING — that sentence and the table's own `120` row are two different remainder rules, and this
review does not get to pick.** With `4p + 3×5 + 15 = 120` the arithmetic leaves `p = 22` remainder 2.
*Odd minutes to the earliest poms* gives **23, 23, 22, 22 with a 15-minute long break**. The row states
**four poms of 22 and a 17-minute long break** — the remainder going to the long break instead, which
is the absorption order, not the odd-minutes rule. Both are stated as ruled, four lines apart.

It is not cosmetic: the same two minutes are spent both ways in the preset table below. *More focus* at
120 reads **90m**, which only the earliest-poms rule produces; *Balanced* at 120 reads **88m**, which
only the long-break rule produces. The **"spread is two minutes"** conclusion drawn from that pair is
therefore the two rules disagreeing, not the two presets differing. And `T1`'s checkpoint hard-codes
one of the two answers as the thing the suite asserts. One rule has to be ruled, and every figure
derived from it recomputed, before `T1` starts.

#### What the trailing long break costs, recorded so it can be revisited

Because a shape is one sprint, **every** shape budgets the long break its final pom earns. That is a
real cost and it should be visible rather than buried: the two-hour case fell from **102 minutes of
focus to 88** when this ruling landed, the long break taking 17 of them.

It is the correct reading of the app's own doctrine — a long break is earned, and a completed sprint
earns one. If the owner later prefers the focus time, dropping the trailing long break is a `D<n>`
and this paragraph is the argument it has to answer.

#### Ruling D — the user decides, on the shape screen

**RULED 2026-09-09.** The agreed rules are the default; the person can change two things, and both
controls live **on the shape screen** beside the numbers they change, with the last choice remembered
between shapes.

**No spec delta is owed, and that is the reason for the placement.** `SPEC.md`'s locked decisions end
*"Timer customization: … Nothing else."* Two new **settings rows** would contradict that clause and
require a `D<n>`. Two controls on the shape being edited fall under Ruling B — *the shape is
editable* — and contradict nothing. The cheaper option was also the more honest one: these change
*this shape*, not how the timer behaves forever.

**Control 1 — end with a long break.** On by default, as ruled. Off drops the trailing long break and
gives the time back to the shape.

**Control 2 — three named presets**, differing by **how much the breaks may absorb**, not by order:

| Preset | Order | Long cap | Short cap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Balanced** *(default, as agreed)* | long → short → focus | 30 | 10 |
| **More focus** | focus → long → short | 20 | 6 |
| **More rest** | long → short → focus | 45 | 15 |

**A correction, recorded because it was nearly built wrong.** These were first described as differing
by *order*, with "More rest" meaning *breaks first* — which is precisely what Balanced already does.
As worded, two of the three presets were the same rule. They differ by cap.

#### What the two controls are actually worth

**Hand arithmetic against the rules above — not a measurement.** No code exists yet, so nothing here
has been run, and the sentence that records a number is the sentence that has to say which it is.
Focus minutes by budget:

| Budget | Balanced | More focus | More rest | **Long break off** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 60 | 40m | 40m | 40m | **45m** |
| 120 | 88m | 90m | 88m | **102m** |
| 180 | 120m | 150m | 102m | **150m** |

**BLOCKING — the *More rest* / 180 cell does not follow from its own caps.** The settings total is
`4×25 + 3×5 + 15 = 130`, so a 180 budget leaves 50 to absorb. *More rest* absorbs long → short → focus
with a 45 cap and a 15 cap: the long break takes 30 of it (15 → 45), the three short breaks take the
remaining 20 (5 → 7, 7, 6), and **focus is never reached**, leaving it at the setting — **100m, not the
102m stated.** That one cell is the whole of the claim that the presets *"genuinely diverge"* at three
hours, which is in turn the stated reason for building the preset control rather than shipping Balanced
alone. Recompute it, or the ruling rests on a figure nobody can reproduce.

**The preset barely matters below three hours, and the toggle always does.** At sixty minutes the
three presets are *identical* — the shape sits at its floors and there is no remainder to distribute,
so the control is inert. At two hours the spread is two minutes. Only at three hours do they
genuinely diverge.

The trailing-break toggle is worth **five minutes at sixty, fourteen at two hours, thirty at three**.

**RULED 2026-09-09: both are built, and the presets are kept deliberately in order to be tested.**
The owner: *"we need to test them before we choose one way or the other."* That is the right way round
— the table above is arithmetic, and arithmetic cannot say whether a three-hour session *feels* better
at 150 focus minutes or 102. Only using it can.

**It owes an owner item, because "decide after testing" is a decision nobody is scheduled to make.**
Proposed: *after real use, does the preset control earn its place, or does the app ship Balanced
alone?* Without a row, the control survives by default rather than by evidence, which is how surface
accumulates.

`T3` must test the presets **at a budget where they differ**. At sixty minutes all three return an
identical shape, so an assertion there passes whatever the code does — the exact shape of every
toothless test this project has already been bitten by.

#### One edge this ruling does not reach

**Should focus absorption be capped too?** At 180 minutes the poms reach 30, above the 25-minute
setting, because focus absorbs last and there is nothing left to stop it. That may be right — it is
what "focus absorbs the remainder" means — but it is unstated rather than decided. It belongs to `T1`
and does not block it.

## Ruling E — where a shape is stored, and for how long

**Raised by the cross-plan review, 2026-09-09: nothing in this plan said where a shape lives.**
`T1` is explicitly no-storage. `T2` was an in-memory override. But `T4` requires a shape to survive
*"one abandoned and relaunched mid-block"* and *"a shape that outlives the app being killed"* — and
no task owned the thing that makes either true. The requirement was written and the mechanism was
not.

**The owner ruled, 2026-09-09:** *"the shape should be persisted in the settings in the most
lightweight storage method Swift allows. I would prefer to not have a db on this unless we reach the
point where we have to."*

### RULED: a single `Codable` value in `UserDefaults`, written by one type, cleared when the sprint ends

Not a thirteenth `@Model`. Not an eighth `AppSettings` column. One small encoded value under one key,
owned by one file, with the whole of `UserDefaults` still forbidden everywhere else.

**This is not free and the plan should not pretend it is.** The recommendation reached this plan
described as moving neither of the counts `F12` and `F16` pin, which is true, but it moves a *third*
pinned number that the recommendation did not mention. `ZenTomatoTests/PolishFenceTests.swift:120`:

```
#expect(
  try Self.countAcrossApp("UserDefaults") == 0,
  "A repair pass has reached for a second kind of persistence.")
```

**Zero, across every line of shipped Swift.** So this ruling trips a fence, and it owes a delta — a
change to `SPEC.md`'s locked `Data | Local only (SwiftData)` row. Referred to here as **the
shape-store delta**, by name and not by number, because it is not ratified.

**That the fence fires is the fence working, not an obstacle to route around.** Its own doc comment
at `PolishFenceTests.swift:115-116` says so: *"If a measurement genuinely demands a cache, this test
failing is the correct outcome: it stops the pass and moves the argument to a delta, where it
belongs."* The wrong move here would be to reach for a storage location that happens not to be
fenced.

#### Why not the three alternatives, in the order they look reasonable

**An eighth `AppSettings` column — refused, and this is the strongest refusal of the three.** Two
reasons, either sufficient.

- **It contradicts Ruling B in the same feature.** Ruling B's whole protection is that running a
  shape never writes `AppSettings`, and `T2`'s fence exists to enforce exactly that. Putting the
  running shape *inside* `AppSettings` makes that fence assert something incoherent: the path would
  write `AppSettings` on every start, and the fence would have to carve out the one column it is
  meant to be watching. A fence with an exception for the thing it guards is not a fence.
- **It collides with `F12`.** `docs/plans/F12.md:239` moves
  `Schema([AppSettings.self]).entities.first.properties.count` from 7 to 8 for the theme. A second
  independent 7→8 move in the same milestone means whichever lands first consumes the other's
  evidence — the second feature's schema change arrives at a fence that already reads 8 and says
  nothing.

**A thirteenth `@Model` — refused.** `docs/plans/F16.md:288` uses precisely this number as its own
mutation signal: `F16-M4` is caught by *"`noNewStoredShape` in `PolishFenceTests` (the `@Model` count
moves from 12 to 13)"*. If `F8` moves it to 13 first, `F16`'s named mutation is caught by nothing and
`F16` ships with a mutation it can no longer run. It also buys a SwiftData migration over everybody's
real history for a value whose intended lifetime is one afternoon.

**Columns on `TimerState` — the genuine runner-up, and worth stating why it loses.** Its lifetime is
right: `TimerState` is the per-run row that already exists *because* a block must survive the app
being killed, and it already freezes six settings values per block for the same reason a shape needs
freezing. Three things beat it. A shape is a **variable-length list of blocks**, which a flat-column
row cannot hold — it would be an encoded blob in the middle of a row whose doc comment
(`ZenTomato/Models/TimerState.swift:26-28`) argues explicitly for *"six plain numbers … to anybody
reading the file"*. It is still a SwiftData migration. And it pins its own number,
`timerStateColumnCount = 17` at `PolishFenceTests.swift:91`, so it is not cheaper on that axis
either. If the owner would rather pay the migration than amend the `UserDefaults` fence, this is the
option to pay it on — it is the first open question below.

#### The argument `AppSettings` already makes against this, answered

`ZenTomato/Models/AppSettings.swift:29` carries a heading, **`WHY SWIFTDATA AND NOT UserDefaults`**:
*"So there is one store to reason about, one backup story, and one place to look when something is
wrong."* This ruling has to answer it rather than ignore it.

**It is answered because it is an argument about preferences, and a shape is not one.** What that
paragraph protects is durable user data being split across two systems: settings you set once and
expect back next year, sitting in one place, backed up with everything else. A shape is **one
sprint's worth of intent** — written when you start, deleted when the sprint ends, never read again.
It has no backup story to be inconsistent with because there is nothing in it worth restoring.

**`SessionPlan` makes exactly this argument for the neighbouring case, and it is already shipped.**
`ZenTomato/Models/SessionPlan.swift:14`: *"**There is one plan.** Making a new one replaces the old
one. A plan is not history — what actually happened is already written down, block by block, on the
finished-block rows, and a plan that outlived its session would be a second, competing account of the
same day."* Every clause of that is true of a shape with the noun changed. The history of a shaped
sprint is on the finished-block rows, the same rows an ordinary sprint writes, which is the whole
subject of `T5`.

**One honest difference, recorded rather than smoothed over:** `SessionPlan` reached that conclusion
and still stayed in SwiftData. So the precedent supports *"a shape is not history"*; it does not by
itself support *"therefore `UserDefaults`"*. What supports the second half is the owner's ruling and
the two collisions above.

#### What the fence becomes, and it gets stricter rather than weaker

`noNewPersistentSurface` does **not** change from `== 0` to `== 3`. A bare number would let a
`UserDefaults` read appear in `TimerEngine` as long as one disappeared elsewhere. It becomes a
statement about *where*:

> `UserDefaults` appears in exactly one shipped file, and that file is the shape store.

Same instrument, same helper, a claim that cannot be satisfied by moving code around — which is the
correction `noNewStoredShape` already had to make for `AppSettings` (`PolishFenceTests.swift:66-73`:
*"a fence that can be satisfied by moving code is measuring file shape rather than the database"*).
Mutation `F8-M5` below is what proves it.

#### The three questions the review asked, answered

**1 · What happens to a stored shape when its sprint ends?** It is **deleted**, at the moment the
shape's last block finishes. Not archived, not kept for a "recent shapes" list, not left to be
overwritten by the next one. There is at most one stored shape, the way there is at most one
`SessionPlan` and one `TimerState` row. A shape that survived its sprint would be the second
competing account `SessionPlan` refused.

The same clear happens when a shaped sprint is **stopped early**, subject to the third open question
below — that case is a genuine preference and this plan does not settle it.

**2 · What happens when settings change underneath it?** **The shape wins, for the whole of its
run, and settings changed mid-shape take effect at the next shape.** The blocks were computed once
and are frozen — the same discipline `TimerState` already applies when it copies the settings into
the row at each boundary so that *"changing the focus length while a focus block is running cannot
shorten the block you are already in"* (`TimerState.swift:21-25`).

The precise rule, because "the shape wins" is too coarse to test:

- **Block lengths, block order, and how many poms — from the shape.** Including
  `pomodorosPerSprint`, which Ruling C may already have reduced below the setting.
- **Everything else — from `AppSettings`, per block, exactly as today.** `soundEnabled`,
  `alertSoundRawValue`, `autoStartNextBlock`. A shape says how the time is cut up and nothing else;
  turning the alarm sound off mid-shape must still work, because that is not a claim about time.

This matters more than it looks: with `autoStartNextBlock` off — which is the shipped default — the
timer is **idle** between blocks, and settings are editable in exactly that window. `SPEC.md`'s
read-only-while-running rule does not cover it. So the mid-shape settings edit is not an exotic case;
it is the ordinary one.

**3 · What happens when a build is installed over a running app?** `UserDefaults` survives an app
update, so **a shape written by an older build is read by a newer one**, and the stored value must be
able to say *"I do not understand this"* without taking the timer down with it.

- The encoded value carries a **version tag**, and a decode that fails for any reason — unknown
  version, a field this build has never heard of, corrupt data — **reads back as no shape at all.**
- **No shape is the safe state, and it is already specified.** `T2`'s acceptance condition is that
  with no shape present the app is byte-for-byte what it is today. So the failure mode of the whole
  storage layer is "the app behaves like v1.0", which is the only failure mode worth having.
- This is the same softening `AlertSound.stored(_:)` already does for `alertSoundRawValue`
  (`AppSettings.swift:65-73`), for the same stated reason: *"a person can move a database between
  versions without losing a fortnight of distraction log over an alarm tone."*
- **BLOCKING — *"`UserDefaults` survives an app update"* is a claim about a system outside this
  repository, and nothing has been run to show it.** The conventions are explicit: where a decision
  asserts that something outside the repo can do something, the confirming command and its output go
  on the row *before* ratification, and the evidence is the observable end state rather than the
  documented behaviour. So this whole sub-ruling — and the version tag it justifies — is written in
  the confident register of something established while being a prediction. **The shape-store delta
  cannot be ratified on this paragraph as it stands.** The check is cheap and it is the owner's own
  hardware: install a build that writes a shape, install the next build over it, read the value back,
  and paste what came out.
- **Staleness is a separate question from decoding.** A shape that decodes perfectly but was written
  last Tuesday is not a shape you want restored. Proposed rule, not ratified: a stored shape is
  discarded on read when the timer row is not running **and** the shape's projected finish is in the
  past. The grace period around that is the second open question.

#### One observation, not a task for `F8`

`AppSettings.swift:8` and `:12` still read *"six columns"* and *"EXACTLY SIX PROPERTIES"* while the
type has seven since `D24` — the schema fence at `PolishFenceTests.swift:76` was updated and the
header above it was not. It is recorded here because it was read during this ruling and because
`F12`'s eighth column will read the same header. Fixing it belongs to whoever moves that number, not
to `F8`.

### Foundational — no user-visible change

#### `F8-T1` — The shape calculator · owner: **agent**

A pure function: `(available minutes, constraints) → [Block]`. No SwiftData, no UI, no engine, no
clock. Constraints come from `SettingsBounds`, which already owns the legal ranges and already
explains why one definition rather than two.

It is first because it is the whole of the feature's arithmetic, and arithmetic is the part that can
be got right before anything is drawn.

**The cases that decide the design, and they are the tests:** 120 minutes exactly · 120 with a
minimum 5-minute break and a 10-minute long break, the owner's stated example · a duration too short
for even one focus block · a duration that leaves an awkward remainder · a duration long enough for
more than one long break · one minute, which `SettingsBounds` says is a real setting
(`ZenTomato/Timer/SettingsBounds.swift:28`, `minutes: ClosedRange<Int> = 1...120`).

**BLOCKING — the second case in that list is the owner's stated example, and the ruled algorithm
cannot produce it.** The words quoted at the top of this plan ask for *"3 sprints over 120 minutes …
a minimum 5 minute break and a 10 minute long break"*. Under `docs/specs/definitions.md` a **sprint**
is the set of poms before a long break, so three sprints means three long breaks; the selection rule
ruled above makes a shape **one** sprint of four poms, and the absorption order gives it a **17-minute**
long break rather than the 10 that was asked for. Two readings are open — *"3 sprints"* was said
colloquially for *"3 poms"*, or the stated break lengths are **inputs the shape must honour** rather
than outputs it computes — and they produce different calculators. The plan quotes the request as the
thing being built and then rules something that cannot express it, with the divergence recorded
nowhere. It is the owner's to settle, and it decides what this task's headline test asserts.

**Mutation, named now and run before this task is called done:** `F8-M1` in the table below — make
the calculator return a shape whose total exceeds the budget by one minute, and confirm the
never-overrun test fails, with the suite count recorded.

**The selection rule and the absorption order above are the specification**, and the worked table is
the test data. Each row is an assertion.

*Checkpoint:* the owner's own example — two hours — produces **4 poms of 22 minutes, three short
breaks of 5, a 17-minute long break and 88 minutes of focus, nothing unused**; sixty minutes produces
the minimum shape exactly; twenty minutes produces a real shape **and** the under-minimum warning. Both
rejected objective functions are present as tests, proving they would return one 120-minute pom and
eight 10-minute poms respectively.

#### `F8-T2` — The override seam and the shape store · owner: **agent**

**The storage work is folded in here rather than given a task of its own, deliberately.** `T2`
already owns the one question *"where does the block the engine is about to run come from?"*, and
Ruling E is the other half of the same answer: it comes from a shape, and the shape comes from
somewhere that survives a relaunch. Splitting them would produce a seam with nothing behind it and a
store nothing reads — two tasks, neither of which is a checkpoint you can hold. It stays one task
with **two checkpoints**.

`TimerSettingsSnapshot(clamping: AppSettings.current(in: context))` appears at **three** sites, not
four: `ZenTomato/Timer/TimerEngine.swift:268`, `TimerEngine.swift:849`, and
`ZenTomato/Models/TimerState.swift:222`. **Two further snapshot constructions exist and the seam has
to account for both**, because neither travels through that call:
`ZenTomato/Timer/TimerSettingsSnapshot.swift:95` — `fallback`, built from a fresh unsaved
`AppSettings` for the state where the database cannot be read at all — and `TimerState.swift:143`,
`snapshot`, rebuilt from the row's own frozen columns, **which is the restore path `T4`'s
kill-and-relaunch case actually travels**. The snapshot resolves from an **optional shape first,
falling back to `AppSettings`**.

**With no shape present, behaviour is byte-for-byte what it is today.** That is the acceptance
condition, and it is what makes this task safe to land before anything visible exists. It is also,
per Ruling E, the failure mode of the entire storage layer: anything the store cannot read is no
shape, and no shape is v1.0.

**The store, per Ruling E.** One `Codable` value, one key, one file, version-tagged, lenient on
decode, cleared when the sprint ends. It also holds Ruling D's two controls — the trailing-break
toggle and the preset — because *"remembered between shapes"* in `T3` is the same durability
requirement wearing different clothes, and a second store for two enum-sized values would be the
split `AppSettings.swift:29` warns about, this time for real.

**Two fences, and they are the point of the task rather than a garnish:**

- **Ruling B's:** this path writes `AppSettings` only through the explicit **Save to settings**, and
  never as a consequence of running a shape.
- **Ruling E's:** `UserDefaults` appears in exactly one shipped file, and that file is the shape
  store.

**It cannot land without the shape-store delta ratified.** The second fence is an amendment to
`noNewPersistentSurface`, and amending a fence to admit the thing it forbids is precisely what a
delta is for. `T1` can land without it; this cannot.

**Mutations:** `F8-M2`, `F8-M3`, `F8-M4`, `F8-M5` in the table below — the settings write, the shape
outliving its sprint, the unreadable shape becoming a default one, and `UserDefaults` escaping its
file.

*Checkpoint one — the seam:* the full existing suite passes unchanged with no shape present, and the
settings-write fence is seen to fail when mutated.

*Checkpoint two — the store:* on the simulator, write a shape, **kill the app from the app switcher**,
relaunch, and read the same shape back mid-block; then finish the shape and confirm nothing is stored;
then hand the store a value with an unknown version tag and confirm the app runs on `AppSettings` as
though no shape had ever existed.

### Vertical slices — each one a thing you can hold

#### `F8-T3` — Ask for a duration, show the shape · owner: **agent**

An entry surface: *how long have you got?* → the proposed shape, shown **before** anything starts —
number of poms, their length, the breaks, and the projected finish time. Nothing runs yet.

**The shape is editable here** (Ruling B), and this screen carries the only **Save to settings**
control in the feature. Saving is a deliberate press; starting a shape without pressing it writes
nothing.

It also carries Ruling D's two controls — **end with a long break** and the **three presets** — with
the shape recomputing as they change, so the effect is visible in the numbers rather than described in
a footnote. Both are remembered between shapes and neither writes `AppSettings`.

**Test them at a budget where they do something.** At sixty minutes all three presets return the same
shape, so an assertion there passes no matter what the code does.

**No capture surface is created.** This asks for a duration, never for a task. Tasks come from
Todoist and from nowhere else, and a session plan is chosen the way it already is.

*Checkpoint:* on the device, enter 120, read back **the shape the worked table's `120` row names** —
four poms, three short breaks and the trailing long break — finishing exactly two hours out, adjust one
value, walk away without starting a timer, and confirm `AppSettings` is unchanged.

**The draft said "three six-minute breaks" here, and no preset produces that at 120.** Balanced's short
breaks are five minutes at that budget and the caps do not bite below the settings total; the only 6 in
the plan is *More focus*'s short cap, which is unreachable at two hours. The checkpoint now cites the
table rather than restating a number, because the exact minutes follow from the remainder rule flagged
under Ruling C and are not settled yet.

#### `F8-T4` — Run a shaped sprint · owner: **agent**

The engine follows the shape: each boundary takes the next block from it rather than from settings,
alarms are scheduled per block as they already are, and the sprint ends where the shape ends.

**The edges that need tests, because they are where this feature meets the existing engine:** a
shaped sprint that is stopped early · one abandoned and relaunched mid-block, which must restore the
shape and not fall back to settings · what a skipped block does to the remaining shape · a shape that
outlives the app being killed.

**Three of those four are survival requirements, and `T2` is what makes them possible.** Before
Ruling E this task listed them with nothing underneath them. It now leans on `T2`'s store and adds
the two cases Ruling E introduced: **the settings edited mid-shape while the timer is idle between
blocks** — the shape's block lengths must be untouched while a sound change takes effect at the next
block — and **a stale shape from a previous day**, which must be discarded rather than resumed.

**And the surface that reads the sprint count second-hand, which no task currently names.** The Lock
Screen draws `SprintCount(completed: metadata.completedInSprint, total: metadata.pomodorosPerSprint)`
(`ZenTomatoActivity/BlockLiveActivity.swift:50` and `:151`), fed from `TimerState`'s frozen column by
`ZenTomato/Timer/TimerEngine.swift:1270`. A shape that reduces the count — which Ruling C's selection
rule does whenever four poms will not fit — reaches that surface **only if the seam writes through
those columns rather than around them**. So a shaped sprint of three poms must be asserted to read
*"of 3"*, not *"of 4"*. It is one assertion, and it is the difference between the shape being real and
the shape being a screen.

*Checkpoint:* a full shaped sprint runs to completion, on the simulator with one-minute blocks, then
on the device — including one deliberate kill-and-relaunch mid-block, and one settings edit in the
idle gap between two blocks that changes the sound and does not change a single block length.

#### `F8-T5` — The log survives it · owner: **agent**

**This is the task that protects the reason the app exists.** Every pomodoro in a shaped sprint must
attach to its Todoist item exactly as an ordinary one does; distraction taps and notes must record
against the right block; the stats counts and the Markdown export must be unchanged in shape.

`StatsMarkdownGoldenTests` already exists and is the instrument — a shaped sprint's export is
compared against a golden file, so a change to the export's shape cannot pass unnoticed.

**If a scope decision ever threatens the log, the log wins.** A shaped sprint that produces a
different-looking export is a defect in F8, not a new export format.

*Checkpoint:* the export of one shaped sprint, read beside the export of an ordinary one.

#### `F8-T6` — A real two-hour gap · owner: **human**

`SPEC.md`'s standard: a *Done when* is closed on hardware, by the owner, with a build number and
their own words. Not an assertion.

Plan a genuine two-hour gap before a real commitment. Run it. Answer three things: did it finish
before the commitment · did the drift between blocks matter · was the export readable in the Rhodia
afterwards.

*Checkpoint:* recorded as an `O<n>`, closed by the owner with the build number.

## Deployment

There is no app store step in v1.5. The build reaches the phone the way every build has: `make
device` with a `DEVELOPMENT_TEAM` set, or TestFlight via the `C9` runbook. **`C22` no longer exists:
it was struck by `D31`, ratified 2026-09-09 (`docs/plans/00-deltas.md:1677`), because the licence
question had already been answered.** So there is no licence blocker here, and none on the About
screen either — the draft's sentence describing one was written against a chore that is gone.

## Mutations

Numbered per plan, following `F16.md`'s precedent — there is no `## Mutations` section in
`docs/plans/00-register.md` and this plan does not invent one. Each is a specific edit to the shipped
code, applied and **seen to fail**, with the suite count recorded in the PR.

| Mutation — a specific edit to the shipped code | The named test that must fail |
|---|---|
| `F8-M1` · Make the calculator return a shape whose total exceeds the budget by one minute | the never-overrun assertion in the `T1` suite |
| `F8-M2` · Make the override path write one resolved value back to `AppSettings` | Ruling B's fence — the path writes `AppSettings` only through the explicit save |
| `F8-M3` · Skip the clear when the shape's last block finishes, so the shape survives its sprint | `aShapeDoesNotOutliveItsSprint` — start a shape, run it to the end, assert the store is empty |
| `F8-M4` · Make the decoder return the *default* shape instead of no shape when it cannot read the stored value | `anUnreadableShapeIsNoShape` — write a value with an unknown version tag, assert the engine runs on `AppSettings` |
| `F8-M5` · Add one `UserDefaults` read to `TimerEngine.swift`, and delete one from the shape store so the total is unchanged | the amended `noNewPersistentSurface` — **and this is the mutation that proves the amendment.** A count-based fence stays green under this edit; a file-scoped one does not |

**`F8-M5` is the one that earns its design**, the way `F16-M2` does in its own plan. It is the edit a
well-meaning person actually makes — *"the engine needs to know the preset, I'll just read it here"* —
and the obvious version of the fence, a bare `== 3`, passes it untouched.

**`F8-M4` is the one most likely to be written toothless.** An assertion that hands the store a value
it built itself, through the same encoder the app uses, tests the encoder round-tripping and nothing
else. The input must be a **byte string with a version tag no build has ever written**, so that the
right and the wrong implementation give different answers.

## Open questions · owner: **human**

Not answered here. Each changes what gets built, is written down nowhere, and cannot be settled by
investigation.

1. **Ratify the shape-store delta as `UserDefaults`, or pay the `TimerState` migration instead?**
   Ruling E argues for the first and states the second's price honestly. This is the owner's call
   because it is a trade between amending a fence and migrating a database, and both are defensible.
2. **How stale is too stale?** The proposed rule discards a stored shape when the timer is not
   running and its projected finish is past. Someone who steps away for twenty minutes and comes back
   would rather resume. Is there a grace period, and how long?
3. **Does a shape survive being stopped early?** `T4` lists *stopped early* as an edge and no ruling
   says whether the shape is cleared with the timer or offered back on the next start. Ruling C's
   doctrine — a long break is earned — points at clearing it; the person who hit Stop by accident
   points the other way.

## Scope fence

Not in F8, each needing its own gate: changing what `autoStartNextBlock` defaults to · a capture
surface of any kind · saving or naming shapes for reuse · recurring or calendar-aware shapes ·
reading the calendar to find the gap · any change to the export format.

**"Saving a shape" is the one most likely to be asked for mid-build**, because it feels small once
the calculator exists. It is a stored user-authored object, which is a different thing from a
one-off, and Ruling B is exactly the argument it would reopen.

**Ruling E makes that ask cheaper and therefore more dangerous, and the fence has to say so.** After
`T2` there *is* a store, and "save this shape under a name" looks like one dictionary key away. It is
not: the store is **single-slot and unnamed on purpose**, it is cleared when the sprint ends, and a
second slot turns one sprint's intent into a library of user-authored objects — the thing
`SessionPlan` refused, the thing `PolishFenceTests.nothingFromTheParkedList` exists to keep out, and
the thing the shape-store delta would not have been ratified for. A named, retained shape is its own
gate and its own delta.
