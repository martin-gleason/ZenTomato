## The tension, named rather than papered over

**The owner's stated reason for this feature does not authorise it, and `CLAUDE.md` is explicit
about why.** The standing rule is: *"Anything not listed there is Phase 2. Do not build it, stub it,
or 'prepare for it.'"* `D16` supplies the test — **would I write this the same way if the parked
feature were never coming?** *"v2.0 will have a fully functioning tool on watchOS, so we should build
out and prep for that"* is, word for word, the thing the rule forbids. A plan that opened by quoting
the owner's sentence as its justification would be a plan that had stopped reading at the sentence it
liked.

**It does not need that justification, and this is the whole of the argument for building it.**

`F17`, the watch-face complication, is item 11 of the ratified v1.5 order, and its earned-entry
sentence in the ratified spec is *"Lets a block be **seen and started** from the watch face."*
`F17-T5` — the tap that starts a pom — **cannot be built without this unit**, for a structural
reason rather than a stylistic one:

- A complication is drawn by a **watchOS widget extension**, a separate bundle and a separate
  process. `project.yml:300-343` shows the watch app is a single-target `WKApplication` today and no such
  extension exists; `docs/plans/F17.md` calls that missing target the reason `F17` is the largest of
  the small items.
- The wrist's `WCSession` lives in the watch app's process — `ZenTomatoWatch/WatchLink.swift`
  activates `WCSession.default` in that app's `init`. **A third process cannot drive it.**
  `WCSession.default` is per-process; the extension does not inherit the app's session, and the app
  may not be running at all when the face is tapped.
- So a face button has nothing to call. It cannot start a block locally — the watch has no engine,
  and `WatchLinkTests.theWatchOwnsNoTimer` (`ZenTomatoTests/WatchLinkTests.swift:55`) reads the watch
  target's sources and fails if `TimerEngine` appears in one. It cannot send a message itself. The
  only thing it can do is **invoke a named command**, which is exactly what an App Intent is, and
  which is the mechanism `DismissBlockIntent` already proves on the phone: *"a Lock Screen button
  cannot be given an ordinary action… the app declares a small named command and the button says
  'run that command'"* (`ZenTomato/Shared/DismissBlockIntent.swift:6-12`).

**That is a v1.5 requirement, today.** The v2.0 benefit is real — an intent is the natural seam for a
more independent watch app — but it is a **consequence of building this properly, not the reason for
building it**, and the distinction is load-bearing rather than rhetorical.

**Here is the falsifiable form of that claim, so it can be checked at the review rather than
believed.** If this plan were prepping for v2.0 it would contain at least one of the following, and
it contains none of them:

| What v2.0 prep would look like | Why it is absent |
|---|---|
| A parameter on the intent — a duration, a task, a shape | `D2`: *never picks a task*. And an intent parameter is a capture surface (`F10` made this argument first). Fenced in `F18-T3`. |
| A local start path on the watch, behind a flag or a protocol | There is no engine to reach. `theWatchOwnsNoTimer` would fail. |
| An abstraction over *"who runs the engine"* | Exactly the provider-abstraction shape `D16` refuses. There is one caller and it is the phone. |
| A watch-side store, cache or queue for launches | `noStoreOnWatch`, and `F14`'s ruling that a launch is **never** queued. |
| A second intent — stop, skip, pause — because they would be needed later | Out. Named in the scope fence. |

**One intent. It starts a block. It carries no parameters and makes no decisions.** If v2.0 never
arrives, this file is unchanged.

## Paraphrasing it back

`F14` is putting a **button** on the wrist, inside the watch app, which sends a launch request to the
phone. `F18` adds a **named command** — the same operation, addressed by name rather than by a
button — so that something which is *not the watch app* can ask for it: the complication (`F17-T5`),
and the watch's Shortcuts app.

The wrist still does not run a timer, does not choose a length, does not choose an attachment, and
does not decide anything about the block. It rings the same doorbell `F14` installs. The phone
answers, decides, and starts. The wrist learns whether it worked from the reply and, in the longer
run, from the state the phone already sends it.

**What this is not.** It is not an independent watch app — that is v2.0 and it contradicts `D2`. It
is not a second transport. It is not a second entry point into the engine: everything arrives at
`BlockLauncher`, the seam `F14-T1` builds, exactly as `F10`'s intent does.

## Does `D2` survive? Yes, intact, and here is the check rather than the assertion

`D2`'s ratified text (`docs/plans/00-deltas.md:81-86`) states four prohibitions:

> The watch **never runs a timer of its own, never controls music, never picks a task, and never
> edits a distraction note.**

Against this feature, one at a time:

- **Runs a timer.** No. The intent's entire body is *construct a request, hand it to the sender, wait
  for the reply*. No `endsAt`, no length, no advance, no alarm. The engine is on the phone and
  `BlockLauncher` decides whether it starts at all.
- **Controls music.** No. Nothing here touches `MusicKit`, and the watch target cannot: `import
  MusicKit` is a forbidden string in `theWatchOwnsNoTimer`.
- **Picks a task.** No — **and this is the one that could be broken by a plausible-looking change.**
  The attachment comes from `SessionPlanStore.takeNextAttachment()` on the phone, as `F14` ruled. The
  moment this intent grows a parameter naming what to work on, the wrist becomes the author of what a
  pom is about and `D2` is contradicted. That is why `F18-T3` is half fence.
- **Edits a distraction note.** No. This intent captures nothing and displays no tally.

**The phone remains the source of truth and runs the only timer engine.** Nothing in this plan moves
authority to the wrist; it moves an *address* to the wrist.

**So: no delta is owed, and I would not propose one.** For completeness, the shapes that would owe
one, by name rather than by number:

- **A parameter on the intent** would owe *the wrist-attachment delta* — it contradicts `D2`'s *never
  picks a task* and `CLAUDE.md`'s no-capture rule at the same time. It is out of scope here.
- **The complication itself** owes the delta `F17` proposes against `SPEC.md` line 58 (*"widgets
  beyond the Lock Screen Live Activity"*). **`F18` does not owe it**: an App Intent is not a widget,
  and `F10` is already building a phone-side intent under no delta at all. This matters for
  sequencing — `F18` can merge while that delta is still unratified.

**No `D` number is cited anywhere in this file for an unratified item**, deliberately.
`DeltaIntegrityTests` walks `docs/` and fails the build on a `D`-token with no heading in
`00-deltas.md`, and three plans in this batch independently allocated the same next-free number.

## The three questions the owner asked, answered honestly, with the unverified parts marked

### 1. Can a watchOS App Intent reach the phone when the watch app is not running?

**I could not verify this, and it is the single question this feature turns on.** What I can state is
the mechanism and where the uncertainty sits:

- An App Intent's `perform()` runs in *some* process, and **which one is the whole question.**
  `DismissBlockIntent`'s comment records the answer for the iOS Live Activity case — *"iOS never runs
  a Live Activity command in the widget's process — it always runs it in the app's"*
  (`ZenTomato/Shared/DismissBlockIntent.swift:36-39`) — and that file's `#if !WIDGET_EXTENSION`
  switch is built on it. **That is a claim about `LiveActivityIntent` on iOS. It is not evidence
  about a plain `AppIntent` invoked from a watchOS widget button, and reusing it here would be
  exactly the mistake `conventions.md` warns about: a familiar system produces a confident sentence.**
- If the intent runs in the **watch app's process**, and the system launches that app in the
  background to run it, then `WatchLink`'s `WCSession` is live and this feature works silently. That
  is the good case and it is what the design below assumes, *provisionally*.
- If the intent runs in the **widget extension's process**, it has no `WCSession`. Whether a watchOS
  widget extension can activate one of its own is undocumented as far as I can establish, and I have
  not run it. If it cannot, the fallback is `openAppWhenRun` / a foreground-continuable intent — the
  watch app visibly launches, sends, and the glance-and-tap becomes a tap-and-watch-an-app-appear.
  **That is a materially smaller feature and the owner should hear it described that way before
  saying yes.** It is Open Question 2.
- There is a third possibility I cannot rule out: that the answer differs between a Shortcuts
  invocation and a complication-button invocation. The spike must therefore test **both**.

Under `docs/conventions.md` — *a decision about what another system can do is not ratifiable until
something has run* — none of this is ratifiable today. `F18-T1` is that run, and it comes first.

### 2. What does it do when the phone is unreachable?

**It fails, promptly, and says so. It never queues.** This is `F14`'s ruling and `F18` inherits it
whole rather than restating it: a launch is *a command about the present*, whose worst failure is
being delivered late, so it travels by `sendMessage` and not by `transferUserInfo`.
`ZenTomatoWatch/WatchLink.swift:98-109` uses `transferUserInfo` for a tap, correctly and for the
opposite reason, and that comment is the clearest statement of the distinction in the tree.

**The claim underneath it is still unverified and `F14` says so** — that `sendMessage` requires live
reachability and errors *promptly* when there is none. `F14-T2`'s spike is where that is measured.
`F18` does not re-measure it and does not assert it.

**An intent has one failure mode a button does not, and it is the reason `F18-T4` exists.** A button
that fails can leave the screen showing a refusal. **An intent that returns `.result()` with no
dialog is indistinguishable from success** — Siri says nothing, the Shortcut goes green, and somebody
walks away believing a pom is running. Every outcome this intent can produce must carry a sentence.

### 3. Does `D2` survive?

Yes — the section above. The short form: `F18` moves an address to the wrist, not an authority.

## Where the code has to live, and why it is not where you would first put it

**A discovery from reading `project.yml` and the test target, not a preference, and it changes the
shape of two tasks.**

`ZenTomatoTests` is an **iOS** test bundle hosted by the phone app (`project.yml:360-377`). It does
**not** compile the watch target. That is why `WatchLinkTests` reads watch sources as *text*
(`watchSources()`, `ZenTomatoTests/WatchLinkTests.swift:132`) instead of calling into them — a string
scan is all that is available.

So anything in this feature that deserves a real assertion **cannot live in `ZenTomatoWatch/`**.
Concretely:

- **`F14`'s plan places the wire types correctly** and for this exact reason: `LaunchRequest` and
  `LaunchOutcome` are to live in `ZenTomato/WatchLink/Payload/`, which `project.yml:306-309` compiles
  into **both** targets. (Neither type exists yet — `F14` has written no code, and that directory
  holds `WatchBlockState`, `WatchLinkKeys` and `WatchTap` today. The precedent is real; the two types
  are not, until `F14` merges.) `F18` adds no wire type. It adds, in that same directory, the **mapping from
  outcome to the sentence a person hears** — so `F18-T4`'s wording is testable by the existing iOS
  suite, as `WatchBlockState` and `WatchTap` already are.
- Only the **`AppIntent` shell** stays in `ZenTomatoWatch/` — the conformance, the title, the
  discoverability flag, and one call into the sender. Everything it does is tested elsewhere.

**One thing `F18` may have to add, and `F14` has already asked about it.** `F14` proposes a `Source`
enum on the launch request carrying a `watchIntent` case it never sends, arguing that without it
`F18` must either edit a sealed type or misreport itself as `.wrist` — and it puts that to the owner
as its own Q8, on the grounds that a named case for an unbuilt feature reads as preparation. **If
you delete it there, `F18-T3` adds it, and that is the right place for it**: at that point it is not
preparation, it is the door that exists.

**Written as a rule rather than an arrangement:** the watch target holds nothing that a test would
want to assert on, because nothing there can be asserted on. The alternative — a wording table inside
the watch app, checked by a string scan — is the toothless-test shape this project has already been
bitten by five times (`docs/conventions.md`, *Assertions are not evidence*).

**And a gap worth naming while it is in view.** `NoCaptureSurfaceTests` is compiled-type-based and
covers phone screens only; `StatsFenceTests.featureFiles`
(`ZenTomatoTests/StatsFenceTests.swift:280`) lists `ZenTomato/Stats`, `Export`, `Sprint`, the stats
views and four named phone files. **Neither looks at `ZenTomatoWatch/` at all.** A `@Parameter` added
to a watch intent would be caught by nothing in the suite today. `F18-T3` closes that.

### Foundational — no user-visible change

#### `F18-T1` — The spike · owner: **agent**

**Nothing ships from this task. Its output is a paste in the PR**, and the rest of the feature is
provisional until it exists.

Three questions, all of them claims about somebody else's system:

1. Invoked from the watch's **Shortcuts app**, with the ZenPom watch app not running: **which process
   runs `perform()`**, and does a `WCSession` in that process reach the phone?
2. Invoked from a **widget button** — a throwaway extension target, deleted after the spike, since
   `F17` has not built the real one: same two questions.
3. If the answer to either is *"the extension, and it cannot send"*: does `openAppWhenRun` produce a
   working send, and **how long does the watch app take to appear and complete it?**

**No App Group, in the spike or after it.** `F14` records that `find . -name "*.entitlements"`
returns nothing and `project.yml` contains no application-groups entry (both run 2026-09-09), and
argues that a handoff through a shared container is read when the app next runs — excluded on the
same grounds that excluded `transferUserInfo`. An App Group also touches the developer account and
the provisioning profile, which is `C2`'s territory and yours. The throwaway extension target the
spike needs is deleted in the same commit that records its output.

**Scoped deliberately narrow.** This spike does not measure how fast an unreachable `sendMessage`
fails — that is `F14-T2`'s question and `F18` inherits its answer. Two plans measuring the same thing
is how the register ends up with two numbers for one fact.

*Checkpoint:* a section in the PR naming, per invocation path, the process that ran, whether the
phone was reached, and — if `openAppWhenRun` was needed — the observed delay. Plus a one-paragraph
recommendation: silent shape or app-launching shape. **If both invocation paths fail, this task's
output is "`F17-T5` cannot be built as described" and the feature stops here**, which is a cheap
answer arrived at early rather than an expensive one arrived at in `T3`.

#### `F18-T2` — One sender · owner: **agent**

`F14-T3` puts the send inside the wrist button's action. `F18` needs the same send from a second
caller, so it is extracted first: **`WristLaunch`, one function on the watch side, the only place in
the watch target that constructs a launch request or calls `sendMessage`.**

Nothing user-visible: the button behaves byte-for-byte as it does after `F14`. The existing suite is
the instrument — it passes unchanged, or the extraction is wrong.

The fence lands here, because a seam nothing enforces is a suggestion: a scan of the watch target's
own sources, in the shape `watchSources()` already established, failing if `sendMessage` is **called**
anywhere but that one file.

**Called, not mentioned, and this is not pedantry — a bare string scan is red today.** The token
`sendMessage` already appears twice in `ZenTomatoWatch/WatchLink.swift`, at line 17 and line 98, both
times in a comment explaining why the tap path deliberately uses `transferUserInfo` instead. A scan
for the bare string is therefore red before any mutation is applied and proves nothing — which is
exactly the trap `F14-M10` records for the mirror-image case (*"a scan of the watch sources for that
string is red before the mutation"*). The fence must match a call site — a receiver and an argument
list, not the word in prose — or, failing that, be driven against a fake session in the way
`theLaunchIsNeverQueued` is. **`F18-T2` is not done until the fence has been seen green on the tree as
it stands**, before `F18-M3` is applied to it.

*Checkpoint:* the full existing suite passes unchanged; `theWristHasOneSender` is seen to fail when a
second `sendMessage` call site is pasted into `WatchTimerScreen.swift` by hand.

### Vertical slices — each one a thing you can hold

#### `F18-T3` — The intent · owner: **agent**

`StartBlockOnPhoneIntent` in `ZenTomatoWatch/`, conforming to `AppIntent` — **not**
`LiveActivityIntent`, which is iOS's and is what `DismissBlockIntent` needs for a different reason.
A `static let title`, `static let` rather than `var` for the reason
`DismissBlockIntent.swift:43-47` records, a `perform()` that calls `WristLaunch` and returns what
`T4` defines, and **nothing else**.

**Half this task is meeting a fence that arrives before it.** `F14-T1` writes `noCaptureAtAnyDoor` —
failing on `@Parameter`, `AppEntity`, `EntityQuery`, `IntentParameter` or `ParameterSummary` anywhere

`F18` writes no new parameter fence and must not: a second scan of the same tokens over an
overlapping tree is two rules that agree today. **What `F18` contributes is the first subject that

**One caveat this plan cannot settle for itself: which unit owns that fence is still contested.**
`docs/plans/F10.md:106` marks it *"Contested — the gate must settle it, and this plan does not"*,

**Discoverability is Open Question 1 and this task does not decide it.** `DismissBlockIntent` sets

*Checkpoint:* on the paired watch, with the phone in another room and the ZenPom watch app **not

phone-started pom would be.

#### `F18-T4` — What it says back · owner: **agent**

Every outcome carries a sentence. `BlockLauncher` produces four — already running · not authorized ·
unreachable · stale — and `T1` may add a fifth (*the watch app has to be open for this*). The mapping
from outcome to sentence lives in `ZenTomato/WatchLink/Payload/`, per the section above, so the iOS
suite can assert on it.

**Wording is the deliverable, and the standard is `F14-T4`'s:** a launch that did not happen must say
so plainly. *"Couldn't start — use your phone"*, never *"nothing is running"*, because the state may
reconcile a moment later and the sentence has to survive being briefly wrong.

**The trap specific to an intent, restated because it is the whole point of the task:** silence reads
as success. No path through `perform()` may return a bare `.result()`.

*Checkpoint:* each outcome produced deliberately — airplane mode for one, a block already running for
another — and read back off the wrist as spoken or displayed text, not inferred from a log line.

#### `F18-T5` — A pom started without opening the app · owner: **human**

`SPEC.md`'s standard: closed on hardware, by the owner, with a build number and their own words.

Phone in another room, **backgrounded, not open**. Watch app **not open** — force-quit it first.
Start a pom from the wrist by the path `T1` settled. Then walk to the phone and answer five things:
did it start · did it start *when you asked*, or minutes later · was it attached to the right thing ·
**was there music** · **did the watch app visibly appear**.

The music question is not decoration: `F14` found that `running.music.start()` is called from
`TimerView`'s `.task` in `ZenTomatoApp`, so a block launched into a backgrounded phone may begin
**silent**. `F14-T6` asks the same question and `F18` inherits whatever it finds; it is repeated here
because `F18`'s path can wake the phone without any watch app in the middle, and I could not verify
that the two paths behave the same.

*Checkpoint:* recorded as an `O<n>`, closed by the owner with the build number.

## Scope fence

Not in `F18`, each needing its own gate: **any parameter on the intent** — a task, a duration, an
`F8` shape · a stop, skip, pause or abandon intent · a capture intent (*"log a distraction"* from
Siri) · anything that reads the tally or the day's count back to the wrist · a watch-side
`AppShortcutsProvider` phrase, unless Open Question 6 rules it in · the complication itself, which is
`F17` · any queueing, retry or persistence of a launch on the wrist · any column recording which door
a pom came through.

**"While you're in there, let it take a task or a duration" is the one that will be asked
mid-build**, because a parameterless intent looks unfinished in the Shortcuts editor — the editor's
own affordance is a row inviting you to add one. It is out on two independent grounds, and it is
worth having both: it contradicts `D2`'s *never picks a task*, **and** an intent parameter is a
capture surface with a different shape, which is `F10`'s argument and which the no-capture rule
forbids outright. `F18-M1` is the mutation that proves the fence works.

**Second most likely: "add a Stop intent while the machinery is warm."** Also out, and for the reason
`F14`'s fence already records: `TimerEngine.stop(reason:)` requires a reason **by its type**,
deliberately, so no caller can quietly abandon a block without one. An intent cannot supply a written
sentence, so a stop intent would either bypass that requirement or invent an empty reason — both are
the defect the signature exists to prevent.

**Third: "make it work when the phone is off, by saving it for later."** That is the one ruling `F14`
made most emphatically and it is inherited whole. A launch that arrives four minutes late is a lie
about when you started.

## Open questions for the gate

Six, batched. **Nothing below is invented an answer for**, though where I have a recommendation it is
stated with its reasoning so it can be disagreed with quickly.

1. **Is the intent discoverable in Shortcuts and Siri, or invisible plumbing for the complication
   only?** The two precedents in this repository point opposite ways: `DismissBlockIntent` sets
   `isDiscoverable = false` because *"offering 'Dismiss block' as a shortcut a person could run at any
   moment would put back exactly the outside-the-app abandon path that was just removed"*;
   `F10`'s `StartSprintIntent` is discoverable because a Shortcut is the point of it. I would make it
   **discoverable**, because that is what makes `F18` useful on the day it merges rather than on the
   day `F17` merges — see question 4. But it is a new surface and it is your call.

2. **If `T1` finds the extension process cannot reach the phone, which smaller feature do you
   want?** Either (a) `openAppWhenRun` — it works, but the watch app visibly launches on every tap
   and the glance-and-tap becomes something slower and more conspicuous; or (b) ship `F18` for
   Shortcuts and Siri only, and let `F17-T5` fall back to opening the app. I cannot recommend between
   these without the spike's numbers, and I do not want to pre-commit you to (a) before you have seen
   how long it takes.

3. **Order: does `F18` wait for `F14`, or do `F14-T1` and `F14-T2` move into `F18`?** As written this
   plan assumes `BlockLauncher` and the launch channel already exist, which your ruling of today makes
   the natural reading. The alternative is that `F18` goes first and carries them, and `F14` shrinks
   to the button on the watch screen. Both are coherent; what is *not* coherent is `F18` building its
   own transport beside `F14`'s, which would be two doors onto one room and is the thing your ruling
   was made to prevent. `docs/plans/F17.md:12` already orders `F18` before `F17`, which does not
   settle this. **`F14`'s own Q8 — whether `Source` carries a `watchIntent` case `F14` never sends —
   should be answered in the same breath**, because the two questions have one answer between them:
   if `F18` follows `F14` closely the case can wait for `F18-T3`, and if it does not, `F14` shipping
   without it costs a later edit to a sealed type.

4. **If the complication delta is rejected at `F17`'s gate, does `F18` still earn v1.5?** This is the
   honest weak point of the argument at the top of this file. `F18`'s v1.5 justification is `F17-T5`;
   if `F17` is not built, what remains is *"a Siri phrase and a wrist Shortcut that start a pom"* —
   which may well be worth having on its own, but it is a different and smaller claim than the one
   this plan makes, and I would rather you rule on it now than have it discovered at the review.

5. **Does the intent work during a break, to start the next pom early?** `F14`'s Open Question 3 asks
   the same thing about its button. **Whatever you answer there must be the answer here** — two doors
   onto one room with different rules about when they open is precisely the drift this seam exists to
   prevent. Named so the two are answered together rather than separately.

6. **Is a watch-side `AppShortcutsProvider` in scope?** That is what gives you a spoken phrase without
   first building a Shortcut by hand. It is a small amount of code and a real user-facing surface with
   its own copy — so it is either part of this feature or it is a separate one, and it should not
   arrive by accident during `T3`.
