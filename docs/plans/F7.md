# F7 — Watch companion  *(spec delta — D2)*

**Gate:** requires F6 merged, your yes, and **ratification of D2**. Until D2 is merged into `SPEC.md`,
this feature does not exist and no line of it may be written.
**Branch:** `F7/watch-companion`
**Proposed spec text:** see D2 in `00-deltas.md`.
**Done when:** three wrist taps during a pomodoro, with the phone in another room, yield three records
on the phone with the right task and timestamps.

## Summary

The distraction log is the point of the app, and its one real failure mode is friction at the moment of
capture: noticing your attention has wandered and then picking up the phone is itself a distraction, and
the phone is where the distraction probably came from. The wrist removes that. That is the whole
justification for this feature, and it is worth holding onto because it also defines the boundary.

**The phone is the source of truth and runs the only timer engine.** The watch displays state it was
given and sends taps. It does not compute, decide, or own anything.

```
        PHONE (truth)                              WATCH (glass)
   ┌────────────────────┐                     ┌────────────────────┐
   │ TimerEngine        │  applicationContext │  block kind        │
   │ SwiftData store    │ ──── latest only ──▶│  endsAt  ──▶ 14:22 │
   │ Todoist, music     │                     │  task title        │
   │                    │                     │                    │
   │ Distraction rows   │◀─── userInfo ───────│  [ I ]    [ E ]    │
   └────────────────────┘   queued, reliable  └────────────────────┘
```

## What the watch does not do

Listed explicitly, because every one of these is a thing that will feel natural to add and each is
either out of scope or a re-litigation of a decision already made:

- No timer engine of its own. It renders a countdown from `endsAt`; it does not run one.
- No SwiftData store of its own. Taps are queued by WatchConnectivity, not persisted by us.
- No music control. Skip-forward is the only control the spec allows and it lives on the phone.
- No task picker. Picking a task is a considered act at a desk, not a wrist interaction.
- No note editing. A sentence typed on a watch is not the reflection F5 is asking for.
- No complication and no watch-face widget. `SPEC.md` puts "widgets beyond the Lock Screen Live
  Activity" out of scope, and D2 does not change that.
- No standalone mode. Phone unreachable means taps queue, not that the watch takes over.

## The transport, and why each choice

This is the entire technical substance of F7. Getting the delivery semantics right is the feature;
everything else is two buttons and a label.

**Phone → watch: `updateApplicationContext`.** Latest-state-wins semantics with no queue, which is
exactly right for "what block are we in and when does it end." An old context is worthless, so dropping
it is correct behaviour rather than data loss. Sent on every block transition and on task change — a
handful of messages per sprint, not one per second. The watch renders the countdown locally from
`endsAt`, so a silent connection for twenty minutes costs nothing.

**Watch → phone: `transferUserInfo`.** Queued, persisted by the system, and delivered when the phone
becomes reachable again — including after the watch app is killed. This is the load-bearing choice:
`sendMessage` requires live reachability and would drop a tap when the phone is in another room, which
is precisely the scenario in the *Done when*. A dropped tap is silent data loss in the one feature the
spec calls the point of the app.

**The tap payload is self-contained:** `{id: UUID, kind: I|E, tappedAt: Date, sessionID: UUID}`. It
carries its own timestamp, taken on the watch at the moment of the press — never the moment of delivery.
A tap delivered eleven minutes late must still say 14:32.

**Idempotency.** Every tap has a UUID and the phone ignores one it has already stored. WatchConnectivity
can deliver more than once, and without this a flaky connection inflates your distraction counts —
corrupting the exact numbers the Rhodia review is reading.

## Tasks

### F7-T1 — Watch target

`ZenTomato Watch App`, watchOS 26.0, bundle `com.martingleason.ZenTomato.watchkitapp`. Shared code
(the `BlockKind` and `DistractionKind` enums, the payload types) moves into a small local Swift package
shared by both targets rather than being duplicated or file-referenced.

### F7-T2 — Session plumbing

`WCSession` activation on both sides, with the delegates in one file per side and no logic in them
beyond decode-and-hand-off. Reachability is observed but never gates a tap.

### F7-T3 — Watch UI

One screen: block kind, countdown from `endsAt`, task title, and the **I** and **E** buttons with
haptic feedback. Buttons sized for a glance under load.

Break blocks show the countdown with the buttons hidden, matching F5's rule that distractions are only
captured during work.

Unreachable phone: the countdown keeps running (it is local arithmetic) and taps still register with a
small "will sync" indicator. The watch never says "disconnected" in a way that suggests the tap was lost.

### F7-T4 — Phone-side ingest

Receive `userInfo`, check the UUID against stored rows, and on a new one write a `Distraction` with the
watch's timestamp, attached to the session named in the payload — resolving task and project snapshots
from that session exactly as a phone-side tap would.

If the named session has already ended by the time the tap arrives, it still attaches to that session.
The tap happened during that pomodoro; late delivery does not change when it happened.

### F7-T5 — The sheet question

A tap made on the wrist gets a sentence field in the phone's end-of-pomodoro sheet like any other,
provided it arrives before the sheet is presented. One that arrives later simply keeps `note == nil` —
a normal, already-supported outcome from F5, not a special case.

## Tests

| Test | Asserts |
|---|---|
| `tapQueuedWhileUnreachableIsDelivered` | the *Done when*: unreachable at tap, delivered on reconnect |
| `duplicateTapIgnored` | same UUID twice ⇒ one row |
| `timestampIsTapTimeNotDeliveryTime` | 11-minute delay ⇒ row timestamp is the tap instant |
| `lateTapAttachesToItsSession` | session ended before delivery ⇒ still attached to it |
| `lateTapInheritsTaskSnapshot` | task and project snapshots match that session's |
| `contextCarriesEndsAtNotRemaining` | the payload is an absolute date; countdown derived on-watch |
| `watchClockOffsetDoesNotSkewDisplay` | watch clock drift ⇒ countdown correct within tolerance |
| `breakHidesCaptureButtons` | break context ⇒ no I/E on the watch |
| `noStoreOnWatch` | the watch target links no `ModelContainer` |
| `phoneWorksWithNoWatchPaired` | every F1–F6 test still passes with WatchConnectivity unavailable |

The last one is not a formality. F7 must be strictly additive: an unpaired watch, a watch left on the
charger, a watch that was never bought — none of them may change how the phone behaves.

## Device check (gated on C4, and on a paired watch)

Start a pomodoro with a task attached. Leave the phone in another room. Tap I, E, I on the wrist at
times you write down. Return, open the phone, and confirm three rows with the right kinds, the right
task, and your recorded times — not the time you walked back into the room.

Then repeat with the watch app force-quit between taps.

## Evidence for the PR

`xcodebuild test` for both targets, `swiftlint --strict`, the phone-with-no-watch regression run, and
the device result with your written-down times next to the recorded ones.

## Risks — read this before saying yes

- **This is the feature most likely to be abandoned unmerged.** It is last in the order, it is the
  delta, and it needs two devices to verify. Against the **September 13, 2026** hard stop, `SPEC.md` is
  explicit that unmerged work is abandoned cleanly rather than rushed — F7 is what that clause is for.
- **C4 gates it twice**: builds on the phone *and* a paired watch running watchOS 26.
- **WatchConnectivity delivery timing is not guaranteed to be prompt**, only eventual. `transferUserInfo`
  can take minutes. That is acceptable here — the timestamp travels with the tap — but it makes the
  device check slow and slightly maddening to run.
- **Scope gravity is strongest here.** A watch app invites a complication, a start button, a task
  picker, "just" a pause control. Every one is out of scope. The reviewer agent should be pointed at
  this list specifically.
- **If D2 is rejected**, delete this file. Nothing else in the project changes — no other plan depends
  on F7, by design.
