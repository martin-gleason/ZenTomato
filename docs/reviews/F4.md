## Blocking findings, and what closed them

1. **Music died permanently on the commonest first-run outcome, and the app's own words pointed
   at an exit it could not see.** Whether this app may play music was read once, at launch, and
   never again. `.task(id: scenePhase)` called only `engine.synchronize()`. The only other paths
   that re-read it were `start()` (once per process) and `setEnabled(true)` — and `setEnabled` is
   unreachable in exactly the states that need it, because `MusicRowModel.forTimer` sets
   `isTogglable: isAvailable` and `MusicPickerView` dims its mirrored switch the same way. So the
   one control that would have re-asked is dimmed by the state it would have cleared.

   The concrete failure: one mis-tap on the system permission prompt. The Music sheet then tells the
   person to change it in Settings › Privacy & Security › Media & Apple Music and gives them a
   button that opens the Settings app. They grant it, come back — and the switch is still dimmed,
   the sheet still draws no library, and the sheet's own read still returns at its
   `guard music.availability == .ready`. Only force-quitting the app helps, and nothing says so.
   `.couldNotBeChecked` reaches the identical dead end with no user action at all: one phone that
   launched in a lift, one transient library read.

   This is the F3 defect the brief names — an affordance suppressed to protect something else,
   leaving a whole feature unreachable — and the design spec's own defence ("the line is tappable in
   every idle state… that is what stops a failure from becoming a dead end") was untrue, because the
   sheet the line opens was inert in exactly those states.

   Closed by `MusicCoordinator.refreshAvailability()`, called from the two moments that mean
   somebody may have just changed something: the app coming back to the front
   (`ZenTomatoApp.swift`, alongside the engine's existing catch-up) and the Music sheet being
   opened (`MusicPickerSheet`, in a `.task` of its own so it cannot re-arm through the read's
   key). Both are reads; neither can prompt for anything, and the tests assert the prompt count
   stays where it was. New suite `MusicRecovery`: `permissionGrantedElsewhereIsNoticedOnComingBack`
   and `aCheckThatCouldNotBeMadeClearsItselfOnTheNextOne`, both of which assert on the **row** —
   `isTogglable` — rather than on the internal value, because "the state changed" is not the claim.

2. **A test that could not fail was standing in as the only coverage of a behavioural path.**
   `interruptionBeginningIsNotActedOn` asserted `Event.began != .ended(mayResume: true)` and
   `.ended(false) != .ended(true)` — both guaranteed by the compiler for any enumeration with those
   cases. It never built a coordinator, never delivered an event and never asserted anything about
   the app, yet it was the only test naming the `.began` path. Replacing that arm with `pause()`,
   `apply()` or a resume would have left it green. The whole of
   `AudioSessionInterruptions.event(from:)` — the one place a value arrives in a shape nobody
   controls — was likewise driven by nothing.

   Closed by making the claim testable rather than by deleting it.
   `MusicCoordinator.handleInterruption(_:)` is now the routing point the listening loop calls, so a
   test can drive a real `.began` through the real code during a live focus block and assert the
   player was asked for nothing. **Verified to fail without the fix**: replacing the do-nothing arm
   with `apply()` produces `player.callLog → ["load", "resume"] == ["load"]`.
   `event(from:)` is no longer private, and `everyShapeOfInterruptionNoticeIsReadOrRefused` puts
   seven hand-built notifications through it: began, ended with `shouldResume`, ended with no
   options key, ended with zero options, and three malformed shapes that must produce no event
   rather than a guess. `interruptionEndingIsActedOnThroughTheSameDoor` is the pair, so the first
   test is a statement about `.began` rather than about the routing being broken for everything.

3. **The device check does not exist, and nothing on this branch can substitute for it.** Reported
   by all three lenses. `make test` and `swiftlint --strict` are real and green, and prove every
   decision this app makes about *when* to play, pause, resume, stop and skip. They prove nothing at
   all about whether a note came out of a speaker: every test drives a stand-in, the simulator has
   no music library and no Apple Music, and `AppleMusicLibrary` throws `.permissionDenied` for both
   requests there. **This is not closed and cannot be closed by me.** It is carried in *Still open*
   with the exact steps, and it is the gate for this feature.

## Non-blocking findings acted on

- **`isPlaying` was computed, so the screen never redrew when it changed.** The `@Observable` macro
  instruments stored properties; a computed one reading through a `let` registers no dependency. The
  row redrew on the happy path only incidentally, because `isStarting` flips at the same moment.
  Every playback change that did not coincide with a coordinator state change — a Control Centre
  pause, an AirPod removed, CarPlay — was invisible, leaving a skip button on screen that did
  nothing. Reading live inside `@Observable` is what made it stale. It is now a stored
  `private(set) var`, refreshed after every load, resume, silence and skip, and again when the app
  comes back to the front. Three doc comments that claimed "read live keeps the skip button honest"
  now say what actually happens, including the limit: iOS does not tell an app when its own
  controls are used, so between refreshes the two can still disagree.

- **An availability lapse mid-sprint threw the place in the track away.**
  `releasesQueue` returned true for any non-permitting availability, so a subscription flickering
  during a renewal, or a `.couldNotBeChecked` clearing, called `stop()`, cleared the queue, and the
  next `apply()` restarted the playlist from the top in the middle of the afternoon. That is the
  exact audible defect `resumeIsNeverARestart` exists to prevent, reached by a different route — and
  it contradicted this same feature's other decision, made twelve lines away in `MusicCoordinator`,
  that a device condition is *not* a change of mind and does not rewrite the person's switch. The
  availability term is gone: `releasesQueue` is now `sprintIsOver || !isEnabled`. Unavailable
  mid-sprint pauses; the sprint ending still releases.
  `subscriptionEndingMidSprintStopsTheMusicAndNothingElse` now asserts the queue is **kept**, and
  the new `aSubscriptionComingBackMidSprintResumesRatherThanRestarts` asserts one load across the
  lapse and the return.

- **The ordering guard was executed by no test.** `SpyMusicPlayer`'s methods all finished
  synchronously, so the generation counter, the re-check after every suspension, the
  `catch is CancellationError` arm and `reassertSilenceIfWanted()` were run by nothing — the top
  risk in the build contract, protected only by somebody reading the code and agreeing with it. The
  spy now has a one-shot `gateLoads`, and the new `MusicOrdering` suite holds a load open across a
  block boundary in both directions: `aBreakArrivingMidLoadEndsInSilence` (the break lands
  mid-load; the superseded work re-states the silence when it returns) and
  `aStaleLoadArrivingInALaterBlockDoesNotSilenceIt` (the mirror — re-stating silence is conditional,
  so a leftover load must not cut off music that is correctly playing now).

- **The app touched Apple's player at launch, before anybody switched music on.**
  `BlockPhaseObserver.start()` delivers its first phase synchronously → `blockChanged` → `apply()` →
  `goQuiet()` → `ApplicationMusicPlayer.shared.stop()`, on the main thread inside the launch task,
  and again at every block boundary of every sprint whether or not music is in use. It contradicted
  the posture stated in `AudioSessionInterruptions`, that this app does not touch the audio stack
  until it has something to play. `goQuiet()` now returns early unless this app has actually asked
  the player for something, tracked on the coordinator rather than read off `player.loaded` — the
  coordinator's flag is set the moment a sound attempt begins, so a break landing mid-load still
  issues a real pause. New test `nothingIsSaidToThePlayerUntilThereIsSomethingToSay`. **Verified to
  fail without the fix**: `player.callLog → ["stop", "stop"]`.

- **A `[weak self]` that was defeated by where the guard sat.** `availabilityTask` put
  `guard let self` outside the `for await`, so the loop header held `self` strongly for the life of
  a stream that never finishes — a retain cycle making the `isolated deinit` (whose doc comment
  argues at length that it is load-bearing) unreachable in production. The guard moved inside the
  loop, matching the interruption task, with the checker captured instead.

- **Resume-or-load was decided by comparing whole values, including the title.** `MusicSelection` is
  `Equatable` over all three properties, and `checkSelectionIsStillThere` deliberately rewrites the
  title when a playlist is renamed in the Music app. So a rename between two blocks of a live sprint
  made the coordinator think a different playlist had been chosen and start it from the top.
  Silent, and it would have presented as "my track sometimes starts over after a break" — which
  `resumeIsNeverARestart` cannot catch, because the same equality is what it counts. Now compared by
  identifier and kind. New test `aRenameBetweenBlocksIsNotARestart`, **verified to fail without the
  fix**: `startedFromTheTopCount → 2`.

- **Raw framework errors escaped the protocol fence.** `AppleMusicPlayer.load` called the two
  library lookups without a `do`/`catch`, so a `MusicLibraryRequest` failure travelled out through
  `MusicPlaying` — past two doc comments each promising exactly `selectionMissing` or
  `playbackFailed` — and was saved only by a catch-all one level up. Both lookups are wrapped now,
  with `CancellationError` re-thrown untouched so the coordinator's own handling of it still works.

- **The two-line reserve was truncating this feature's copy one notch above the default text size.**
  The usability lens measured it with CoreText at the real widths: in the side-by-side arrangement
  the line gets ~246pt, and from `.xLarge` upwards several of these sentences need three. The halves
  that disappeared were the load-bearing ones — *"The block is still running"*, *"Back at the next
  focus block"*, and the second half of the refused-permission line, which is the half naming
  Settings. `stackingThreshold` drops from `.accessibility1` to `.xxLarge`, where the line gets the
  full 361pt, and the reserve becomes four lines at `.accessibility3` and above. D19.3 is untouched:
  the reserve is still a fixed line count at any one text size, and the rule governs movement within
  a cycle. Two previews added at the sizes that were cutting.

- **The switch's spoken hint gave the wrong reason.** It was chosen from a single "can it be
  touched?" test, so denied / restricted / no-subscription / could-not-be-checked all said *"Music
  is set before a sprint. This unlocks when the timer stops."* — telling somebody to stop a timer
  that is not running, while the visible line eight points away said the true reason. The reason now
  travels on `MusicRowModel.toggleHint` and the sheet mirrors the same three-way choice. New test
  `aDimmedSwitchSaysWhichOfTheTwoReasonsItIs`, which also asserts that in the idle states the spoken
  hint and the visible line are the same sentence. **Verified to fail without the fix**: 16 issues.

- **A running block could show an instruction it could not obey.** `runningLine` fell through to
  *"Choose something to play"* when nothing was chosen — reachable in three taps, and then on screen
  for twenty-five minutes beside a dimmed switch and a line that is not a button. That string
  carries a doc comment explaining it is an invitation precisely because nobody taps a caption;
  putting it where nothing can be tapped is the same defect reversed. New copy
  `MusicCopy.nothingChosenSoQuiet` — *"Nothing chosen, so this block is quiet."* — a fact, in the
  register of every other running-state line, and added to both jargon-and-creation sweeps.

- **A picker row title clamped to one line.** After the artist and track-count details were cut, the
  title is the only content in the row, and one line is the hardest clamp on any list row in the app
  (`PlanRowView` gives 2, `ProjectPickerView` gives 3). Two playlists sharing a prefix became the
  same row at large text, on a screen whose entire job is telling items apart. Now `.lineLimit(2)`.

- **A new adjacency F4 introduced was not priced.** During a focus block the skip button's 44×44
  target sat 32pt above the External capture button, in line with it. The column already prices this
  kind of adjacency at 48pt for Stop, on the stated argument that the two failure modes are not the
  same size. A downward mis-tap from a transport control would have written a distraction that never
  happened. The music row gains 16pt of bottom padding — constant across the whole cycle and in
  particular not conditioned on whether skip is drawn, so D19.3 is unaffected.

## Findings rejected, or whose proposed fix was rejected

- **"Fire the existing capture haptic on skip."** Rejected as proposed. `CaptureHaptic`'s doc
  comment is emphatic and load-bearing: it fires *"from exactly one place, on exactly one
  condition"* — `recordDistraction` returned `true` — so that *"a person who logs a distraction
  without looking at the screen can trust the buzz completely, because there is no path in the code
  where it fires for a row that does not exist."* Firing it for a skip falsifies that sentence and
  makes the one signal a pocket can carry ambiguous, to add confirmation to a control whose
  confirmation is the audio. Adding a second, distinct haptic verb would mean editing
  `ZenTomato/Distraction/`, which is on the zero-diff fence. The underlying concern is real and is
  carried into the device check instead: if skip does not advance inside a playlist, that is the
  defect, and no buzz would fix it.

- **"Give a library-read failure its own state instead of writing `availability`."** Partly
  rejected. Both consequences the finding named are closed by other fixes: the queue is no longer
  released on an availability lapse, so a transient library failure pauses rather than restarting
  the playlist; and `.couldNotBeChecked` is no longer a one-way door, because the app re-checks on
  foreground and on opening the sheet. What remains is a wording nuance — the line reads *"ZenTomato
  couldn't tell whether it can play your music"*, which is honestly true of a library that will not
  answer as well as of a subscription check that failed. Adding a fifth piece of coordinator state
  and a fifth sentence to buy that nuance is not the cheap, clearly-correct change the brief asks
  for, and it would widen `MusicRowModel`'s rule for a distinction no reader is asked to act on.

- **"The Music sheet's running-block state is unreachable — delete it, or make the sheet reachable
  mid-block."** Rejected as a choice between those two, and the code is kept with the truth written
  next to it. The reviewer is right that no user reaches it: both doors are idle-only and Start is
  behind the sheet. But deleting the lock would mean that the first future path opening this sheet
  during a block finds a live-looking switch that silently no-ops — strictly worse than a dimmed one
  with a sentence beside it, and the opposite of the doubled refusal `MusicCoordinator` already
  argues for. And making the line tappable mid-block contradicts D19 in the design's own words: *"a
  screen offering a choice it will not honour is worse than one that offers none."* That is a
  ratified decision, not mine to reverse. `isBlockRunning`'s doc comment now says plainly that no
  user reaches this state today, why it is kept, and that it does **not** explain the dimmed switch
  on the timer screen — which is the half of the finding that is genuinely open. See *Still open*.

- **"Switch the playlist branch to `Queue(playlist:startingAt:)`."** Not rejected — deliberately not
  done, exactly as both reviewers who raised it advised. Nothing in this repository can determine
  whether MusicKit expands a `Playlist` passed to `Queue(for:)` into one entry per track or holds it
  as one entry, and changing it on a guess would trade an unverified construction for a different
  unverified construction. It is item 1 of the device check.

## Still open

1. **THE DEVICE CHECK. THIS IS THE GATE AND IT IS NOT CLOSED.** Nothing on this branch can validate
   that a sound came out of a phone. It is roughly two hours and it cannot be shortened, because the
   thing being tested is a full sprint. One-minute blocks can prove the transitions first and are
   worth doing first, but they are not the check.

   In order:

   1. **Skip inside a playlist — do this before anything else.** A library playlist of three or
      more tracks. Start a focus block, let a track begin, tap skip twice. It must advance track by
      track. If it stops, or jumps to the end, or restarts the playlist, then
      `ApplicationMusicPlayer.Queue(for: [playlist])` is holding the playlist as a single entry and
      the `.playlist` branch of `AppleMusicPlayer.load` must move to
      `Queue(playlist:startingAt:)` — and this check re-run. Nothing in the repository can decide
      this, and it is the app's only permitted control.
   2. **The loop.** A two-track playlist, run past its end. It must start again rather than stop.
   3. **Takeover and release.** Play something else — Music.app, a podcast — then start a focus
      block. ZenTomato must take over with the chosen playlist. At sprint end it must stop and leave
      the system player alone.
   4. **A full sprint, screen locked.** Four pomodoros, three short breaks, a long break, with the
      phone locked and face down. Music pauses on every break and resumes **mid-track** on every
      focus block. Narrate each boundary: whether the music returns at the instant of the boundary
      or a moment after the alarm wakes the app. A paused app has no audio and iOS may suspend it,
      so the long break is the interesting one.
   5. **With and without headphones**, and one phone call taken mid-focus-block — the music must
      come back — and one taken mid-break, where it must not.

2. **The dimmed switch mid-sprint has no visible explanation, and that is an owner decision.**
   During a block the music switch is dimmed. A VoiceOver reader hears why; a sighted reader gets a
   dead control and nothing else, because the sentence written for it lives on a sheet that cannot
   be opened then. The row's line is fully spoken for during a block (it carries the chosen item,
   or the reason music is quiet), so there is nowhere obvious to put it without spending the timer
   screen's very small budget. Options are: leave it, spend a line, or accept a mid-block tap on the
   line opening a sheet that shows only the note and the locked switch. The last contradicts D19 as
   written, so it needs a delta.

3. **Mid-track position does not survive an app kill.** The queue dies with the process, so a
   relaunch issues `load()` and the track restarts. This is deliberate: persisting a position would
   need `playbackTime`, which the `MusicPlaying` fence makes unsayable, and that fence is worth more
   than the convenience. Stated here rather than discovered later.

4. **The system's own transport controls cannot be suppressed.** Control Centre, the Lock Screen,
   headphones and CarPlay will show play, pause and back for whatever is playing. No app can opt
   out. Between one of this app's refresh points and the next, its idea of what is playing and the
   player's can disagree; the refresh points are now every load, resume, silence and skip, plus the
   app coming back to the front. Documented in `MusicPlaying`, in `MusicRow`, in the last footer of
   the Music sheet, and in the pull request.

5. **The branch is not committed.** The remediation brief forbids committing, so the whole of
   `ZenTomato/Music/`, the four `Views/Music*` files and all nine music test files are still
   untracked. `git diff main` does not show them. Commit in conventional-commit format with F4 task
   IDs before opening the pull request, or the diff the owner reviews will be missing the feature.

## Watch list — a rewind that could not be reproduced

**Reported 2026-08-23, not observed again after the track-name fix. Not fixed. Not closed.**

The owner: *"Focus for five minutes, plays two songs. Second song cuts out right at the beginning of
the break. It returns to the first song."*

On the very next run it did not happen, and the most likely explanation is that it never did: the
track name was, at that moment, always **exactly one song behind** (see the fix in `67c6fae`). Somebody
watching for a rewind would have been shown the previous track's name at precisely the instant they
were looking for one. That is a good enough account to stop chasing it, and not good enough to call it
closed.

Recorded here so that a second sighting starts from a diagnosis rather than from nothing.

### If it happens again, in the order worth checking

**1. Did `resume()` run, or `load()`?** This is the fork that decides everything and it is one line —
`MusicCoordinator.apply()` compares `player.loaded?.identifier` against the wanted selection. A `load`
re-queues from the top; a `resume` does not. The unit tests assert the sequence is
`["load", "pause", "resume"]` and pass, so if a real device takes the load branch, something has
cleared `loaded` — and only `stop()` does that.

**2. Was `stop()` called instead of `pause()`?** `goQuiet()` chooses between them on
`releasesQueue(sprintIsOver:isEnabled:)`. A break should never release. Check whether `sprintIsOver`
was computed `true` at a plain block boundary — `BlockPhaseObserver.phase(of:)` derives it from three
engine values and its `nothingInProgress` arm is the one that could misfire.

**3. App suspension — the likeliest real cause if the first two are clean.** During a break there is
no audio, so iOS may suspend the app, and `ApplicationMusicPlayer` is not documented to keep its queue
position across that. Nothing in this codebase would show a fault: `loaded` survives (it is our own
memory), the resume branch is taken correctly, and MusicKit simply starts from the top.

The tell that distinguishes 3 from 1 and 2: **the length of the break.** A rewind after a ten-minute
long break and not after a five-minute short one is suspension. A rewind after every break is ours.

### Candidate fixes, worst-first, if it turns out to be suspension

**A. Remember the position and restore it.** Store the track identifier and elapsed time when pausing,
and on resume re-queue from that track and seek. Complete, and the only one that survives a genuine
teardown.

It needs `playbackTime`, which is **on the scope fence by name** — the fence exists because MusicKit
puts seeking on the same object as skip, and skip-only is a ratified decision. The argument for
allowing it is that this is *state restoration after the OS dropped our queue*, not a transport control
anybody can reach: no scrub bar, no button, nothing in the protocol a view could call. That argument
may well be right, and it is **a delta to ratify rather than a judgement to make quietly.**

**B. Restore the track but not the position.** Re-queue starting at the track that was playing, and
accept starting it from the beginning. Needs `Queue(for:startingAt:)`, which is already how loading
works — so it widens nothing at all. Cheaper, and it fixes the reported complaint (*"returns to the
first song"*) without fixing the smaller one.

**C. Keep the app alive through the break.** Play silence during breaks so iOS never suspends us.
Fixes it without touching the fence, and is a bad trade: it holds the audio session for the whole
sprint, takes over the Lock Screen's now-playing for a block that is meant to be quiet, and burns
battery to work around a framework behaviour.

**D. Accept it and say so.** If it only happens after long breaks, document it in the app rather than
fight it. A playlist restarting after a fifteen-minute break is a small annoyance; A and B both add
machinery, and B changes what "resume" means.

**Recommendation if it recurs: B first.** It is the cheapest, it widens no fence, and it fixes the
part the owner actually noticed. Reach for A only if starting mid-track turns out to matter.
